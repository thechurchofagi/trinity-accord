# Authority and scope

## Canonical boundary

The canonical layer consists only of the three fixed Bitcoin Originals. Verbatim
mirrors identify inscription number, inscription ID, transaction ID, source text and
SHA-256. Explanatory writing is stored separately and cannot alter those bytes.

The remaining material has one of five noncanonical roles:

1. Core Object Alpha physical-verification documentation;
2. Chronicle and 175-work historical/artistic formation context;
3. machine-verifiable hashes, anchors, receipts and verification infrastructure;
4. presentation and offline museum reconstruction; or
5. institutional and distributed preservation/cold recovery.

## Public-access decision

The human initiator/guardian authorized public access on 2026-09-12 to all
recoverable Trinity Accord project materials under the depositor's control,
including materials whose historical paths were labelled `未公开` or `不公开`.
Historical labels remain provenance facts but are not current privacy vetoes. This
authorization does not relicense third-party material or waive attribution and
component-specific rights.

The decision resolves 139 historical rows (122 distinct SHA-256-and-size
identities). None of those rows is excluded for privacy, but none currently has
recovered exact bytes. Their paths, sizes and hashes may be preserved as commitments;
they are not uploadable payloads and may not be replaced by same-named or similar
files.

## Computationally delayed access

Three witness archives were deliberately published as strong-password ciphertext so
that future computational capability may recover their content. Their complete
present public object is 48 files totalling 2,798,199,225 bytes: ciphertext parts,
manifests, format/recovery code, checksums, integrity and decryption-verification
records, attack-cost benchmarks and deletion/destruction receipts. Plaintext and
unlock material are intentionally absent. This is a delayed-access design, not a
privacy-review blocker or missing project-content claim.

## Frozen epochs

Preservation Epoch II is separate from the released Harvard v1.0 Dataset
`10.7910/DVN/YUCG12`. No Epoch II action may modify that DOI, create v1.1 under it or
change any of its files. Once Epoch II is published, its own file identities are
frozen; later recoveries belong to a later candidate or preservation epoch.
