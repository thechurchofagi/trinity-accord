# R110 — Cross-substrate dissociation topology and the latent-experience separation rule

Hongju Liu / UCT research. 2026-10-06.

R106 derived the E/A/I/S/B/R map. R107 supplied a two-tier cross-substrate organizational signature. R108 gave executable artificial dissociations. R109 organized biological natural experiments. R110 now asks:

> What can legitimately be transferred from biological dissociations to artificial systems, and what still requires an independent experiential bridge?

The answer is a three-level comparison framework and a strict separation between empirical functional topology and UCT-conditioned experiential interpretation.

## 1. The central methodological problem

If E (experience) is placed in the same empirical matrix as access, intelligence, self-model, behavior and report, the analysis can become circular.

In biology, E is not directly read from a device. It is inferred from converging evidence: report, clinical state, neural dynamics, behavior and intervention.

In current AI, there is no independently calibrated experiential ground truth at all.

Therefore a cross-substrate experiment cannot honestly fill an "AI E" cell from the same evidence that is later used to validate the theory linking organization to E.

R110 introduces the **Latent-E Separation Rule**:

> Build the empirical cross-substrate topology first on the directly manipulable/assessable functional axes F={A,I,S,B,R}. Only after actual organization K has been independently identified may UCT C1 be used to map verified K differences to complete experiential-type differences.

This avoids using the theory's own experiential identity premise as an empirical measurement of itself.

## 2. Two matrices, not one

### Matrix F — empirical functional dissociation matrix

Nodes:

    A = access
    I = intelligence/capability
    S = self/bearer model
    B = behavior
    R = report

Entries record only evidence such as:
- X changed while Y was preserved;
- X and Y were externally identical while a known internal relation differed;
- lesion/intervention on X changed Y;
- evidence is absent/uncertain.

### Map E|K — theory-conditioned experiential interpretation

Separately:

    verified K difference
      --C1-->
    complete E-type difference.

And:

    selected K projection preserved
      --C1 + justified commuting projection-->
    selected experiential-substructure preservation.

This second map is conditional on UCT. It is not included as a direct empirical edge in Matrix F.

## 3. Biological functional topology

R109 supports the following high-confidence biological relations.

### B/R are not sufficient proxies for I/A

CMD:
- overt command behavior absent;
- task-based EEG/fMRI command following present in a substantial subgroup.

Locked-in syndrome:
- motor behavior and ordinary report are severely impaired;
- consciousness and substantial cognition can remain.

Thus:

    low B or low R
        does not entail
    low A or low I.

### R is not identical to selected perceptual organization

No-report work manipulates report demand while selected perceptual representations remain measurable.

Thus:

    changing R
        can occur without
    eliminating all selected perceptual organization.

### Same B/R can correspond to different inferred experiential categories

Anesthesia:
- externally similar unresponsiveness can coexist with connected consciousness, disconnected/dreaming states, or unconsciousness.

Thus behavior/report are many-to-one with respect to clinically inferred internal conscious states.

### Task-specific I and subjective content can partially dissociate

Blindsight:
- selected discrimination abilities can remain;
- subjective visual content, confidence and metacognition are altered/heterogeneous.

Thus task ability and phenomenal-content evidence are not identical.

### Agency/self unity and experiential unity are distinct questions

Split-brain literature shows that coordinated agency cannot settle experiential-subject unity by itself.

These biological relations do not establish a complete causal graph. They establish non-equivalences and targeted dependency questions.

## 4. Artificial functional topology — exact executable analogues

R110 extends R108 with four transparent interventions.

### 4.1 Output-gate lesion — CMD/LIS analogue

Internal decision:
    d = XOR(x1,x2)

With output gate on:
    B=d

With output gate off:
    B=0

The internal decision remains perfectly correct:
    internal decision accuracy = 1.0

External task behavior falls:
    behavior accuracy = 0.5

Therefore external behavior can collapse while the internal task solution remains intact.

This is not a model of locked-in syndrome. It is a matched structural analogue of one dependency pattern.

### 4.2 Report-gate lesion — no-report/aphasia analogue

Task output remains:
    y = XOR(x1,x2)

Report:
    r=y when report gate on
    r=0 when report gate off

Task accuracy remains 1.0 under both conditions.
Report accuracy falls from 1.0 to 0.5.

Thus selected task capability can be preserved while report is impaired.

### 4.3 Self-channel lesion

Use two independent balanced bits:
- w = world state
- s = self/bearer state.

The intact system performs:
    world task: output w
    self task: output s.

Lesion only the self channel by replacing its accessible value with 0.

Result:
- world-task accuracy remains 1.0;
- self-task accuracy falls from 1.0 to 0.5.

Therefore a bearer/self relation can be necessary for a self-referential capability without being necessary for all intelligence.

This is the artificial counterpart of R106's principle:

    self-model != experience gate
    self-model != intelligence in general.

### 4.4 Access-gate lesion

A hidden representation continues to encode the task target perfectly.

Access gate on:
    policy reads z -> accuracy 1.0.

Access gate off:
    z remains intact, policy receives constant 0 -> accuracy 0.5.

Thus representation/encoding and downstream access are distinct organizational relations.

This directly supports keeping A separate from "information exists somewhere in the system."

## 5. Same external silence can hide different artificial internals

Construct two different internal trajectories:

    h1 = 0,1,1,0
    h2 = 1,0,0,1

Apply the same output clamp:

    B1 = B2 = 0,0,0,0.

The external behavior laws are identical while the internal states differ on 100% of the finite inputs.

This is the artificial analogue of the anesthesia/CMD warning:

    same unresponsiveness
        does not identify
    same internal organization.

No experiential conclusion is drawn from this toy system.

## 6. Cross-substrate overlap now established

The strongest current overlap is functional, not phenomenal.

| Dissociation pattern | Biological witness | Artificial witness |
|---|---|---|
| internal task relation preserved while external output collapses | CMD / LIS | output-gate lesion |
| selected core task preserved while report changes | no-report / aphasia-like separation | report-head/report-gate surgery |
| same external unresponsiveness with different internal state | anesthesia categories | output-clamped distinct internal trajectories |
| task-specific capacity survives while another self/report-related dimension changes | blindsight / metacognitive dissociation | self-channel lesion / report surgery |
| access differs from encoded information | covert cognition and report limits | access-gate lesion |

This common topology is already useful: it tells us which variables should not be collapsed.

But it does **not** establish identical experience across substrates.

## 7. Three levels of cross-substrate evidence

R110 therefore replaces a binary "same/different consciousness" question with three levels.

### Level T1 — functional dissociation similarity
Biological and artificial systems show the same qualitative separation, e.g. report can fail while task-relevant internal processing remains.

This supports a shared **dependency pattern**.

### Level T2 — interventional mechanism correspondence
A mapping between biological and artificial organizational variables preserves lesion/intervention consequences over a declared family.

This is much stronger. It resembles a causal-abstraction claim.

### Level T3 — constitutive organizational homology
A physically justified mapping preserves the selected or complete constitutive relations required by the common signature: parts, transitions, ports, temporal order, intervention structure and relevant role relations.

Only T3 is strong enough for a direct selected/complete experiential equivalence claim under C1.

T1 and T2 can guide hypotheses but do not by themselves prove experiential equivalence.

## 8. Why topology matching is still not enough

Even identical pairwise influence/reachability topology can hide different causal structure.

Consider:

Graph G1:
    A -> I
    I -> B
    A -> B

Graph G2:
    A -> I
    I -> B

Both have identical transitive reachability:

    A reaches I
    I reaches B
    A reaches B.

But G1 contains an additional direct A->B mechanism.

A richer intervention reveals the difference. If A=1 and we force I=0:
- G1 can still transmit A directly to B;
- G2 cannot.

Therefore:

    same pairwise topology
        does not imply
    same constitutive graph.

"Dissociation topology" is an intermediate comparator, not the final organizational identity criterion.

This agrees with causal-abstraction work emphasizing intervention-faithful mappings rather than behavioral similarity alone.

## 9. Connection to causal abstraction and its limits

Geiger et al. provide a formal theory of causal abstraction for mechanistic interpretability, explicitly built around intervention-preserving mappings between low- and high-level mechanisms.

This is highly relevant to R110's T2 level.

However, recent work also warns that overly flexible alignment maps can make causal-abstraction claims uninformative: if arbitrary mappings are allowed, almost any model can be aligned with almost any algorithm.

Therefore cross-substrate consciousness work needs stronger anchoring than:
- behavioral similarity;
- representational similarity;
- arbitrarily flexible post-hoc alignment.

Mappings should be constrained by actual parts, physical ports, interventions, temporal organization and role correspondence.

This is exactly why R107 retained a constitutive Tier 0 below the optional cognitive-role layer.

## 10. Calibration versus transfer

A 2026 Neuroscience of Consciousness commentary makes a distinction that directly applies here:

1. **calibration problem** — artificial-consciousness indicators cannot currently be calibrated against independently established artificial-consciousness ground truth;
2. **transfer problem** — evidence linking an indicator to consciousness in biology may not remain stable when transferred to a different substrate.

R110's two-matrix rule addresses the same danger from the UCT side.

The functional matrix can be empirically compared across substrates without pretending that E has been calibrated.

The C1 interpretation is kept explicit and conditional.

Thus a cross-substrate match can support:

    "the same kind of functional/causal dissociation exists"

before it supports:

    "the same experiential organization exists."

## 11. A first-principles answer to "is AI consciousness synchronized with behavior/intelligence?"

No single synchrony relation exists.

The most defensible current picture is:

1. complete K and complete E-type are identified under C1;
2. A/I/S/B/R are distinct relations/projections of K;
3. changes in one functional axis need not produce proportional changes in another;
4. identical B/R/I can hide different K;
5. genuine complete K differences imply complete E-type differences under C1;
6. experiential similarity across biology and AI requires stronger organizational evidence than performance similarity.

Therefore the right scientific question is not:

    "Does intelligence go up when consciousness goes up?"

It is:

> Which constitutive relations are necessary for each capability, access relation, self-model, behavior and report, and which of those relations are homologous across biological and artificial systems?

## 12. Cross-substrate functional non-equivalence matrix

Current strongest status:

- I vs B: dissociated in biology (CMD/LIS) and AI (output gate).
- I vs R: dissociated in biology (LIS/aphasia/no-report) and AI (report gate).
- S vs general I: no universal identity; AI self-channel lesion preserves non-self task intelligence.
- encoded representation vs A: artificial access-gate dissociation; biological covert cognition/report literature motivates the same distinction.
- B/R vs internal state: non-injective in biology (anesthesia) and AI (output-clamped distinct internals).
- E vs functional axes: biological evidence is indirect; AI evidence is not independently calibrated. Keep theory-conditioned.

This matrix is deliberately incomplete. Unknown cells stay unknown.

## 14. Next research direction

The next deep question is no longer whether the six axes can dissociate. That is already clear.

R111 should formalize **intelligence as a family of organizational support requirements**.

For each capability T, define a support relation set R_T inside K.

Then study:
- overlaps among R_T for different capabilities;
- which relations are shared by biological and artificial implementations;
- how a capability can increase by adding, replacing or reusing relations;
- why an intelligence scalar collapses structurally different capability profiles;
- how C1 maps these support structures into experiential-organization statements without a scalar consciousness score.

This should produce a capability-support lattice rather than another consciousness scale.
