# A4A source scope

## Fixed project sources read

- **UCT I v1.2**, especially C1/U1, §§8.11–8.14 and §14.7. These fix the physical-witness criterion, locate body-centered organization and the conceptual I inside experience, preserve overlapping processes, and prohibit an extra owner or a new experience-existence gate.
- **R157**, coordinate transport and semantic residual. A physically grounded relation may be transported conditionally on one complete same-instance C1 binding; its familiar-mineness name is not thereby selected.
- **R173**, retentive binding and familiar-mineness boundary. Actual prior-occurrence-to-current-consumer use is different from a present value, current agency, report, or an analyst's archive.
- **A3Y/A3Z**, measurand-identity and one-way anchor limits. Falsification of a bridge is stronger than proxy concordance; formation fidelity is not phenomenal fidelity.

## Primary causal-inference antecedents checked

- Robins, J. M. and Greenland, S. (1992), *Identifiability and Exchangeability for Direct and Indirect Effects*, **Epidemiology** 3:143–155, DOI `10.1097/00001648-199203000-00013`. The article explicitly treats effects relayed through an intermediate variable and warns that ordinary adjustment for the intermediate need not identify a direct effect.
- Pearl, J. (2001), *Direct and Indirect Effects*, Proceedings of UAI 17, pp. 411–420; later author-posted version `arXiv:1301.2300`. It distinguishes effects defined by intervention on intermediates from ordinary conditioning and gives conditions for path-specific identification.
- Hernán, M. A., Hernández-Díaz, S., and Robins, J. M. (2004), *A Structural Approach to Selection Bias*, **Epidemiology** 15:615–625. It supplies the relevant causal-graph account of bias induced by conditioning on common effects.

## Attribution boundary

Post-treatment bias, collider selection, positivity, total effects, controlled direct effects, and mediation are established causal-inference ideas. A4A does not rename them or claim them as new mathematics. The scoped project contribution is to apply those distinctions to A3Z's proposed formation-matching clause and to show exactly why an apparently stronger match can either manufacture a residual contrast or remove the retentive organizational path that the UCT application intended to test.
