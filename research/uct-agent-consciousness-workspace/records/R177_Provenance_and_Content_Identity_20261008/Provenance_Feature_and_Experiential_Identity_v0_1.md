# Provenance, represented feature, and experiential identity

R177 v0.1 — 8 October 2026. Unpublished UCT research note; no new human experiment.

## 1. Result, with the previous advance preserved

IA20261008 §10 supplies physical source identity invariant under legal retiming. It explicitly leaves named experiential content open. R177 does not refute that result or repeat its reachability proof. It identifies a further distinction inside the word “content”: **which occurrence a signal descends from** and **which feature its present consumer represents** can vary separately.

The constructive result is a selected source-and-feature binding that survives delay and a compensated code change, but detects an uncompensated feature change even when provenance is fixed. This lets a future order comparison keep its functional feature targets fixed without defining them by temporal rank. It does not identify a human quality such as red, the feeling of acting, or familiar mineness. The current literature check supplies no actual instance of the complete UCT-to-quality bridge.

This distinction matters to the author's question about intelligence and experience. A system can maintain, translate and use a feature distinction across a different encoding or a delay. That is a specified information-processing competence. Under C1 its grounded organization has an experiential structural counterpart. Neither competence nor the same decoded feature establishes the same complete experience, named quality, report or self-related feeling. No new competence threshold for basal experience is introduced.

## 2. The target must say which identity it means

| Object | Fixed meaning here | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Source occurrence identity s(u) | The unique admitted source event connected to use u by the IA trace relation | Present feature value or experienced quality |
| Source role rho(s) | Prediction-source or feedback-source port in the declared episode | Actual ownership of every source, felt agency or a unique subject |
| Payload z(u) | The code physically delivered to a selected consumer | Meaning from bit equality alone |
| Executed interpretation c(u) | The actual output of the declared decoder at that use | Phenomenal quality, correctness outside this finite domain, or verbal report |
| Candidate experiential label Q | Independently specified content/quality and occurrence target | Not defined as c(u), source name, temporal rank or classifier output |
| Evidence for Q | An explicitly scoped, fallible measurement/interpretive relation | The target itself or a basal-experience condition |

A source occurrence can have two descendants at different times; therefore source identity alone is not one-to-one use-event identity. A source role can also recur across trials. Our temporal comparison fixes one use per nominated source within one episode. Additional descendants, same-feature repeats or merged sources require finer binding or an explicit set-valued result. Do not repair ambiguity by calling whichever item occurs first “the B content.”

## 3. A fully specified finite construction

Keep IA's two distinct source events B and C and disjoint source-to-use paths, with one use UB and UC. Independently fix their roles as action-prediction marker and sensory-feedback marker. Each source can carry one feature c in the abstract alphabet {0,1}. These are two task-defined feature categories, not two asserted qualia. Every feature is admitted at either source; this is an explicit four-case toy domain, not a biological independence claim.

The encoder is e(c)=c. A receiver has an actually executed decoder d(z)=z. At each use it writes d(z) into a feature register that a separate equality comparator consumes. The comparator produces Match=[c(UB)=c(UC)]. It is a functional match signal, not felt familiarity or a report. No operation inspects temporal rank when determining c.

Source-role and feature are thus crossed:

| Source role | Feature 0 | Feature 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction B | (B,0) | (B,1) |
| Feedback C | (C,0) | (C,1) |

Same-source/different-feature cases are alternative episodes on a fixed source-role convention, with different source states. They are not two simultaneous payloads for one fixed event. Different-source/same-feature cases are two events in one episode. Source provenance and decoded feature therefore neither determine one another in this declared class. IA's source identities remain well defined in every case.

Compare three stipulated implementations along the same role-labeled trace paths:

1. Baseline: e sends c, transmission is identity, d is identity.
2. Compensated recoding: transmission sends g(z)=1−z, and the installed receiver is changed to d'(z)=1−z. Its executed interpretation is still c.
3. Uncompensated transformation: transmission sends 1−z but the installed receiver remains d(z)=z. Its executed interpretation is now 1−c.

Both transmission operations depend on their source input. This is not a disconnected replay or an assertion that graph reachability alone proves informational influence. The complete physical circuits differ; only the named trace topology/source roles are held fixed. Changing d' is a real circuit change, not an analyst relabeling a failed answer after inspection. All implementations and their finite input domains are declared before evaluation.

## 4. What is proved and what is not

**R177-P1, selected feature preservation under compensated recoding.** Let C,Z,Z' be finite sets, e:C→Z and d:Z→C satisfy d(e(c))=c for every admitted c. Let g:Z→Z' be a bijection. If the transmitted payload is g(e(c)) and the installed consumer is d'=d∘g⁻¹, then d'(g(e(c)))=c for every admitted c.

Proof: substitute and cancel g⁻¹∘g, then apply d∘e=id_C. QED. This is classical functional composition, not a new mathematics result. Without the installed compensation premise the conclusion fails: the two-bit example gives 1−c instead of c. A bijective transmission alone does not preserve the fixed receiver's interpretation. A noninjective transmission can merge categories and cannot generally be undone on both by any deterministic decoder.

**R177-P2, a delay-independent selected binding.** Define J(u)=(s(u),c(u)) for the selected one-source/one-use binding. In the stateless model above, legal retiming that preserves source states, executed paths and encoder/decoder operations preserves J. Compensated recoding also preserves J. Uncompensated bit inversion preserves s but changes c and hence J. Distinct source events can have the same c while keeping distinct J.

Proof: IA-4 preserves s under the stipulated retiming; the time-free executed maps preserve c, and P1 covers compensated recoding. The explicit bit-inversion and crossed-source witnesses prove the failures of the converse/omitted assumptions. A valid delayed schedule is B source/relay/use at 1/2.5/3 and C at 2/2.1/2.2. A baseline is B at 1/1.1/1.2 with C unchanged. Every trace edge remains forward. Use order reverses, but each J is preserved if its admitted operations are unchanged. QED.

The independence from time is a premise of this stateless model, not a universal nervous-system result. Decay, temporal adaptation, stateful context and time-dependent decoding can break it. With repeated equal features, c alone cannot identify which event is meant; retaining the source-use binding prevents collapsing two occurrences. With multiple sources at a use, the singleton J is undefined under the IA convention, and a set-valued extension must be declared instead.

The construction supplies a positive *functional* content-preservation condition for R176's selected comparison. It does not supply the map from that content to a named experienced quality. A decoder may be executed in a process with no language report; the definition neither adds nor removes experience. Matching selected J across two implementations is not complete K-isomorphism and does not license global experiential equivalence.

## 5. The main-line consequence for action prediction and feedback

In the four-case toy domain, actual source relation and represented agreement are separate axes:

- Prediction and feedback can be source-distinct but feature-matched.
- The prediction-source path can remain intact while its consumer receives a transformed feature, producing a mismatch.
- Compensating the representation can restore the selected match despite changed raw code.
- Delaying the prediction can reverse use order while preserving source binding and feature match.

These are explicit organizational possibilities, not four kinds of experience inferred from a table. They prevent two shortcuts: “from my prediction path” does not mean “the same represented content,” and “prediction matches feedback” does not mean “this feels mine.” A conceptual-I sentence is another possible consumer, not the owner that generates these relations. Familiarity and agency are not equated to Match=1.

The result gives a concrete way to formulate the next bodily-self hypothesis: hold task-feature content and event bindings fixed while changing the actual self-related source/use relation, or hold the source relation fixed while changing feature agreement. Which contrast changes a separately admitted felt-mineness target remains an empirical/semantic question. This round does not restart a generic control program or claim that all self-feelings are decomposable into two bits.

## 6. What primary evidence does and does not supply

Nishida and Johnston (2002), *Current Biology* 12:359–368, DOI 10.1016/S0960-9822(02)00698-X, proposed temporal-marker correspondence rather than differential processing delay for the visual effects they studied. Only the publisher abstract was retrieved here; full-text access failed. This is a scoped precedent against assuming that one latency automatically names experienced order, not a proof against every H_use implementation.

Huang, Su and Zhou (2023), *Journal of Vision* 23(1):6, DOI 10.1167/jov.23.1.6, used feature-correspondence and temporal-order tasks. Their correspondence-task effects differed by task, while temporal-order judgments and response-time/model measures did not give interchangeable summaries. We inspected abstract, task methods and relevant results, without raw-data reanalysis. This cautions against importing an identity or timing measure across tasks without an explicit bridge.

Our inference from these sources is limited: a physically named stimulus feature and a task response are useful evidence, but neither establishes IA's actual complete source/use interpretation or the named-quality restriction of C1. No actual case completing that restriction was found in this scoped check. The papers do not establish our binary transducer or prove that human contents can be varied independently of all timing/context variables. They are not evidence that reports are worthless or that nonreporting systems lack experience.

Source links: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096098220200698X ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9838589/ . The work is not claimed as new discovery of temporal binding, code invariance or source/content separability.

## 7. Conditional UCT attachment and explicit failure of the tempting shortcut

For an actual P/I/K, independently ground the source, trace, code transformation, actual decoder execution, feature-register and comparator-use relations. Under A:C1 and the already established R157 transport theorem, the corresponding grounded formulas and all parameters transport to Phi_K(P). This is an application of existing theory, not a newly counted consciousness theorem. It places source association, feature distinction and feature matching inside the stipulated experiential organization, without introducing an extra owner.

The attempted shortcut was: source identity is known, therefore the named experiential content is fixed. It fails twice. Within the constructed finite model, source identity does not fix even the executed feature. After that feature is fixed, R157's semantic residual still prevents silently identifying it with an externally named phenomenal quality. This is not an arbitrary assignment of two experiences to one complete actual organization: the finite circuit alternatives differ physically, and the further naming alternatives are epistemic/semantic, not established distinct actual qualia.

The positive gain is correspondingly exact: R176's order comparison can now freeze both physical provenance and an executed feature distinction under a stated transformation class. It can no longer treat a feature-changing delay/encoding manipulation as automatically “same content.” Whether such functional constancy suffices for the intended phenomenal target is left open rather than inserted as a theorem.

## 8. Four thought-experiment families and audit

- Ancestor/formation: an organism may acquire a predictive feature match while already having basal experience in UCT. Learning this operation is not the onset of experience or conceptual I.
- Abacus/calculator: static code labels alone do not execute e, transmission and d. A specified coupled mechanical implementation may execute them; that selected competence does not settle its named qualities.
- Human-realized agent: fix the relevant implemented process and consumer, without substituting the participants' reports or experience for the implemented target. Nested/overlapping bearers remain permitted.
- Copy/rewiring/memory: raw-bit identity can preserve a code while the decoder changes; raw bits can also change while a compensating decoder preserves the selected feature. Neither comparison alone proves transfer of a subject, preserved complete experience or familiar recollection.

All conjunctions refer to one declared instance; compensated and uncompensated circuits are alternative models. Source events, use events, code types, task-feature types, phenomenal names and evidence are not interchanged. Internal feature/register equality is not experiential identity. No new edge concludes F_O, F_A or F_F. R175/R176/IA effective amendments remain in force; QC-08–10 and IA-QC11 are not self-closed.
