## Current primary empirical sources

1. D'Onofrio Pacheco, P. N., & Zimmermann, E. (2026). *Movement enhances tactile sensitivity through prediction*. Journal of Neurophysiology 135(4):888–895. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00558.2025. Read scope: PubMed/publisher abstract and accessible primary article text/snippets. Used only for matched-kinematics distinctions among perceived intensity, discrimination precision and passive-movement predictability. No raw data were reanalysed.
2. D'Onofrio Pacheco, P. N., & Zimmermann, E. (2026). *Effects of prediction and attention on tactile precision in somatosensory gating*. Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics 88:145. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-026-03288-7. Published 11 June 2026. Read scope: complete publisher abstract, PubMed record, open-practices statement and accessible full-text excerpt. Used for the active/passive/still by attention design and the distinct perceptual-bias versus precision pattern. Data/code at Zenodo/GitHub were identified but not downloaded or reanalysed.
3. Kilteni, K., Engeler, P., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2020). *Efference copy is necessary for the attenuation of self-generated touch*. iScience 23:100843. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2020.100843. Scope inherited from R205/SCU: abstract and accessible excerpts, not raw-data inspection. Its protocol-specific active result is not treated as universal motor necessity.
4. Kilteni, K., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2017). *Body ownership determines the attenuation of self-generated tactile sensations*. PNAS 114(31):8426–8431. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1703347114. Read scope: PubMed abstract and figure captions. Used to show that body-state/ownership manipulation can change attenuation and therefore may be a context variable; not used to identify a complete phenomenal target.
5. Kalckert, A., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2012). *Moving a rubber hand that feels like your own: a dissociation of ownership and agency*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 6:40. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040. Read scope: PubMed abstract and figure captions. Used for protocol-level dissociation of reported ownership and agency; not for a report-independent phenomenal identity claim.
6. Kilteni, K., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2022). *Predictive attenuation of touch and tactile gating are distinct perceptual phenomena*. iScience 25:104077. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.104077. Read scope: indexed full-text abstract/excerpts. Used as prior-art support for separating predictive attenuation from tactile gating, not as a source of new quantitative claims.
7. Zaadnoordijk, L., Besold, T. R., & Hunnius, S. (2019). *A match does not make a sense: on the sufficiency of the comparator model for explaining the sense of agency*. Neuroscience of Consciousness 2019(1):niz006. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz006. Scope inherited from SCU. Used only for the prior insufficiency of match alone for causal self-attribution.

## Source-derived versus derived claims

The studies provide protocol-specific endpoint constraints. MPC-C1–C4 and the eager/short-circuit countermodel are new derivations/applications checked by `model.py`; they are not claimed to have been reported by those experiments. MPC-C5 is an inference from source-described patterns, explicitly qualitative and non-anatomical. C1/U1 interpretation is conditional on UCT's internal axiom and actual-instance premises, not an empirical conclusion from the cited data.
