# R194 primary-source scope

## Sources used

1. Kunkel, D. and Peruggia, M. (2020). “Anchored Bayesian Gaussian Mixture Models.” *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 14(2), 3869–3913. Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08304. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/20-EJS1769
   - Used only for the established point that exchangeable mixture labels are arbitrary and anchors break label invariance by an added membership assumption.
   - Not evidence for phenomenal target semantics, UCT C1 or `B_fam`.

2. Pohl, S., Schulze, D., and Stets, E. (2021). “Partial Measurement Invariance: Extending and Evaluating the Cluster Approach for Identifying Anchor Items.” *Applied Psychological Measurement* 45(7–8), 477–493. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8640350/ ; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/01466216211042809
   - Used for cross-group scale indeterminacy, the need for anchor assumptions and the distinction between relative item functioning and substantive anchor choice.
   - Not used to claim that psychometric invariance measures experience.

3. Allman, E. S., Matias, C., and Rhodes, J. A. (2009). “Identifiability of parameters in latent structure models with many observed variables.” *Annals of Statistics* 37(6A), 3099–3132. https://doi.org/10.1214/09-AOS689
   - Used for the established background that latent-structure parameters may be identifiable only up to class permutation.

4. Chen, Y., Li, C., Ouyang, J., and Xu, G. (2023). “DIF Statistical Inference without Knowing Anchoring Items.” *Psychometrika* 88, 1095–1122. Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11112
   - Used only as a warning that “anchor-free” identification still relies on an explicit identifying condition (there, a sparsity/minimal-norm condition); it does not deliver phenomenal polarity for free.

## Claim boundary

No source above is cited as evidence that a marker is constitutive of experience, that a particular latent class is familiar mineness, or that any current artificial system is conscious. R194's exact result is independently reproducible from `check_polarity_graph.py` and uses standard signed-graph parity reasoning.
