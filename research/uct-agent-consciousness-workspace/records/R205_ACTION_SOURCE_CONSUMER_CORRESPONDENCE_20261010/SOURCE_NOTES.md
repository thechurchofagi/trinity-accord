# Source constraints, not phenomenal validation

[Kilteni, Engeler and Ehrsson (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2020.100843), online 16 January 2020, compared active, passive and external triggering of self-touch. Its indexed abstract reports attenuation for active triggering. Read scope: abstract/figure-caption retrieval, not full methods or participant data. Its source-sensitive result is tied to the stated protocol; it cannot serve as a universal experience or agency gate.

[D’Onofrio Pacheco and Zimmermann (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00558.2025), ahead of print 17 March and online issue 2 April 2026, compared active/passive movement with matched kinematics. The publisher retrieval separates reduced perceived intensity from improved tactile sensitivity. Its [public data link](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17414491) was identified but not downloaded or reanalysed. R205 uses the distinction between readouts as a design constraint, without fitting its model to those observations.

[Johnen, Bayer and Zimmermann's preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6545167), posted 9 April 2026, reports passive transport with attenuation comparable to self-generated movement, tied to proprioceptive predictability. Direct access returned 403; only its primary abstract was retrieved through search. The provisional finding motivates a proprioceptive alternative to motor-copy-only necessity. Different protocols and uninspected methods prevent treating it as a definitive refutation of the earlier experiment.

Our inference: a mechanism comparison should hold stimulus/kinematics fixed where appropriate and separately specify motor-source consumption, proprioceptive-source consumption, intensity, discrimination and the selected self-related target. None of these papers identifies R205's physical source registers, supplies a complete constitutive signature, or validates C1 or H. No raw-data evidence is claimed for the exact model.
