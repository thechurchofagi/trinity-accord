# Prior art and evidence boundary

R191 uses established agency research as a constraint, not as proof of UCT's actual or phenomenal premises.

- Chambon & Haggard (2012), *Sense of control depends on fluency of action selection, not motor performance*, Cognition 125(3), 441–451, DOI: `10.1016/j.cognition.2012.07.011`. Action-selection fluency can influence control judgments. This blocks any novelty claim that conflict/fluency is related to agency.
- Sidarus et al. (2017), *Investigating the Prospective Sense of Agency: Effects of Processing Fluency, Stimulus Ambiguity, and Response Conflict*, Frontiers in Psychology 8:545, DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00545`. Three experiments orthogonally manipulate prospective cues and outcome discrepancy; conflict/fluency effects are smaller than, and can be overridden by, retrospective discrepancy. This supports cue plurality and non-identity.
- Berberian et al. (2012), *Automation Technology and Sense of Control*, PLOS ONE 7(4):e34075, DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0034075`. Automation and operator involvement modulate intentional binding and explicit control ratings. This constrains action-selection involvement but does not establish an experience structure.

Internal overlap is also explicit. R173 supplies the general actual-history/carrier/consumer lesson; R184 supplies an earlier reflex-twin limit; R190 supplies the fixed PC+BDCC background. R191 does not rename these results. Its bounded increment is the OCCA timing/authorization contract, the exact post-hoc failure, and the current-signature/history-signature fork.

Evidence ladder: finite construction → logical existence/nonentailment; behavioral or timing data → evidence about selected cues under a protocol; actual installed route → separate carrier/consumer/timing premise; named feeling → further interpretive bridge. No lower rung entails a higher one by itself.
