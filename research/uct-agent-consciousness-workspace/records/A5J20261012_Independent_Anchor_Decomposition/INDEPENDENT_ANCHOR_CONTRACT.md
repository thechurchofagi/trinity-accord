# Independent-Anchor Contract

## Intended target

Fix one participant, held tool, interval, binary physical contact coordinate `P`, independently crossed nuisance cue `C`, selected experiential-location target `H`, and two preregistered channels `Z` and `A`. The proposed conclusion is the domain-bounded identity `H=P`, not basal experience, ownership of the tool, a conceptual self, or the truth of C1.

## Simultaneous obligations (`all_of`)

| ID | Obligation | Exact content | What does **not** establish it | Failure-capable test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F` | factorial cue exclusion | all four `P×C` cells are realized and both channels track `P` rather than `C` | ordinary trials with `P=C`; high accuracy | a `P≠C` cell in which either channel follows `C` |
| `SD` | semantic source diversity | one channel reads the selected `H` source and the other reads the independent `P` reference | different sensors, electrodes, classifiers, labels, or anatomical sites | a preregistered cut/rescue or swap showing that both channels can be generated by the same direct-`P` route |
| `CO` | common orientation | the two source-to-output maps have the same prospectively fixed polarity | equal observed outputs, post-hoc label alignment, or a shared response mapping | a polarity reversal/remapping that preserves output agreement only by complementing one source |

Within the declared finite model, `F AND SD AND CO` identifies the target law `H=P`. It leaves two selector-swapped implementations because the output table alone does not identify which named channel reads `H`. That residual is harmless for the target-law conclusion but decisive for any anatomical or mechanistic source claim.

## Exact leave-one-out result

| Conditions | Compatible models | Compatible `H` laws |
|---|---:|---:|
| `F` only | 12 | 6 |
| `F+SD` | 4 | 2 (`P`, `notP`) |
| `F+CO` | 10 | 6 |
| `F+SD+CO` | 2 | 1 (`P`) |

Thus `SD` and `CO` are each necessary relative to the other for target-law identification in this family. `F` excludes a direct `C` channel but does not exclude a direct `P` channel. Hardware multiplicity is already present in the 12-model baseline and changes no count.

## Evidential boundary

`SD` is partly a source-admission premise: calling one path an `H` reader already requires an independently warranted target bridge. `CO` is a polarity-transport premise. Neither follows from agreement with `P`. The contract can therefore reject a proposed anchor, and it can state what a successful prospective design would have to establish, but it cannot manufacture a phenomenal source from nonphenomenal output convergence.

No clause is a necessary condition for basal experience under UCT. Reports and keypresses may be fallible downstream evidence; they do not define `H`.
