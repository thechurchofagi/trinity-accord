# Overlapping bearer-centres without an exclusive owner

A4W20261012 · `A4W-OCF-v0.1.0` · disabled UCT application and exact finite result.

## 1. Question and answer

A4V identified a local `BearerCentredRoute` (`BCR`) but left open whether two simultaneous, partly overlapping centred routes create a contradiction or demand a further selector. A4W gives a two-level answer.

First, there is no formal inconsistency in having multiple local centres. `BCR` is indexed by process, event, target, anchor, route, and consumer. A set with two differently bound satisfying tuples is an ordinary many-member relation, not two incompatible values of a function. Under C1/R157, if the whole binding is actual and complete, both relations are transported as structure inside experience. Nothing in that transport selects one extra owner.

Second, two local centres do not by themselves show that one downstream action or target is organized through both. That stronger target-relative claim needs actual non-exclusive participation edges from both routes to the named downstream consumer. This is not a unique-owner selector: the relation can have zero, one, or many members.

## 2. Positive organizational proposal

The full disabled contract is `ConcurrentBCR2`: the same `P/I/K/event/target` binding contains two A4V BCR conjunctions, both active in the event, with separately bound live anchor and route roles. Physical overlap is allowed: the routes may share target, event, consumer, substrate, or intermediate occurrences when the relevant ports remain typed. Equal values do not collapse the roles.

For a named target activity `tau`, define `Participates_tau(r_i,m,e)` only when the target consumer actually reads/uses the occurrence descending from route `r_i`. Then

```text
JointlyCentred_tau = ConcurrentBCR2
  AND Participates_tau(r_1,m,e)
  AND Participates_tau(r_2,m,e).
```

The `AND` is essential. Two local BCRs distributed across the same organization are not a substitute for their downstream use.

## 3. Exact result

The finite witness uses two XOR bearer-centred encoders/decoders and enumerates all sixteen downstream Boolean mergers. Both local decoded coordinates equal the same target, so normal operation visits only diagonal merger inputs. Exactly four mergers reproduce the target: two one-branch projections and two symmetric two-input mergers (AND/OR). They are indistinguishable on every normal row.

This establishes three bounded points:

1. correct output does not require a unique selector;
2. correct output plus two local BCR traces does not establish joint downstream use;
3. off-diagonal branch challenges and an independently grounded consumer are needed to distinguish selection from merger.

The result does not show that AND/OR is the unique or preferred physical organization. It shows only that an exclusive selector is not logically required and that a non-exclusive participation premise is the right missing type for the target-relative claim.

## 4. Counterexamples

- **Projection countermodel:** both local BCRs exist, but the target consumer uses only one branch. This defeats `ConcurrentBCR2 -> JointlyCentred_tau`.
- **Direct bypass:** `z=x` while both route traces are merely recorded. This defeats output-to-use reversal.
- **Diagonal aliasing:** projection, AND, and OR agree on normal operation. This defeats inference from success and passive logs.
- **Copied/stale anchor:** a copied anchor bit can preserve every value while failing current live binding. This defeats value-to-actuality reversal.
- **Body or tool labels:** anatomical membership and extra-organismic location do not determine the internal centring relation, preserving A4N's repair.
- **After-the-fact report:** a single “mine” report can be shared by projection and merger worlds. It cannot install either participation edge.
