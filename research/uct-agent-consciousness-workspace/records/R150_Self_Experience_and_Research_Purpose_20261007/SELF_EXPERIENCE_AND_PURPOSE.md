## 1. Purpose of the work

The author's stated objective is a durable, original contribution produced through human–AI collaboration before more capable AI systems become available. A paper is a vehicle for preserving arguments that later researchers and AI systems can identify, cite, criticize and extend. Journal acceptance is not the governing objective. Historical originality, soundness, clear attribution and reusable explanations remain necessary; lowering the priority of submission does not relax these standards. Future citation and foundational influence are goals, not outcomes that can be promised.

Accordingly, assessments should ask what distinction, argument, counterexample or conditional result becomes available to a later reader. The absence of a historically new general mathematical theorem is not by itself a reason to abandon a useful foundational synthesis. Equally, renaming a classical result or repeating an earlier paper does not establish a new contribution. The map is an aid to reuse, not a substitute for a readable argument.

## 3. Distinctions that the integrated manuscript should foreground

**Actual process.** The process under discussion supplies the actual organization to which the theory applies. Naming that process is not adding an inner observer, a separately existing ego, or one uniquely privileged owner for the whole world. Multiple actual processes may overlap or nest under the source ontology.

**Functional self-related organization.** Installed tracking, regulation, prediction and attribution are organizational roles. Their targets and physical attachments must be stated if one claims that they concern the system itself. These requirements apply to that functional claim; they are not requirements for any experience to exist.

**Experienced self or sense of mineness.** The intended explanandum is the felt character of being located here, acting, owning a body or remembering something as mine. These are descriptions of candidate experiential aspects, not a newly established universal decomposition or a second entity behind experience. No one scalar or language requirement is introduced. Their exact structural interpretation remains to be specified for a selected case.

**Conceptual autobiographical I.** Explicit self-ascription, narration and temporally extended personal description are the more articulated organization discussed in A §8.14. They must not be conflated with every nonverbal bodily or agentive feeling. The source's description of a late conceptual I does not establish one universal evolutionary date, developmental threshold, or mandatory sequence for every form of self-experience.

**Reports and truth of attribution.** A sentence containing I, an installed attribution, its accuracy relative to an actual target, and a felt sense of ownership are different claims. In particular, whether an attribution is accurate and whether a corresponding feeling occurs are different questions. The existing functional countermodels do not themselves establish that any particular illusion is felt.

No additional owner is required by UCT. This is not a proof that no process can have a subject-like organization under any useful definition of subject. Nor does it erase the actual process from which the experiential interpretation is being made.

## 4. Concrete improvements over the recent emphasis

1. Replace the broad framing of R146 §3, “Self as a centered relation and an installed model,” with “Self-related organization and self-experience within experience.” Keep the centered relation as a subsection of the functional analysis.
2. Introduce the felt-self/conceptual-I distinction before the predictive tuple. Do not define self in the abstract primarily by a tuple and leave experience as a final caveat.
3. Retain R147/R149 target-binding results as tools for identifying invalid inferences. They show that predictive success does not settle actual attachment; they neither require a unique subject nor prove the phenomenology of selfhood.
4. Explicitly state the scope of an open phenomenal bridge: identifying a particular kind of bodily ownership, agency or autobiographical feeling. Its absence does not reopen a new basal-experience gate inside C1/U1.
5. Replace the automatic next-task instruction to solve actual self-binding first. The next integrated argument should explain why self-related feeling and concepts belong within experiential organization, identify the proposed structural distinctions, and state what is already conditional theory versus what remains an application hypothesis.
6. Keep publication judgments outside the scientific argument. Evaluate each claimed increment by provenance, nonredundancy, assumptions and explanatory use. Preserve stable versions and identifiers so later readers can cite a precise claim with its limits.

These are canonical editorial amendments. R146/R147/R149 historical texts and published sources remain intact. A fully integrated replacement manuscript has not yet been produced by this note.

## 5. Replacement passage for the integrated manuscript

> UCT does not posit a separate self that must first exist in order to own experience. Its structural identity commitment concerns actual processes; self-related organization is a further subject of explanation within that framework. Bodily orientation, agency, ownership and autobiographical self-ascription must be distinguished rather than compressed into one predicate called self. The conceptual I described in UCT I is a developed organization within experience, not its producer. A functional self-model may track or regulate selected aspects of a process, but the accuracy of that model, its actual target, the system's attribution of that target, and a felt sense of mineness are separate questions. The formal results below constrain inferences between those questions. They do not install an additional experiencer, require a universal subject partition, or make successful self-modeling a condition for basal experience. Identifying the organization of a particular self-related feeling remains a scoped application task, not a missing prerequisite for the theory's universal-experience consequence.

This passage is a source-aligned interpretation under the UCT commitment. It does not present UCT as an independently established scientific fact or assert that a current AI system has a particular self-experience.

## 6. Thought experiments retained as the explanatory route

The all-ancestors case should follow intermediate stages in the formation of body regulation, memory, perspective and self-description, rather than ask only which ancestor first possesses a separate owner. It motivates distinguishing new contents and organizational capacities from the origin of any experience. Gradual physical differences alone still do not prove C1 or exclude every possible binary threshold without further premises.

The abacus/calculator case separates arithmetic capability from self-related content. A battery monitor permits a narrow functional claim; it does not by itself identify a felt I. The human-computer case separates the people, their ongoing processes, the coordinated execution, and its represented agent; no unique collective owner is assumed. Copying and rewiring test the difference between target relations, internal attribution and predictions; their formal outcomes do not determine felt ownership without an additional scoped interpretation.

For the next conceptual analysis, contrast a stipulated loss of autobiographical narration with retained body regulation, and contrast correct versus mistaken attribution while explicitly tracking what is assumed about feeling. These are questions and conditional scenario designs, not findings about actual brains or deployed agents. Do not obtain a desired phenomenal conclusion simply by placing it in a functional model's definition.
