## Primary empirical sources checked

1. Farrer, C. et al. (2003). *The role of proprioception in action recognition: a study of a deafferented patient*. Neuropsychologia 41:541–548. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1053-8100(03)00047-3. Used for active/passive action-recognition evidence and the deafferented case; not for an all-input block.
2. Gandevia, S. C. et al. (1990). *Voluntary activation of human motor axons in the absence of muscle afferent feedback*. Brain 113:1563–1581. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/113.5.1563. Used for preserved voluntary motor-axon recruitment after muscle-afferent block; cutaneous feedback remained.
3. Gandevia, S. C. et al. (2006). *The perception of illusory and non-illusory movement: a study using ischemic anaesthesia of the arm*. Journal of Physiology. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2005.103093. Used for motor-effort-linked phantom movement; no normal limb movement/contact was preserved.
4. Walsh, L. D. et al. (2010). *Illusory movements of a phantom hand grade with the duration and magnitude of motor commands*. Journal of Physiology. Used for graded motor-command contribution under experimental phantom conditions; no matched actual movement.
5. Whitham, E. M. et al. (2011). *Visual experiences during paralysis*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 5:160. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00160. Used for attempted-gaze effects during neuromuscular paralysis; ocular domain and report endpoint only.
6. Jayasinghe, S. A. L. et al. (2024). *Goal-directed reaching with profound proprioceptive loss*. Experimental Brain Research. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-024-06901-z. Used for action with continuous visual compensation in case GL; chronic adaptation prevents a reversible frozen-token interpretation.

The absence claim is restricted to the inspected source set through 10 October 2026, not an exhaustive proof that no ethical human approximation exists. The formal proposition is a new scoped application of elementary conjunction, not a claim of inventing Boolean logic or sensory deafferentation.
