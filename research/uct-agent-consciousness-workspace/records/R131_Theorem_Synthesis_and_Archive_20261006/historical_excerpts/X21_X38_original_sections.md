### X21. Three returning routes, none individually indispensable

**Attack:** the naturally used return must be identified by a nonzero effect of replacing one carrier at a time.

Three carriers all hold `z XOR c`. The internal return update is their majority. Replacing any one with the alternative seed's value leaves the output unchanged; replacing any two flips it. A real source distinction has travelled out and returned, yet every baseline single-carrier test is zero.

**Outcome:** single-carrier necessity is false. Coalitions identify a redundancy-masked pathway. This is a causal false negative, not a demonstration that the circuit is conscious. **Executed.**

### X22. Many minimal witnesses, not many subjects

With five agreeing return carriers, every triple is a minimal effective replacement. There are ten such triples, each overlapping several others. With nine carriers there are 126 minimal effective five-carrier replacements. These are alternative counterfactual witnesses for one logical return update, not ten or 126 experienced worlds.

**Outcome:** count distinctions, causal witnesses, executions, and perspectives separately. **Executed.**

### X23. A genuinely unused route remains unused collectively

Retain the previous fake-return construction: the natural final update uses the locally retained seed and context, ignoring every returned carrier. Replacing one, several, or all returned values has no effect as long as the mode is preserved.

**Outcome:** coalition testing rescues redundant causal use without automatically rescuing the previous unused-route artifact. **Executed control.**

### X24. Two changes cancel

Let the final update be `Y=M1 XOR M2`. At `00`, replacing either one by `1` changes `Y`; replacing both restores `Y=0`. Larger replacements need not have larger effects.

**Outcome:** a minimal-witness list can lose information. Retain the full intervention-response family unless monotonicity is separately established. Exhaustive three-carrier readout checks confirm that this limitation is common in that finite family. **Executed.**

### X25. A protected distinction with diminishing single-carrier sensitivity

Each redundant carrier flips independently with probability 0.1 before the majority update. Increasing odd redundancy from one to nine raises the correct logical-return probability from 0.9 to 0.99910908. Meanwhile, the probability that changing one chosen carrier changes the majority falls from 1 to 0.0045927.

**Outcome:** reliable information retention and local perturbation sensitivity can move in opposite directions. Neither can be renamed “amount of experience.” **Executed and derived.**

### X26. One corrupted cable feeding every replica

Replace independent carrier errors with one shared error bit copied to all carriers. Majority voting now has error probability 0.1, regardless of redundancy.

**Outcome:** many physical paths are not automatically independent evidence or independent fault tolerance. Common-cause structure matters. **Executed.**

### X27. Near-perfect retention is not infinite survival

A recurrent memory repeatedly re-encodes its current majority output and then suffers fresh independent noise. Each cycle has a small but positive probability of a logical flip. Net memory of the initial bit decays with the number of cycles, even though a flip can later flip back.

**Outcome:** finite-horizon robustness is not permanent preservation, let alone proof of personal survival. **Executed exact Markov calculation.**

### X28. The same present content, different distance from failure

The triples `000` and `001` both decode to logical zero. Under independent 0.1 bit-flip noise applied *before* the next majority readout, their probabilities of producing one are 0.028 and 0.172.

**Outcome:** current decoded content alone is not a sufficient state for the future disturbance response. This is a specific abstraction failure, not evidence that the two states have different experiences. **Executed.**

### X29. A repairing system and an off-line technician

One mechanism contains the voter and feeds its correction directly into the next update. Another merely leaves a corrupted record that a scientist can later decode. The records might support the same off-line answer, but only the first contains the specified internal repair loop.

**Outcome:** an analyst's decoding ability does not establish endogenous repair. The technician can be included in an expanded causal system, but expanding the boundary is a new model and does not settle a subject boundary. **Conceptual; no biological procedure performed.**

### X30. More reliable content is not more kinds of content

Nine copies protect one logical bit. They have at most two logical codewords and at most one bit of information about that bit. Nine independent logical bits can encode 512 alternatives but need not form a new integrated whole.

**Outcome:** redundancy can improve fidelity without expanding the content alphabet. Exact copied observations, independent noisy observations, and independent logical contents require different accounting. **Derived; finite channel checks.**

### X31. A coordinate change turns a small disturbance into a large one

Encode three physical bits as `(x0, x0 XOR x1, x0 XOR x2)`. A single flip of physical `x0` changes all three displayed coordinates. Conversely, a single change of the first displayed coordinate can represent three physical flips.

**Outcome:** retaining “one-bit noise” after an arbitrary recoding changes the physical disturbance model. A true relabeling carries both decoder and perturbations. **Executed across all 168 invertible three-bit linear encodings.**

### X32. Correct every possible logical rule

Encode each bit of an arbitrary finite binary state in an odd repetition block. Decode each block, apply any desired logical rule, and re-encode. A bounded number of within-block errors is corrected before the logical update.

**Outcome:** correction is an implementation resource available to ordinary storage, feedforward maps, erasure maps, contextual recurrence, and complicated reversible computation. It is not intrinsically a selector for experience. Every two-bit rule is explicitly checked. **Executed and proved.**

### X33. A protected reversible scrambler

Use X32 on a large reversible, context-sensitive recurrent computation. Arrange actual internal decoding and recurring use rather than an off-line readout. It can be rich and fault tolerant without being biological.

**Outcome:** the candidate must either allow such a mechanism as a possible experiential realization or state an independently motivated further condition. Merely adding repair does not remove the v0.4 hard case. **General construction; no experience attribution.**

### X34. A narrow, stable experience in a rich supporting system

Consider a conditionally stipulated experiential episode with a single stable content, sustained by a much larger capable mechanism. No new statistical variation or error-correction event need occur during the observed interval.

**Outcome:** content simplicity is not the same as simple physical support. Actual repairs need not occur for a repair capacity to exist. The case does not independently prove that a one-bit device experiences. **Conditional scenario.**

### X35. Richness without needing protection

Consider an ideal noise-free mechanism retaining many organized alternatives. Adding a “must demonstrably correct an error” condition could reject it merely because the error never occurs or is outside its actual environment.

**Outcome:** observed error correction is not adopted as a universal necessary condition. Disturbance tolerance can be a relational property without an actual error event. **Conditional scenario.**

### X36. Same robust content, opposite reward labels

Change the label of an external performance statistic while leaving the return, encoding, and actual dynamics fixed. None of the proved reliability results acquires a pleasant or unpleasant sign.

**Outcome:** correction failures are not automatically suffering; protection is not automatically pleasure. A valence law remains additional. **Inherited control, logically reapplied.**

### X37. Two protected copies with one apparent history

Two independent implementations retain identical logical states and error-correcting structures. They have corresponding structural types but different physical event histories. One stored checkpoint restored twice creates a different causal story from one uninterrupted execution.

**Outcome:** protection cannot collapse distinct tokens or establish personal survival. No destructive intervention is recommended. **Inherited provenance scenario.**

### X38. The wrong kind of final result

Imagine declaring “a sufficiently complex, protected, recurrent system is conscious” after choosing a threshold solely to exclude the latest awkward circuit. The added condition has no independent phenomenal calibration and no new distinguishing prediction.

**Outcome:** this produces a completed sentence, not an established law. A real positive theory must accept a precise consequence even when it is unintuitive; it must also risk failure rather than choose its threshold after every counterexample. **Methodological adversarial case, not a mathematical test.**
