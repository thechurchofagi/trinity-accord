## 1. The decision and the purpose it serves

The purpose is to preserve original, inspectable knowledge that a future AI or researcher has a substantive reason to cite and can apply without reconstructing hidden assumptions. A DOI, a longer manuscript, a larger map, and additional successful test cases do not independently satisfy that purpose. Conversely, an elementary result is not automatically worthless: a useful new application or counterexample can deserve preservation even when its mathematical ingredients are classical.

R188 meets the latter, narrower standard. It supplies an exact finite average-error application with an explicit source-access restriction, a clean matched six-state counterexample, an attaining protocol, and reproducible code. Those are useful reference material. It does **not yet supply a sufficiently substantial additional result to justify the current broad synthesis as another independent theory paper**, once the already published UCT papers, the new MGTD and RT/TH publications, and their formal attachments are included in the baseline.

The correct disposition is therefore to preserve R188 as a versioned technical record and, if useful, develop a shorter companion supplement to the existing capability or task-relative-continuity line. The decision is not “there is no increment.” It is “the remaining increment is a particular application and benchmark, while most of the paper's larger conceptual and methodological claims already have a citable source.” The broad working manuscript's own section 9.3 is an earlier readiness judgment; this assessment supersedes that judgment for the present decision without altering the historical file.

Three findings determine this decision:

1. The elementary coding calculation is sound, but its solution follows directly from established response-table, graph-coloring, and Bayes-decision methods. External priority for the exact displayed formula remains unverified.
2. The paper's umbrella inference—restored behavior does not identify complete organization, and an ideal decoder need not be installed or have the needed inputs—is already explicit in published UCT I, UCT III, TA25, and RT/TH. The newer publications make this overlap materially larger than the earlier R188 source list indicated.
3. The synthesis currently compares separate masking mechanisms. It does not derive a new joint identification result, establish a nontrivial composition theorem under one actual organizational contract, or ground the proposed access class in an independently studied system. An organized table of those mechanisms is useful exposition; it does not turn the inherited results into an additional discovery.

## 3. What is genuinely different in R188

The new contract is precise. A source sees target \(T\) but not the reader's pair-context \(e=\{a,b\}\). The reader may send one feedback label from an alphabet of size \(L\); after receiving it, the source sends one reply from an alphabet of size \(K\). The reader then estimates \(T\). All current-input information and timing channels must be accounted for. For positive endpoint-context masses, set

\[
w_e=\min\{p(a,e),p(b,e)\},\qquad W=\sum_e w_e.
\]

The exact optimum is

\[
S^*(K,L)=1-W+\operatorname{Cut}_{K^L}(G,w).
\]

This is not identical to the route-blind problem in published RT/TH. RT/TH's route reader lacks a route label for a family of reversible transformations. R188 constrains what the source encoder sees and allows a particular interactive exchange through bounded alphabets, including nonuniform average-error scoring. Conflating those two access directions would incorrectly erase R188's particular application.

Nor does the old R133 message-cover theorem already state this weighted formula. The map audit read the originals and reports that R133 §4 concerns probability-one recovery with a free ideal deterministic encoder, and expressly excludes noisy, entropy, variable-length, and adaptive extensions. R137 includes finite-error common-interface optimization and a common-simplex LP, but not the arbitrary joint-prior encoder-budget Bayes objective used here. R188 can therefore be recorded as a specific new application within this project. It cannot be described as discovering common-action compatibility, finite-error optimization, or the source-access problem for the first time.

The matched example adds a useful control beyond UCT III §7.2. Its two organizations receive the same external uniform \((T,J)\) stimulus and are scored on the same fixed target \(T\). Their internal context routers produce the incidence structures of \(K_{3,3}\) and the triangular prism. Both have six targets, nine contexts, three contexts per target, uniform marginals, two equally likely candidates per context, and the same conditional entropy and reader-only success. Both allow perfect isolated context-aware one-bit recovery. Nevertheless, one common source-only bit yields values 1 and \(8/9\). A timely feedback bit repairs the latter. This precisely demonstrates the implementation restriction that a context-by-context ideal helper calculation omits.

Those facts are a useful benchmark. They do not establish equality of the full joint distributions: the changed target-context incidence is the difference in organization. They do not measure experience, prove physical isolation of operating-system processes, or show that a real biological or AI compensator actually has this access class. The manuscript's present qualifiers correctly preserve those boundaries.

## 4. Why the exact result is a narrow application rather than a substantial new mathematical mechanism

The reduction is immediate once the classical response-table idea is used. Each target value is assigned the word

\[
c(t)=(f(t,1),\ldots,f(t,L))\in[K]^L.
\]

There are at most \(K^L\) distinct words. If both endpoints of a reader's pair have the same word, no feedback coordinate separates them and the Bayes-optimal error in that cell is its smaller joint mass. If their words differ, the reader selects a differing coordinate and obtains perfect identification. Optimizing the word partition gives the weighted cut value. Independent randomization cannot improve a fixed-distribution average objective because conditioning on the random tapes leaves a deterministic legal protocol under the same law.

Thus the proof combines three established ingredients: response vectors for limited interaction, the characteristic-graph obstruction imposed by decoder side information, and optimal guessing within an indistinguishable observation cell. The zero-error threshold \(\chi(G)\le K^L\), the balanced complete-graph formula, and the prism's two unavoidable uncut edges are immediate corollaries. Their correctness is valuable, but they should not be counted as several independent breakthroughs.

R188's prior-art record identifies the relevant originals: Witsenhausen (1976), [DOI 10.1109/TIT.1976.1055607](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1976.1055607); Orlitsky (1990), [DOI 10.1109/18.57210](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.57210); and Charpenay, Le Treust, and Roumy (2024), [DOI 10.3390/e26040338](https://doi.org/10.3390/e26040338), whose full article directly studies giving the encoder a function of the decoder's context. The exact finite fixed-alphabet average-error objective differs from the latter's asymptotic zero-error rate problem. That distinction supports a carefully bounded application record. It does not by itself establish that the elementary formula has never appeared elsewhere. This assessment inherits the disclosed access limits in `PRIOR_ART_AND_REUSE.md`; it does not pretend that abstract-only source access was full theorem verification.

The internal top-K recovery theorem is another relevant predecessor. Its achievability construction gives the helper both \(T\) and the earlier record \(X\). R188 shows exactly why its upper bound may be unattainable when the encoder sees only \(T\). That is a good instance of applying an existing theorem's access qualification. UCT III §7.3 already warns that coupling policies across observations invalidates its pointwise optimum formulas, and UCT II already requires source-anchored, non-oracular construction. The six-state graph makes that warning calculable; it does not introduce the warning itself.

## 5. Does combining UI, HOM, CM, R185/R187, and R188 create another result?

At present the manuscript's section 6.2 combines them as a comparison table. Update interference restricts a commuting-update alternative; finite action horizons can hide higher-order behavior; compensation can cancel a selected output contrast; an allowed interface may fail to identify occurrence multiplicity; source access can prevent otherwise ideal recovery. These are legitimate, differently scoped results. The table is clear about their separate models and avoids improperly joining premises from different instances.

However, the present combination does not prove an exhaustive classification of behavioral ambiguity. It does not show how measured evidence from one fixed experiment selects among those alternatives, establish a new sharp bound when the mechanisms coexist, or supply an operationally justified procedure that closes a previously open inference. Its common conclusion—specify access, permitted interventions, and timing—is already supported by the published baseline and standard identification/coding theory.

A synthesis could itself become a substantive contribution if it yielded such a new result under one justified contract. Merely putting the ingredients in one paper does not yet do so. Their correct separate hypotheses are essential for reuse, but not evidence that a new joint theorem exists.

The executable evidence supports the declared finite models. The final exact receipt contains 51 result records and 38,013 enumerated tables; the process reference contains 126 cases using 252 fresh spawned workers. These figures establish coverage of those checks, not scientific novelty or independent empirical UCT support. This reviewer originally inspected the 50-record snapshot and did not independently rerun it. The later mixed-role block-code correction and 51-record receipt are documented in `REVIEW_RESPONSE.md`; the unchanged original review is retained. No unresolved material defect was found in the final core formula, weights, complete-graph expression, or matched example during that audit.

## 6. What is worth retaining now

Retain the exact finite contract, its proof, the matched router construction, attaining feedback protocol, diagnostic controls, and versioned executable evidence. A future AI can use that unit to check whether a claimed recovery procedure quietly grants the encoder knowledge available only to the reader. That is a real reason to cite the technical record.

A narrower supplement should lead with that question, cite the published foundations, and explicitly label the result a classical-method specialization and project benchmark. It should avoid reusing the whole conceptual arc of I/III/TA25/RT/TH as if it were newly established. The broad manuscript can remain a frozen working synthesis, with this later decision linked to it.

This recommendation does not require abandoning the research line or withholding a useful artifact until a grand theory appears. It distinguishes preserving the specific knowledge already earned from asserting that the whole current package constitutes a substantial new standalone paper. Neither extra typesetting, another DOI, additional graph sizes, nor a rerun of the confirmed cases would resolve the remaining deficit.

## 7. One concrete next question worth investigating

**Can a non-oracular causal reader retain the same specified memory target at every required time while its physical representation changes, using only its actual history and a bounded amount of timely calibration?**

This question is connected to the original line: useful continuity through replacement or changing representation requires an installed causal process that can keep using the target. Published RT/TH already settles important endpoint and static route-blind cases and explicitly leaves history-dependent readers and interaction open. A meaningful next result would explain when past observations and an adaptive reader can replace repeated route metadata, and when fresh calibration is unavoidable before a deadline. It would provide a positive organizational construction, not only a warning that output equality is inconclusive.

The investigation should first compare R157, TE, TH, RT, R167–R172, and the published map attachments. General belief-state safety, factorization, invariant transport, elementary dynamic recoding, and the distinction between endpoint recovery and all-time availability are not new targets. A viable finite problem must add a genuine causal-access obstacle left unresolved by those predecessors.

The intended contract is one time-respecting source–representation–reader assembly, one fixed target and external law, a reader state updated only from observations and messages it actually receives, and an explicit deadline at each prefix. Success requires the same target to remain correctly usable at every required prefix. An informative result would give an exact condition and attaining finite protocol for a restricted but nontrivial family, together with a sharp failure condition under the same law and resource accounting. A solution that secretly gives the reader the changing route, lets a helper inspect unavailable target/context data, or returns a missing key only after an earlier scored deadline would not solve the problem.

This is an investigation target, not a claimed breakthrough or a promise that the answer is new. If the finite model collapses into an already published invariant or a straightforward classical coding substitution, record that negative originality result and move on. The UCT interpretation must remain conditional: preserving a selected operational target neither proves numerical personal identity nor introduces a threshold for basal experience. Actually continuing local processes retain their status under U1/P3.
