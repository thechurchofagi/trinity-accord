# Redundant same-event participation in a many-centred bodily/action organization

Version `A4X-RPP-v0.1.0`. Disabled research application; no physical or phenomenal installation.

## 1. The problem left by A4W

A4W showed that two concurrent `BearerCentredRoute` relations can coexist without an exclusive owner. Normal behavior alone cannot decide whether their shared downstream target uses one route or both: projection, AND and OR agree whenever the local decoded coordinates are equal. Its `Participates_tau` predicate was intentionally left open.

The immediate repair is not a stronger success score and not the demand that every redundant input be individually necessary at its actual value. Redundant mechanisms are precisely where simple but-for tests can fail. The needed distinction is among an actual route occurrence at a named consumer port, the consumer's actual read of that occurrence, and counterfactual evidence that the port belongs to the frozen mechanism.

## 2. Positive bounded account

The accompanying contract defines `SEPP_i` as one same-instance conjunction: binding, route-to-port lineage, actual read, frozen consumer boundary, independent factorial reachability, port-specific contingent sensitivity and alternate-path exclusion. `LINEAGE AND READ` grounds the event; the challenge terms supply evidence of relevance; neither direction is reversible.

This makes two improvements over a raw but-for requirement. First, AND at `(0,0)` and OR at `(1,1)` are not rejected merely because either individual input flip leaves the actual output unchanged. Second, a copied trace, unused wire, intervention-aware hidden controller or compiled one-port endpoint cannot pass by output contrast alone.

The criterion remains deliberately bounded. It is not offered as a universal analysis of actual causation. Halpern and Pearl's structural-model work already treats counterfactual dependence under contingencies; A4X credits that prior art and uses only the elementary idea that redundancy can require a declared contingency. The new work here is the exact A4W port-boundary application and the separation between actual read/lineage and functional challenge evidence.

## 3. Exact result and failure cases

All sixteen Boolean consumers were enumerated. Four match the normal diagonal. Only AND and OR are sensitive to both ports under some sibling setting. Neither survives a demand that both ports be but-for necessary at every normal row. Both off-diagonal challenges are required if either projection is a live rival.

Five failures delimit the inference:

1. **Diagonal-only access.** A one-port identity, a projection and a dual-port merger remain behaviorally equivalent.
2. **Intervention-aware controller.** A hidden mode bit can generate the expected off-diagonal outputs without reading the challenged route occurrence.
3. **Passive recorder.** Both values are logged, but only one enters the target-forming mechanism.
4. **Common fan-out or stale copy.** Equal values reach both ports without two current live route lineages.
5. **Boundary relocation.** An upstream two-port compiler followed by a one-port `m` supports participation at the compiler boundary, not at `m`.

These are GAP conditions, not reasons to infer nonparticipation in every untested system.
