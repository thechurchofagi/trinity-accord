## New primary-source checks

1. Whittlesea, B. W. A., & Williams, L. D. (2000). *The source of feelings of familiarity: the discrepancy-attribution hypothesis*. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 26(3), 547–565. DOI `10.1037/0278-7393.26.3.547`; PubMed PMID 10855417. Role: fluency is not by itself familiarity; attribution matters.
2. Sidarus, N., Vuorre, M., Metcalfe, J., & Haggard, P. (2017). *Investigating the prospective sense of agency: effects of processing fluency, stimulus ambiguity, and response conflict*. Frontiers in Psychology, 8, 545. DOI `10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00545`; PMID 28450839. Role: action-selection fluency can affect agency judgment but discrepant feedback has larger effects; nearby self-related judgments have multiple cues.
3. Weser, V., & Proffitt, D. R. (2019). *Tool Embodiment: The Tool's Output Must Match the User's Input*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 12, 537. DOI `10.3389/fnhum.2018.00537`; PMID 30687050. Role: proprioceptive-drift effects depend on tool/user matching; embodiment proxies require mechanism-specific interpretation.
4. Prosthesis and motor-learning sources already scoped and pinned in R199. Role: motivate longitudinal formation/use manipulation; not reinterpreted as `H` validation.

No systematic review, meta-analysis, new human/animal/AI experiment, complete biological signature, preregistration, proof-assistant verification, DOI publication or priority claim was performed. Web checks support scope and falsifiers; they do not close the semantic bridge.
