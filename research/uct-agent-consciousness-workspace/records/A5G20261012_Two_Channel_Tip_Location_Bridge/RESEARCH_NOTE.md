# A5G — Two-channel convergence does not identify distal experience

## 1. Result

A5F supplied an actual protocol-level anchor–grip–distal coordinate but left `H_tip`, the experienced distal contact location, open. A5G tests the most plausible small repair: combine crossed-tool temporal-order judgments (`E_TOJ`) with a second visuotactile-interaction/extinction channel (`E_VTI`). The positive empirical attraction is real. Crossed active tool use can reverse spatial constraints in tactile temporal-order judgments, while task-dependent visuotactile interactions can be strongest near actively used tool tips rather than handles or midshaft.

The exact result is negative but useful: convergence of two tasks is not equivalent to independent access to the target. A ten-model equivalence class fits the same two-channel reversal. Eight models let the latent target track the organizational coordinate with either orientation; two leave the target invariant while both channels read the shared organization. Thus even conditionally independent responses can be dependent on the same organizational cause and fail semantic or measurand independence.

## 2. Exact finite obstruction

Let the intervention be `I`, actual anchored organization be `O_I=I`, and the latent target be either `H_I=I xor b` or invariant `H_I=k`. Each decoded evidence channel reads either `O` or `H` with an unknown orientation. Requiring both channels to equal `I` leaves ten compatible models: source patterns `OO:4`, `OH:2`, `HO:2`, `HH:2`; target classes `H=O:4`, `H=not O:4`, invariant `H:2`.

If one channel is independently constrained to read `H`, invariant-target models disappear, but four models remain and both target orientations survive. Fixing that channel's target orientation in advance leaves two models, both with `H=O`. This is a sufficient identification repair only within the declared finite model; it is not evidence that any current experiment meets the repair.

## 3. Empirical scope and failure rule

Maravita and colleagues' crossed-tool temporal-order task and cross-modal tool literature ground distinct task operations. Holmes, Calvert and Spence varied tip-, shaft- and handle-use and measured visuotactile interaction at three distances: tip/shaft use strengthened interaction near tips, whereas handle use concentrated it near the hands. This makes a preregistered task-by-location dissociation possible.

However, the second channel remains a behavioural response and may track the same task-sensitive organization. It is not a no-report measure, a direct readout of `H_tip`, or the complete biological `K`. A preregistered failure is therefore essential: if TOJ reverses while VTI remains hand/midshaft-local under fixed anchor, grip, geometry and response mapping, reject the shared distal-coordinate generalization. If both converge at the tip, license cross-task support for `O_tip`, not `H_tip`.

## 4. Positive bridge contract

The minimum repair has two logical parts: at least one channel must be independently `H`-sensitive within fibers of fixed `O`, and its target pole must be fixed before the convergent result is seen. Same-binding, non-definitional use and a failure-capable protocol are additional UCT safeguards. The contract separates response independence, conditional independence and target-measure independence.

No reviewed primary source currently discharges this contract. Therefore `B_tip`, `B_pc`, ownership and agency remain open. The protocol may sharpen actual organizational localization without identifying a phenomenal location.
