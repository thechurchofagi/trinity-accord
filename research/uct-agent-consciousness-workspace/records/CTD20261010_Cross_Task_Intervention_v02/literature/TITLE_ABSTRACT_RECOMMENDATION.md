# Title and abstract recommendation

Draft for the root author, updated after the corrected cross-validation audit on 2026-10-10. All stated empirical directions have been checked against the repaired run. Final placement and length remain the author's decision.

## Preferred title

**From temporal response curves to sensory noise: an identification audit of body-ownership and simultaneity judgments**

This title identifies the inferential step under review without claiming to establish a sensory mechanism, to create a new general noise theory, or to validate a practical paired-report experiment.

## Shorter alternative

**Sensory–decision ambiguity in temporal judgments: a reproducible audit of an alpha-stimulation study**

## Abstract core

Observer-model noise parameters are often used to interpret changes in temporal judgments as changes in sensory uncertainty. We examine what that inference establishes using public body-ownership and simultaneity counts from an alpha-stimulation experiment with 30 participants. Independent evaluation reproduces the released fitted likelihoods and identifies a sign discrepancy in the published information-criterion calculation; correction retains the aggregate preference between the two source candidate models. A conditional binomial test rejects the response symmetry required by both centered models (Monte Carlo p = 0.00005). Equally applied task-location extensions improve held-out prediction; within a fixed count-splitting procedure assuming exchangeability, the effective-noise-varying model has a lower held-out NLL than its prior-varying counterpart. We then apply established Gaussian criterion-noise reasoning to the source observer: a constant sensory variance combined with condition-dependent criterion-center variability exactly reproduces its complete marginal response probabilities when the original threshold trajectories are retained. This construction quantifies an implementation ambiguity rather than evidence that criterion noise actually caused the intervention effect. Under calibrated marginals and lapses, a shared internal sensory draw, and independent decision noises, one additional paired-response probability identifies a common variance component. Correlated decision noises restore nonidentifiability, and weak separation can make the measurement impractical. The analysis separates a corrected relative model comparison from identification of its sensory interpretation and specifies what an additional, conditionally informative observable would need to measure.

## Wording decisions

- Keep the source arithmetic correction and the response-twin result separate. The former changes numbers; the latter limits implementation inference even if the corrected preferred response model fits well.
- Describe the symmetry result as a rejection of the centered independent-binomial family. It is not a rejection of all Bayesian causal-inference models.
- Say that location terms are inherited from the source-model lineage, citing Chancel et al. 2022 BCIbias. Their particular application and predictive audit are new here.
- Say “one additional paired-response probability,” not “one experiment” or “one trial.” The probability requires estimation, calibration, and exclusion assumptions.
- “Noise parameters” in this abstract are fitted effective parameters; “sensory variance” denotes a component in the declared generative construction. Do not equate either with a directly measured neural quantity.
- Do not use UCT in the title or abstract. The present data do not furnish a theory-specific numerical bridge to phenomenal endpoints.

## Contribution sentence for the introduction

“Building on established timing-observer and joint-response methods, our contribution is a reproducible audit of one intervention model: we reconcile its public code and numerical comparison, test a mandatory response constraint, instantiate exact sensory–decision response twins at its released parameters, and characterize the extra observable and assumptions needed to distinguish one such decomposition.”

## Information to avoid in the abstract

The exact raw-data source paths, broad theory graph, old proportional-width result, all exploratory model variants, and full finite-bound optimizer diagnostics belong elsewhere. The abstract should contain the substantive empirical check, exact interpretation boundary, and conditional measurement implication. A long list of caveats cannot substitute for those findings, but the three material restrictions—response family, actual joint-observation absence, and decision-noise independence—must remain visible.
