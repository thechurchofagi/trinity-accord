## 1. Decision and precise article question

**Proceed with one self-contained formal methods/application paper, after integrating the model review.** The defensible article concerns how to diagnose the relation between a physically addressed action source and two actual consumers when nominal task success and prediction are matched. It should not assemble R190–R204 into a collection of nominally new consciousness theorems.

A suitable question is: **Under a declared sensorimotor mechanism class and intervention interface, what can a prediction-discrepancy transcript identify about the sources actually supplied to execution and prediction, how many binary source probes are required, and what remains unidentified?** This gives a coherent progression from R204's success/alignment/use separation to SCU20261010's exact source-discrimination protocol and adversarial boundaries.

Suggested framing: *Identifying Shared Action Sources Under Matched Sensorimotor Performance: Exact Probe Limits and Interpretation Boundaries*. “Shared” means a specified relation between grounded source carriers and consumers. It does not mean ownership, felt agency, or familiar mineness. Other precise titles are possible.

The master guide's §0 permits a worthwhile original application, synthesis, repair, or negative result; it does not require a new mathematical field or positive phenomenal measurement. Its earlier HOLD decisions are revisable. Here the contribution is a reusable diagnostic construction combining a mechanism contract, exact intervention budgets, and countermodels that distinguish what the protocol establishes from what it cannot establish. This is enough for a modest formal methods paper. It is not evidence of journal acceptance, broad historical priority, or a breakthrough establishing H.

The mathematical coding arguments are elementary and substantially inherited. The article must say that directly. Its value is the exact application and the resulting design discipline, not the size of the enumerated model suite.

## 5. External primary-source subtraction

**Comparator sufficiency.** Zaadnoordijk, Besold, and Hunnius (2019), [“A match does not make a sense”](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2019/1/niz006/5488527), DOI [10.1093/nc/niz006](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz006), is a direct predecessor. The article examines why comparator matching or a self/other bin label does not by itself explain causal self-agency, including infant and artificial-agent implementations. The new paper must cite this in its introduction. Generic “matching is insufficient for agency” is not its contribution. The additional task is to specify a physically grounded, limited diagnostic target and solve its intervention problem. Read scope: publisher abstract, introduction, explanatory analysis and robotics discussion through the beginning of future directions; no claimed independent validation of the cited experiments.

**Binary separating systems.** Bollobás and Scott, [“On separating systems”](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/separating.pdf), directly state the classical `ceil(log2 n)` pair-separation bound in the introduction, crediting Rényi. All six pages were read. Thus SCU20261010's distinct binary source signatures and their logarithmic lower bound are an application of known separation mathematics. Avoid “new information-theoretic law.”

**Adaptive intervention lower bounds.** Shanmugam, Kocaoglu, Dimakis, and Vishwanath, [“Learning Causal Graphs with Small Interventions”](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.00041), already connect deterministic adaptive intervention lower bounds to separating systems. Theorem 2 and Appendix 6.3 were read, alongside the problem assumptions. Their target is full DAG orientation under a specified causal learner, whereas SCU20261010 asks whether two fixed consumers share a source under a discrepancy-only oracle. The application differs; the adversarial indistinguishable-label principle is inherited.

**OR mechanisms and superimposed codes.** D'yachkov, Rykov, Deppe, and Lebedev, [“Superimposed Codes and Threshold Group Testing”](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.7485), §2 Definition 1 and Proposition 1 state the cover-free/singleton-row condition. Taking one selected column against all remaining columns gives exactly the private activating row used in the unrestricted OR-source lower bound. Read scope: abstract, introduction, definitions through Proposition 1, and selected threshold proofs; not a complete audit of all rate bounds. The classic Kautz–Singleton 1964 original was located but its large scan/mirror could not be read reliably, so full reading must not be claimed.

**Sensorimotor background.** Wolpert, Ghahramani, and Jordan (1995), DOI [10.1126/science.7569931](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7569931), has an accessible [author-hosted abstract](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/zoubin/zoubin/kalman.abstract.html) describing state estimation after movements in the dark under external forces. Mazzoni and Krakauer (2006), DOI [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5317-05.2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5317-05.2006), and Tseng et al. (2007), DOI [10.1152/jn.00266.2007](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00266.2007), are predecessors for separating task strategies, sensory prediction errors, and corrective/adaptive behavior. Access during this review was abstract/search-excerpt level; publisher pages returned 403 and repeated PMC/PubMed retrievals became blocked or empty. They may support concise background claims at that scope, not assertions of full methods reanalysis.

**Active/passive contrast.** Kilteni, Engeler, and Ehrsson (2020), DOI [10.1016/j.isci.2020.100843](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2020.100843), compared active, passive and external touch generation. Its accessible primary abstract reports attenuation in the active condition. This empirical distinction is already known and does not establish a universal efference-copy criterion or the present source-equality theorem. Read scope was abstract and retrieved article excerpts, not full raw-data inspection.

**Prediction-error probing already has diagnostic applications.** Ganesh et al. (2018), DOI [10.1126/sciadv.aaq0183](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaq0183), [PubMed primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29750195/), proposed perturbing expected sensory input and decoding the resulting mismatch from EEG to infer movement intentions. Read scope: complete abstract and displayed experiment description. Therefore even the general idea of using sensory perturbations and prediction errors for diagnosis is not new. SCU20261010's target and exact finite intervention contract must carry the contribution.

This was a targeted primary-source search, not a systematic review or exhaustive priority search. No source supplies H semantics, validates C1, or verifies that a present artificial system realizes the protocol's physical premises.

The pure-route proof is sound in its declared class: source j has a k-bit codeword c_j; the observed discrepancy is `c_a XOR c_b`. Zero occurs exactly when the columns coincide. Distinct columns are therefore necessary and sufficient for all possible pairs. For an adaptive protocol, the all-zero branch must still assign distinct source histories or some unequal pair follows exactly the same branch as an equal pair. No probability or new experience axiom is needed.

For unrestricted nonempty OR subsets, compare all roots N with `N minus {i}`. They differ only when source i is the uniquely active source. Along the equal-subset all-zero branch, every such singleton probe is necessary. There are n of them, and they suffice by directly exposing subset membership. The n≥2 qualification matters because `N minus {i}` must be nonempty. With one root, the target is trivial and zero probes suffice.

Baseline equality must remain explicit. The code holds task success and prediction fixed for the yoked nominal source sequences, including tested multi-step target words. **Diagnostic probes intentionally break source synchrony.** The article must not say that all diagnostic episodes preserve success and prediction matching. Indeed, a discrepancy protocol diagnoses unequal sources by producing a mismatch under an informative probe.

The exact result assumes stable routes, adequate state reset or a correctly modeled intervention history, independently addressable roots, faithful binary writes, declared plant/predictor maps, pre-consequence prediction, and the specified readout. Clamp effects, unmodeled sources, dynamic gating, redundant mechanisms, noisy interventions, or hidden state change the inference problem. A model execution record confirms its constructed reads; it does not retrospectively prove which unobserved physical token an unrelated system used.

## 7. Errors found and repaired

The map reviewer additionally found that R194's general anchored parity-count statement omits consistency between multiple anchors. An acyclic edge constraint `x=y` with anchors `x=0,y=1` has zero solutions despite cycle consistency. The corrected general rule is: if all edge and anchor constraints are consistent, there are `2^c` solutions, with c the number of unanchored components; otherwise there are zero. The unanchored complement result and the executed single-anchor examples survive. This correction is separate from the source-consumer theorem and is recorded by the map reviewer.

R201's sufficient weak-bias/transport margin theorem remains valid. Its original strictness tuples are not coherent probability witnesses: `rho=1` forces `H=J` almost surely on supported strata, hence genuine residual `b=0`, contrary to its displayed `b=1/10`. Two rational full-joint witnesses with `rho=1/2` now replace them. The equality example disproves replacing strict `>` by `>=` globally; it does not establish sharpness for every positive budget combination. The map's separate error binding a strict-margin premise to equality/below-margin counterexamples also requires repair.

R202's exact latent-mixture inversion remains valid under conditional independence, or more generally aggregate within-class covariance residual `R=0`. “Or separately bounded departures” does not justify the original point formula. The existing R203 dependent twin has `R=3/50` and reverses the sign returned by the invalid zero-residual calculation. The correction explicitly inserts the common residual; a bound yields a feasible set, not automatic point identification. The two marginal intervals cannot be treated as independent because the same residual enters with opposite signs.

Complete algebra, two R201 full-joint witnesses, one reused R203/R202 counterexample, and exact executable checks are in `R201_R202_CORRECTIONS.md`, `R201_COHERENT_WITNESSES.json`, and `R202_RESIDUAL_CORRECTION.json`.
