## Primary empirical sources scoped

1. **Cordo, Gurfinkel, Bevan & Kerr (1995), “Proprioceptive consequences of tendon vibration during movement.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1995.74.4.1675. Read PubMed abstract/method/result scope. Random tendon vibration during a proprioceptively coordinated elbow–hand sequence changes endpoint timing in frequency- and timing-dependent directions. It is a strong anchor-perturbation component, not a tool-incorporation comparison.
2. **Iriki, Tanaka & Iwamura (1996), “Coding of modified body schema during tool use by macaque postcentral neurones.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-199610020-00010. Read PubMed abstract. Bimodal receptive fields expanded along a rake or reachable space during trained tool use. No independently isolated bearer-pose intervention or route-specific block/rescue is reported in the accessible scope.
3. **Cardinali et al. (2009), “Tool-use induces morphological updating of the body schema.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.05.009. Read the two-page primary report. Tool use changed later free-hand kinematics and tactile landmark localization; a weight control addressed fatigue. The critical evidence is pre/post and cross-task, not a same-event pose-anchor perturbation of the tool route.
4. **Cardinali et al. (2016), “Proprioception Is Necessary for Body Schema Plasticity: Evidence from a Deafferented Patient.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00272. Read full primary case report methods/results/discussion scope. The longitudinal deafferentation case supports a proprioceptive contribution to tool-induced body-schema plasticity, but the lesion is not a within-event independently assigned anchor intervention and the design does not bind a learned route block/rescue.
5. **Takahashi et al. (2017), “Optimal visual–haptic integration with articulated tools.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-017-4896-5. Read full primary article methods/result/discussion scope. Virtual articulated tools changed hand–world mappings and psychophysical cue integration. Tool geometry/mapping varies; bearer pose is not independently perturbed at fixed tool/target state, and incorporation status is not causally separated.
6. **Zografos Themelis, Medendorp & Miller (2026), “Gaze-centered spatial coding of touch on a hand-held tool.”** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00165.2025; preprint DOI https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.04.647167. Read publisher abstract/search-accessible primary text and preprint excerpts. Object location and gaze position vary independently and a model supports hand-to-space-to-gaze remapping. Gaze is not the A4N bearer-pose anchor; the experiment does not supply route-specific causal consumption or the three-way incorporation comparison.

## Retrieval limits and attribution

The 2026 publisher full text returned an access error; only the publisher abstract and accessible preprint excerpts were used. The Iriki paper was available only at abstract scope. `UNCLEAR` or inaccessible details are never upgraded to `YES`. Reviews were used only to locate primary papers, not as empirical premises. Tool incorporation, coordinate transformations, tendon-vibration methods and multisensory integration are established literatures; A4O claims no mechanism or priority.
