## Primary empirical feasibility precedents

- Diedrichsen, J. (2007), “Optimal Task-Dependent Changes of Bimanual Feedback Control and Adaptation,” *Current Biology* 17:1675–1679, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2007.08.051`, PMID 17900901. Participants used two independent cursors/targets or one cursor at the spatial average of both hands.
- Dimitriou, M., Franklin, D. W., and Wolpert, D. M. (2009), “Shared Bimanual Tasks Elicit Bimanual Reflexes During Movement,” *Journal of Neurophysiology* 102:3142–3155, DOI `10.1152/jn.91335.2008`, PMID 19793874. The study compared independent and shared cursor feedback and applied unilateral perturbations.
- Reichenbach, A., Costello, A., Zatka-Haas, P., and Diedrichsen, J. (2013), “Mechanisms of Responsibility Assignment during Redundant Reaching Movements,” *Journal of Neurophysiology* 109:2021–2028, DOI `10.1152/jn.01052.2012`, PMID 23365179. A shared cursor, rapid cursor/target perturbations and hand-specific corrections demonstrate feasibility of route-resolved compensation measures.

These studies are used only to support apparatus-level physical credibility. They do not implement A5C's exact two-target matrix, validate `H_pc`, establish a UCT bridge or prove actual installation in this archive. No worldwide-priority claim is made. The label-symmetry algebra and linear mappings are standard.
