Complementing `H` in both domains, replacing each prevalence by its complement and swapping every `(p0,p1)` leaves both tables exactly unchanged. With no transport and no anchor, the two domains may flip independently: four orientations. Exact class-conditional transport imposes `S_c=S_t`, leaving two. An independently signed calibration anchor adds `S_c=0`, leaving one. By contrast, a signed calibration channel plus only marginal target similarity leaves the target flip free and therefore two orientations.

The result is a scoped latent-label symmetry and an exact countermodel, not new probability theory. It shows what a sign-anchor claim must add; it does not prove that no future physical or first-person method can add it.

## 3. Physically credible cross-orbit manipulation

Two two-axis handles control two named scalar targets. In the joint mapping, each target is the normalized sum of the corresponding axes of both handles; in the distributed mapping, the left route controls target 0 and the right route controls target 1. The resulting edge sets have four and two edges and occupy different route-swap orbits. Brief route-specific perturbations can measure whether the other route supplies a targetwise correction. Existing shared-versus-independent bimanual cursor and perturbation experiments establish feasibility of the apparatus class.

This protocol is prospective. No participant, apparatus, raw route trace or report has been collected. Mapping code is not by itself actual-use evidence, and a correct cursor endpoint is not a complete `Pi/W` witness. The protocol therefore includes frozen mappings, raw-port time binding, perturbation lineage, alternate-path inventory, route cut/rescue and replay refuters.

## 4. Why the tempting anchors fail

- **Phenomenally worded report:** fixes the question's intended content; it remains fallible and may be systematically inverted or proxy-driven.
- **Rapid continuation choice:** can be generated by learned policy, reward or task fluency with unchanged or opposite `H_pc`.
- **Corrective force or latency:** shows motor response organization, not which phenomenal pole is present.
- **Obvious forced interruption:** validates physical inability to continue; identifying that contrast with felt practical continuity is the bridge under dispute.
- **Channel agreement or latent class:** recovers at most an unoriented partition unless one channel has an independent sign warrant.
- **Compensation success:** is typed as `C`; using it to label `H_pc` would erase A5B's central distinction.

Failures of coding invariance, route fidelity or copy/switch predictions can refute a proposed bridge. Their success does not establish phenomenal identity. This retains A3Z's asymmetric evidence rule.
