## 3. Exact classification

Let `r=f(x,a)` and `y=g(r,a)` for bits `x,a,r,y`. Requiring `y=x` for all four `(x,a)` inputs forces each anchor-indexed encoder section to be a one-bit bijection. Therefore

`f_a(x)=x XOR c_a`, `g_a(r)=r XOR c_a`

for one bit `c_a`. The four choices of `(c_0,c_1)` are the complete solution set. When the two constants agree, the route ignores the anchor. When they differ, fixed-target anchor change flips the route code and the consumer's joint anchor/route transformation compensates it. Hence exactly two perfect pairs are bearer-centred.

The enumeration confirms: 256 total pairs, 4 perfect-output pairs, 2 centred pairs, 2 anchor-insensitive pairs. It also confirms that all four perfect pairs use `r`. An output or route-use test alone cannot separate them.
