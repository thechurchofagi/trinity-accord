## External primary sources

1. Ghafari Goushe, R. et al. “Perception of effort decreases with motor sequence learning.” *Scientific Reports* 15, 44403 (published 19 December 2025). DOI: `10.1038/s41598-025-20733-z`. Scope read: publisher abstract, methods/result summary and discussion/limitation text. Used only for the observed relation between sequence-specific learning and perceived effort, and for the explicit-awareness caveat.
2. Song, S., Howard, J. H. Jr. & Howard, D. V. “Perceptual sequence learning in a serial reaction time task.” *Experimental Brain Research* 189, 145–158 (2008). DOI: `10.1007/s00221-008-1411-z`; PMID 18478209; PMCID PMC2672106. Scope read: PubMed abstract and open full-text metadata. Used for probabilistic sequence learning and explicit-search/observation conditions, not for consciousness identity.
3. Ghilardi, M. F. et al. “Learning of a sequential motor skill comprises explicit and implicit components that consolidate differently.” *Journal of Neurophysiology* 101, 2218–2229 (2009). DOI: `10.1152/jn.01138.2007`; PMID 19073794; PMCID PMC2681421. Scope read: PubMed abstract. Used for a task-relative explicit/implicit component distinction.
4. Perruchet, P. & Amorim, M.-A. “Conscious knowledge and changes in performance in sequence learning: evidence against dissociation.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 18, 785–800 (1992). DOI: `10.1037/0278-7393.18.4.785`; PMID 1385616. Scope read: PubMed abstract. Used as a counterweight to strong performance/awareness dissociation.
5. Shanks, D. R. & Johnstone, T. “Evaluating the relationship between explicit and implicit knowledge in a sequential reaction time task.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 25, 1435–1451 (1999). DOI: `10.1037/0278-7393.25.6.1435`; PMID 10605830. Scope read: PubMed abstract. Used for the same dissenting boundary.
6. Topolinski, S. “The sensorimotor contributions to implicit memory, familiarity, and recollection.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 141, 260–281 (2012). DOI: `10.1037/a0025658`; PMID 22004167. Scope read: PubMed abstract. Used only to show that sensorimotor interference, familiarity and recollection need not behave identically in those stimulus-memory tasks.

## Explicit non-claims

- None of these sources validates the A4I wording or equates a report with `H_cont`.
- “Implicit” is not used as evidence of absent explicit knowledge.
- Memory familiarity for words or tunes is not treated as bodily continuation familiarity.
- Reduced effort is not treated as the sign or definition of an experience-internal familiar way.
- No source establishes actual UCT C1 satisfaction in a participant or the current assistant.
