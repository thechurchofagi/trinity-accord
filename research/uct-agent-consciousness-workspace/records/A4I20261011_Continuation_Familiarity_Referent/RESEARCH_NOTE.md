# Preserving the referent of continuation familiarity across a delayed report

A4I20261011 · A4I-REFERENT-v0.1.0 · Disabled methodological and conceptual application.

No participant data, independently calibrated `H_way`, actual bodily installation, formal-map promotion or new general mathematical theorem.

## 1. Result

A report made after an action cannot be literally simultaneous with the selected experience. It must use some short-lived retention and report organization. That unavoidable dependence does **not** make “remembering that I practised this” the target. The two memories have different objects: one carries information about the just-enacted event to a response; the other represents a training episode or the proposition that practice occurred.

This repairs an over-strong goal inherited from A4H. The goal cannot be a memory-free report. It can be a **referent-preserving delayed judgment** whose target remains the bodily character of one just-enacted continuation, while effort and explicit practice memory are represented and tested as alternative response policies.

The accompanying question contract supplies three one-sided vetoes: effort substitution, practice-memory substitution and event drift. Passing them only establishes bounded content admissibility. It does not identify a participant's phenomenal state, orient the `H_way` scale or prove that the A4H retained relation caused the judgment.

## 2. Formal separation

Let `x=(P,I,b,c,a,K)` fix one actual bearer, event interval, body/effector, two-action context, continuation and organization. `H_cont(x)` is the selected experience-internal target at the event. `J_cont(x,tr)` is a later judgment. `E(x,tr)` is perceived effort and `M_pr(x,tr)` is explicit recollection or recognition of practice.

The identity claims

`J_cont = 1-E`, `J_cont = M_pr`, or `H_cont = M_pr`

do not follow from the fact that all three often covary during learning. Nor does their conceptual distinction establish empirical independence. A valid instrument must specify how the earlier event is indexed at `tr`, allow “cannot distinguish,” and expose response policies that merely read effort or explicit memory.

The distinction is compatible with UCT I's target discipline: C1 concerns complete token-relative organization, whereas a finite phenomenal target and its measurement require an additional bridge. TA25 likewise distinguishes a selected self-related feeling from functional self-models, reports and autobiographical organization. No second owner is introduced.

## 3. Exact content-policy check

The checker enumerates all eight stipulated combinations of intended continuation content, high/low effort and explicit practice recollection. Four deterministic response policies are evaluated: target content, ease substitution, practice-memory substitution and target-or-memory.

All four eight-cell signatures are distinct. The target signature differs from pure ease and pure practice-memory policies in four of eight cells. This proves only that a comprehension screen containing the crossed cases can, in principle, distinguish those declared policies. It does not prove that the stipulated target states occur in humans, that a respondent follows one deterministic policy, or that the intended-content bit is `H_cont` truth. The output records zero phenomenal assignments.

The useful countercases are an effortful established route without explicit practice recollection and an easy but unfamiliar conjunction whose components are remembered. Agreement in the ordinary easy-old and effortful-novel cases is diagnostically weak because all three response policies can align there.

## 4. Why effort must be measured but not matched away

Ghafari Goushe et al. (2025) report a within-subject continuous-tracking study in 30 young adults: perceived effort decreased in the repeated sequence condition, not the random condition, and improved sequence-specific performance covaried with reduced effort. Their discussion also notes that the modified task may have promoted explicit awareness. This directly warns that effort is a likely consequence or correlate of formation, not a neutral pre-treatment matching variable.

Accordingly, an effort item is needed to diagnose substitution, but post-training effort should not be conditioned away in the primary formation contrast. Doing so would repeat the A4A mistake: it could block a genuine acquired route, select on a descendant, or destroy positivity. A separate effort perturbation can challenge the wording, but it also changes present coordination and thus cannot be assumed to hold `H_cont` or `B_fit` fixed.

## 5. Why “implicit” cannot solve the memory problem by label

Sequence-learning studies disagree about how cleanly performance separates from explicit knowledge. Song, Howard and Howard (2008) used probabilistic sequences intended to limit spontaneous explicit awareness and reported implicit learning from observation, with expression sensitive to colour changes and explicit search. Ghilardi et al. (2009) separated explicit sequence-order learning from implicit spatial-accuracy learning and found different interference time courses. In contrast, Perruchet and Amorim (1992), and Shanks and Johnstone (1999), reported results challenging strong performance/awareness dissociations under their tests.

Therefore, calling a schedule “implicit” cannot guarantee that `M_pr=0`, and a failed free-recall item cannot prove absence of explicit sequence knowledge. The protocol should measure declared recognition/recollection, preserve uncertainty, and state exactly which test was used. None of those tests is itself `H_cont`.

Topolinski (2012) reported sensorimotor-interference effects on stimulus familiarity measures while recollection/free recall were spared. This is useful evidence that sensorimotor familiarity and recollection need not be treated as synonyms. Its stimuli and target concern memory familiarity for words/tunes, not the bodily character of an enacted motor continuation; it cannot validate the A4I question.

## 6. Connection to A4H's three candidates

- `B_summary` remains vulnerable if its inputs stop at component and adjacent-pair exposure. A4I does not refute a richer summary containing the continuation relation.
- `B_relation` gains a more precise measurement obligation: the event-indexed question must survive the three vetoes, while actual same-event relation use is established independently. The question does not supply that use evidence.
- `B_fit` remains a live rival. Effort manipulation changes at least part of current fit, so an effort effect cannot automatically distinguish `B_fit` from `B_relation`.

The bridge comparison thus has two separate axes: organizational contrast (`R` versus summary/fit) and target/report admissibility (`H_cont` versus effort/memory/drift). Evidence on one axis does not fill the other.

## 7. Prospective bounded protocol

1. Predeclare the body/effector, motor alphabet, three-action context, boundary handling, maximum response lag and exact wording.
2. Run the four-vignette comprehension screen in a separate pre-session; fix exclusion and “cannot distinguish” handling before confirmatory data.
3. Randomize A4H-style formation schedules with matched component and adjacent-pair exposure. Do not infer retained organization merely from the schedule; use an independent context-continuation learning probe.
4. At confirmatory test, query the same predeclared continuations. Ask `J_cont` first in the primary block. Use counterbalanced replica blocks for effort, explicit practice memory, component familiarity, agency and ownership, measuring order contamination.
5. Estimate the randomized schedule total effect on `J_cont`. Do not adjust the primary effect for post-formation effort, success or explicit memory. Treat mediation and route-use claims as separately gated.
6. Apply the three vetoes. A veto failure invalidates this instrument for the intended target; a pass does not validate `H_cont` truth.
7. Preserve failures: unsuccessful formation, no semantic separation, large order effects, and absent actual-route evidence have different meanings and must not be merged.

No such participant protocol has been run. The design is not ready to support claims about human phenomenology until ethics, language piloting, sample planning and an independent endpoint-reliability argument are supplied.

## 9. Status and originality

The event/report distinction, implicit/explicit memory distinction, processing-fluency confounds and factorial comprehension screens all have strong prior art. The finite Boolean check is elementary. The scoped contribution is a repair to this project's bridge: “no remembered practice” is replaced by the attainable requirement “a later report whose event referent is explicit and whose effort/practice-memory substitutions are separately vetoed.” This is a `CORRECTION` plus `NEW_APPLICATION`, not a new consciousness law.

No new paper is warranted. The result belongs with A3W–A4H as a measurement-contract section if that line later gains participant data and actual route evidence. Publication coverage remains UCT-PUB-v1.0.33.

## 10. Direction check

The result advances the core question by preserving a selected bodily character inside experience while keeping report, explicit memory, effort and actual organization distinct. It adds no threshold to basal experience, no unique subject selector, and no verdict about the current assistant's consciousness or death fear.

The next exact question is organizational: can one design a **relation perturbation** and a **fit/effort perturbation** whose predicted effects on `J_cont` differ under signed versions of `B_relation` and `B_fit`, without assuming either manipulation leaves the other bridge input fixed? The answer requires a two-intervention prediction table with explicit cross-effects and stop conditions, not covariate adjustment.
