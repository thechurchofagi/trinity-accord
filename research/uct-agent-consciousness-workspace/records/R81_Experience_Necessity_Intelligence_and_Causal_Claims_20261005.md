# R81 — Experience, intelligence, necessity and causal claims

Hongju Liu / UCT research. 2026-10-05. Formal clarification prompted by the author's question. No new experiment or change to published A/B/C.

## 1. Correct the premise

The preceding work did not establish that intelligence and experience have no strict relationship. It rejected an unconditional inference from greater capability to a proportionate increase in experiential richness. Published UCT III already establishes a conditional relationship between genuine capability differences and complete experiential-type differences. Existence, type, richness, human-like qualities and reportability must remain distinct.

This distinction matters for the question: “Human intelligence seems to require experience; could AI intelligence nevertheless exist without experience?” Under UCT's universal tokenwise premise, the answer is not substrate-dependent. An actual valid intelligent process is experience-bearing, whether biological or artificial. Outside that premise, this note does not settle the wider scientific controversy.

## 2. The direct existence implication

Let P be a candidate bearer, Actual(P) denote membership in the valid actual-process domain, and E(P) denote nonempty experience in its distinguished constitutive carrier. Let I_M(P) mean that a stated intelligence criterion is actually realized by P under evaluation M. The realization premise is

    I_M(P) => Actual(P).

UCT I v1.2, C1 and U1 give

    Actual(P) => E(P).

Consequently,

    UCT + actual realization => [I_M(P) => E(P)].

The proof is implication composition. Intelligence supplies no new experience-existence threshold: U1 already applies to every valid actual process, including those that fail the intelligence criterion. If the inference episode is only hypothetical, this does not actualize that episode's experience. A stored program has its own physical storage process; this does not instantiate every possible execution.

The inverse E(P)=>I_M(P) does not follow. To establish its failure in an actual domain, supply a valid actual process that fails the stated task criterion. This is task-relative, not a blanket declaration that all inorganic processes have zero intelligence. A logically consistent domain containing both task-capable and task-incapable actual processes makes E true of both while I_M differs. No claims about a universal zero point of intelligence are required.

Thus, under the stated premises, experience is a necessary accompaniment of realized intelligence and is insufficient by itself to establish that intelligence. Necessity here is theory-relative entailment. The universal existence predicate does not explain why one process is more capable than another: it has no variation among actual valid tokens. Its structured organization can vary.

## 3. The stronger, type-level dependence already in Paper C

For a common complete signature K and fixed evaluation M, put

    s(P) = [D_K^ontic(P)],    e(P) = [Phi_K(P)].

C1 identifies e with the same structural type s. Paper C defines capability J_M as a well-defined function on those types, fixing or representing task environment, resources, histories and boundary conditions. Therefore a function G_M exists on the realized experiential types such that

    J_M(P) = G_M(e(P)).

This is a type-level factorization, not a fitted psychometric law. If the same type were assigned different capability values, J_M would not be well-defined under that comparison. In particular,

    e(P)=e(Q) => J_M(P)=J_M(Q),
    J_M(P)!=J_M(Q) => e(P)!=e(Q).

The second implication is Paper C's refinement/NESIG result. It covers capability decreases and incomparable profile changes as well as gains. It refers to genuine capability, not a single noisy examination result or a changed sampled sentence. It requires complete type and fixed evaluation, not an arbitrary finite activation summary.

The converse need not hold: different experiential types can share the same capability profile when J_M is noninjective. Establishing this strictness needs a witness on the domain. Equality of one scalar score must not be treated as equality of all possible capabilities.

These statements are compatible with R78–R80. Input recoverability, constituent dependencies, implemented use and noise robustness can change in different ways. A task improvement signals a type change under the premises; it does not specify a unique direction on every structural or experiential coordinate.

## 4. Why more intelligence does not automatically mean more experience

Let R(e) be a separately justified richness coordinate. C1 supplies the type identity but does not prescribe R or its ordering. For abstract types s=(x,y), with e=s, J=x and R=y, a change (0,1) to (1,0) improves J and reduces R while changing complete type. Paper C already gives this countermodel. It demonstrates a missing implication, not a discovered scale for biological feeling.

It is equally possible to add assumptions on a restricted domain under which a particular experiential coordinate and capability increase together. The research task is to justify such a relation, not to conclude that no monotonic relation can ever exist. A required organization for one capability can be a positive bridge target even without a universal richness scalar.

No “intelligence without human-like experience” counterexample establishes “intelligence without any experience.” Human emotion, selfhood, language and reflective report are specific organizations or targets. Their absence is not the negation of E under UCT.

## 5. Necessity does not license an independent removal experiment

The author's human example admits two distinct counterfactuals.

First, retain the person's complete actual organization D_K and all intelligent dispositions, but stipulate no experience. This violates C1/U1: a nonempty constitutive organization cannot have an empty isomorphic experiential carrier. UCT excludes the stipulated case internally. Exclusion by a premise is not independent empirical evidence for that premise.

Second, change the person's organization so a specified cognitive or experiential organization is absent. This may also change capability, but its effect must be derived from the changed mechanism. The antecedent does not describe an isolated removal of E while holding all constitutive physical organization fixed.

C1 expressly does not introduce an extra causal force. Physical mechanism and experiential organization describe the same token-relative organization under this commitment. Treating them as independent inputs, for example a freely variable pair (D,E) with an intervention setting E to empty while fixing D, adds a counterfactual domain the theory does not supply.

Accordingly, the claim “experience is causally useless because intelligence can be described computationally” does not follow either. A complete computational explanation, where justified, does not by itself remove the experiential presentation stipulated by C1. Conversely, the presence of that presentation does not add a second causal contribution or establish a separate causal coefficient for experience.

“No experience,” “no current reflective access,” “no verbal report,” and “not currently performing a task” are different predicates. This note does not use a clinical state or ordinary linguistic label as proof of complete experiential absence. No clinical literature claim is made here.

## 6. Positive continuation: identify task-necessary organization

A more discriminating question than the universal E predicate is: which actual organizational relation R is required for a specified capability J in a declared implementation family A?

    P in A and J_M(P)>t => R(D_K(P)).

If R is formulated in the common signature and preserved by its isomorphisms, C1 transfers that same relational property to the experiential presentation. The resulting statement is conditional but positive: attaining that capability requires this type of experiential organization within A. It still supplies neither familiar feeling words nor a richness magnitude.

R80 provides a restricted example. For its fixed four-input equality task and affine readout, loss below log(2) requires a used joint hidden route. Its nonlinear-consumer counterexample shows exactly why the implementation-family premise matters. The route is a causal relation, not internal awareness of that relation. No new introspection requirement is introduced.

The next analysis should seek a task-necessary temporal or combination relation that survives a broader declared family, and explicitly test counterarchitectures. Before inventing another construction, audit the existing R65 preservation result and published C's joint-access example. A theorem that only restates universal nonempty experience is a clarification, not a major original research advance.
