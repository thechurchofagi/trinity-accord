# Cross-effects do not force a pure-manipulation test of continuation familiarity

A4J20261011 · A4J-CROSS-PERTURBATION-v0.1.0 · Disabled conditional method/application result.

No participant data, signed `H_cont` calibration, actual bodily route installation, completed-map promotion or new general causal theorem.

## 1. Result

A4I left a precise design problem: a relation-directed perturbation can also change present fit, and a fit/effort perturbation can also change retained-relation use. Treating either manipulation as selective would hide the main uncertainty. Treating off-target effects as covariates would repeat A4A's post-formation adjustment error.

The repair is a set-valued prediction table over randomized total effects. Under strict positively oriented pure candidates, an independently verified decrease in `R` on the relation arm forces only the first sign under `B_relation+`; the fit-arm sign remains free because that intervention may also change `R`. Conversely, an independently verified decrease in `F` on the fit arm forces only the second sign under `B_fit+`; the relation-arm sign remains free.

This yields a partially identifying table without an exclusion restriction. `(-,-)` is shared and therefore inconclusive. `(-,0/+)` is relation-only; `(0/+,-)` is fit-only; four remaining signatures reject both scoped pure candidates. If neither targeted first-stage sign is independently warranted, both candidate prediction sets expand to all nine signatures and the comparison identifies nothing.

The positive result is thus not “cross-effects do not matter.” It is: cross-effects can remain unrestricted while independently signed target first stages impose different one-coordinate constraints. The cost is narrow scope. Mixed, interactive, threshold and nonmonotone bridges remain outside the two pure candidates.

## 2. Typed setup

Fix one bearer population, just-enacted continuation event, body/effector, motor grain, response lag, response coding and baseline. Let `R` denote actual current use of the retained context-to-continuation relation from A4H, `F` a predeclared present coordination/fit coordinate, and `J_cont` the later event-indexed judgment repaired in A4I.

For randomized intervention `z`, write `tau_X(z)=E[X|do(z)]-E[X|do(base)]`. A relation disruption `Z_R` must be shown independently to have `sign tau_R(Z_R)=-1`; a fit disruption `Z_F` must be shown independently to have `sign tau_F(Z_F)=-1`. These are first-stage/admission conditions, not outcome-conditioned covariates. Their failures invalidate the corresponding arm rather than count against a bridge.

`B_relation+` and `B_fit+` are protocol-level signed candidates. Each claims that, in the declared family, the sign of the total `J_cont` effect follows the sign of its named coordinate. C1 does not supply either sign. A4I's wording does not supply signed reliability. The candidates are therefore hypothesis contracts, not established UCT consequences.

## 3. Exact table and proof

Let `Omega={-,0,+}`. With the two strict target directions fixed and cross-effects free,

`P_relation={-} x Omega`, while `P_fit=Omega x {-}`.

Their intersection is the singleton `{(-,-)}`. Set membership gives the complete nine-signature classification. The checker enumerates the nine latent cross-effect worlds, all nine possible observed signatures, and a weak-sensitivity variant. In the weak variant, targeted effects may be `-` or `0`; four signatures are shared, so nulls carry little separating force.

If all four input sign sets are `Omega`, then both products equal `Omega^2`. This is the negative theorem: named intentions to perturb relation or fit do not create identification without an independently warranted change in the named input.

The proof is elementary Cartesian-product logic. The application is useful because it shows exactly which outcome patterns survive “fat-hand” interventions and which extra premises are doing the work.

## 4. Why this does not adjust away the problem

The primary observations are the two randomized total effects on `J_cont`. The protocol never conditions on post-formation effort, success, memory, `R` or `F` to form a purified residual. First-stage evidence is used to admit or reject the predeclared intervention contract. It is not inserted into an outcome regression after seeing the result.

This distinction matters. If `Z_R` also worsens fit, a negative `J_cont` effect can be explained by either pure candidate and the result is correctly inconclusive. If `Z_F` raises or leaves `J_cont` unchanged despite a verified fit decrease, the pure fit candidate fails while the relation candidate may survive if `Z_R` has the required negative effect. The off-target effect is informative variation, not an automatic nuisance to delete.

## 5. Counterexamples and stopping rules

1. **Both-down counterexample.** Both perturbations reduce both inputs. Both candidates predict `(-,-)`; two interventions do not guarantee discrimination.
2. **Unverified first stage.** A manipulation is named relation-directed but actual `R` is not measured or physically warranted. Replacing that absence with the label `Z_R` makes every sign possible.
3. **Mixed bridge.** `J_cont` depends on both `R` and `F`. A signature rejected by both pure candidates can still arise without implying no target experience.
4. **Referent drift.** Load changes the participant from judging the last continuation to judging whole-task difficulty. The outcome is no longer the A4I target.
5. **Response remapping.** The intervention changes use of the scale or abstention propensity rather than the selected event character. Total-effect signs are then not comparable without an independently frozen measurement model.

Accordingly, failed first stages or changed target/report mappings are `INVALID_PROTOCOL`; overlap is `INCONCLUSIVE_BETWEEN_PURE_CANDIDATES`; an outside signature is `REJECT_SCOPED_PURE_CANDIDATES`. None is a verdict about basal experience.
