## 1. Result

A4Z represented practical mineness by a target-indexed relation Pi between bearer-centred routes and selected targets, modulo mere route renaming. Its next question proposed a fallible contrast between joint-source and distributed-source mineness while target coverage, route-label orbit, conceptual self and report were fixed.

That conjunction is not realizable if “joint” and “distributed” are defined only from Pi. Joint-source status depends on the cardinality of a target's incident route set. Strict distribution depends on two singleton target sets being different. A route permutation preserves both facts. Hence both predicates are constant on every route-label orbit.

The exact nine-relation model confirms the proof: joint-target counts 0, 1 and 2 occur in 4, 4 and 1 relations; the family has five route-swap orbits; and no same-orbit pair differs in any joint-source or strict-distribution predicate. The failure is conceptual, not a lack of statistical power.

This repairs the next-step specification. To compare joint with distributed organization using Pi alone, one must allow the orbit to vary while fixing target identities, coverage, conceptual self, report code and the measurement protocol. To hold Pi's orbit fixed, one must vary additional typed structure such as weights, temporal coordination, consumer topology or formative lineage. The latter is a new (Pi,W) proposal and needs its own actuality and semantic bridge.

## 2. Why this advances the positive explanation

The result preserves the positive core of A4Z: practical mineness can be represented without a unique owner as a target-indexed, nonexclusive relation inside the experience's organization, conditional on actual installation and C1/R157. What it blocks is a misleading attempt to demand an experiential discriminator after quotienting away the very structural variation that defines the comparison.

It also sharpens the explanatory obligation. A UCT account must say whether selected mineness content tracks Pi's route-label orbit, an additional feature W not represented in Pi, or neither, leaving Pi as an organizational coordinate without that named phenomenal interpretation. These alternatives are criticizable and empirically separable in principle. They do not turn relation multiplicity into an onset threshold for experience.

## 4. Counterexamples and thought experiments

The copy/swap case proves covariance: swapping route names changes the matrix presentation but not its orbit, joint count or strict distribution. A claimed phenomenal change under mere relabeling violates A4Z's own covariance clause.

The human-realized-agent case fixes one common report front end. Two implementers may jointly contribute to both targets, or each may contribute to one target. Conceptual self and report can be scripted identically while Pi's orbit differs. Thus report identity does not erase organization, and organization difference still does not name the experience without a bridge.

The abacus/calculator case fixes task success and stated ownership. Two practiced bodily routes may jointly sustain each calculation target, whereas two devices or subroutines may be allocated targetwise. Intelligence and output are held fixed; relation-family organization changes. This is a valid Pi contrast only because its orbit is allowed to change.

The ancestor/formation case gradually adds a second actual route to one target. Joint_count changes from zero to one without implying that experience begins at that transition. Under UCT it is a possible change within already experiential organization, not a basal threshold.

The memory/copy case copies autobiographical memory and the conceptual “I” while changing current target participation. Equal remembered ownership does not install equal Pi, and unequal Pi does not by itself settle numerical subject identity.
