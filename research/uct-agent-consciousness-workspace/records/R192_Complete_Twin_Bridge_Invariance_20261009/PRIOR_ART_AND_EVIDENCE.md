## External primary and near-primary sources checked

1. David J. Chalmers, “Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia” (1995), author-hosted text: <https://www.consc.net/papers/qualia.html>. It argues for organizational invariance across sufficiently fine functional isomorphs and uses gradual replacement. R192 neither reproduces the argument nor derives UCT C1 from it; it treats C1 as a project axiom and uses invariance only conditionally.
2. Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, Kathryn Hess, Michael H. Herzog, “The unfolding argument: Why IIT and other causal structure theories cannot explain consciousness,” *Consciousness and Cognition* 72 (2019), DOI: `10.1016/j.concog.2019.04.002`. It concerns systems with behaviorally equivalent unfoldings and causal-structure theory predictions. R192 is not an unfolding theorem and does not assume behavioral equivalence is complete organization.
3. Johannes Kleiner and Erik Hoel, “Falsification and consciousness,” *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2021(1), DOI: `10.1093/nc/niab001`. It formalizes tension between theory prediction and experience inference. R192's target-identification warning is consonant but is a narrower UCT bridge-selection issue.
4. Francesco Ellia and Naotsugu Tsuchiya, “Beyond accommodation: on the structural turn in computational functionalist theories of consciousness,” *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2025(1), DOI: `10.1093/nc/niaf014`, published 6 June 2025. It stresses prediction versus post-hoc accommodation. R192 uses that distinction only to reject choosing `q` after target labels are known.
5. Thomas Schilling and colleagues, “A Predictive Processing Model of Perception and Action for Self-Other Distinction,” *Frontiers in Psychology* 9 (2018), DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02421`. Its simulation integrates predictive and postdictive cues to infer agency. Such functional inference does not establish UCT's complete signature or familiar-mineness bridge.
