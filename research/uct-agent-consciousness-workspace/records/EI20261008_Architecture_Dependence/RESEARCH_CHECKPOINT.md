## 1. The question and the inherited foundation

The author asks why loss of human experience seems to impair intelligence whereas a calculator can calculate precisely without evidently rich human-like experience. First distinguish basal experience, selected experiential content, cognitive/report access and task competence. Under UCT I v1.2 C1/U1, an admitted actual process with its complete organization held fixed but its entire experience independently removed is not an admitted UCT counterfactual. This is a consequence of the theory's premise, not an independent refutation of rival theories. Sensory input loss, report loss and basal experiential absence cannot be substituted for one another.

UCT III v1.0 §5 already proves the conditional implication from capability-profile change to complete experiential-type change, while equal competence does not determine equal experience and no scalar richness ordering follows. Its §7 already treats stored versus usable information and the value of information. TA25 separates actual organization, finite views, self-models, named feelings and report. These are inherited, not new findings.

The narrower question is: **does a task's dependence on one process carry over to another equally capable architecture?** A biological/silicon dichotomy is not assumed. Continuing local processes remain covered by U1/P3 even when included in a larger organization; task dispensability is not absence of experience.

## 3. Three arithmetic organizations with identical intact answers

At t0 a cue c in {0,1} is copied into two existing registers H=Q=c. At t1 a clamp can set H, Q, both or neither to zero. No later access to the cue source is available. At t2 the fixed arithmetic mechanism receives operands x,y in {0,1,2,3} and a selector bit. At t3 an independent context-description port outputs R=H. All 32 triples (x,y,c) are equiprobable.

The task is F(x,y,c)=x+y for c=0, and xy for c=1. Every architecture contains both registers, arithmetic units and report port; only the selector's routing differs:

    shared:    selected=H
    bypass:    selected=Q
    redundant: selected=H OR Q.

H is NOT called consciousness, and R is a context description, NOT a direct subjective report. The intervention is a value clamp, not killing the process or removing biological tissue. All three intact runs have identical H,Q,selector,answer and report values for every input.

| Architecture | Intact | Clamp H=0 | Clamp Q=0 | Clamp both=0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shared | 1 | 9/16 | 1 | 9/16 |
| bypass | 1 | 1 | 9/16 | 9/16 |
| redundant | 1 | 1 | 1 | 9/16 |

Context-description accuracy in every architecture is respectively 1,1/2,1,1/2. Therefore a deterioration of that description channel can coexist with preserved arithmetic, and arithmetic deterioration can coexist with an accurate description.

### EI-T1 — Necessity is not transported by intact task equivalence

These three systems agree on all intact answers yet differ under the same named AND physically matched register clamp. Thus intact equality does not entail equality of the dependency profile. Redundant routes also show that neither single register must be individually indispensable even when their joint loss matters.

Proof: substitute the selector equations. If context is lost, the system always adds. It remains correct on all 16 c=0 tasks, and on the two c=1 pairs satisfying xy=x+y, namely (0,0) and (2,2). Hence 18/32=9/16. The other table entries follow from survival of an unclamped route. This is a finite causal counterexample, not a neural experiment or a new general theory of causation.

## 4. A positive necessity: preserve the task distinctions somewhere

Let C index N distinct complete task-response profiles f_c:X->Y. At a specified cut, all remaining context-dependent information accessible to future execution is encoded as e(c) in Z, with |Z|<=M. The decoder and future mechanism are fixed across contexts. Future query x may vary, but no future input reveals additional c information. Exactness requires d(e(c),x)=f_c(x) for every c,x.

### EI-T2 — Distinction-cut bound

Exact performance requires M>=N. Proof: if two distinct profiles have the same remaining code, the fixed decoder gives the same output on every common query, contradicting a query on which their profiles differ. The elementary pigeonhole/decoding principle is established mathematics.

For uniformly sampled distinct profiles, optimal identification of the ENTIRE profile from M code states has success at most min(1,M/N), achievable with an arbitrary code occupying min(M,N) classes. Each occupied deterministic code class can contribute at most one correctly identified profile. A stochastic encoder obeys the same bound because sum_z max_c P(z|c)/N <= M/N.

Do NOT confuse whole-profile identification with accuracy on one randomly sampled task. Profiles may agree on some queries.

### EI-T3 — Optimal arithmetic after both cue routes are erased

Allow any decoder of x,y, not just our original program, while excluding later c-dependent inputs. For 14 of the 16 operand pairs, addition and multiplication differ, giving best correctness 1/2 under uniform c. For two pairs they agree, giving 1. Thus optimal single-query accuracy is (14/2+2)/16=9/16, attained by always adding. The bound reflects missing distinctions, not the weakness of one algorithm.

A later operation cue restores a channel rather than violating the result. Compiling a different mechanism for each c moves the distinction into mechanism choice and violates the fixed-mechanism premise. Counting a person supplying the operation plus a calculator as an isolated context-blind calculator changes the evaluated boundary.

## 5. Minimal sufficient route families, not a universal consciousness core

Under this fixed zero-clamp and no-adaptation regime, sufficient cue-route sets have minimal members {H} for shared, {Q} for bypass, and either {H} or {Q} for redundant. There can be multiple alternatives and no unique individually indispensable register. This is a task-specific core family, not the minimum anatomical center of human experience. Different targets, times, permitted compensation and boundary inputs yield different families. No monotonicity of general neural lesion effects is assumed.

This also clarifies the sensory-removal thought experiment: preserving visual content, arithmetic competence, autobiography and basal experience are different targets. A missing sensory route need not remove every remaining actual process or its experience. Neither continuing local experience nor surviving task performance entails that all original experiential content remains unchanged.

## 6. What follows in UCT, and what does not

The physical organization performing a calculation has an experiential structural description under C1. UCT does not add an independently switchable phenomenal substance to an otherwise complete mechanism. Human manipulations may alter organization that both supplies task distinctions and participates in a selected experiential organization; changes can therefore occur together. Another architecture may carry those task distinctions elsewhere. This supplies a possible organization-relative account of joint impairment and dissociation without two different substrate laws.

This model does not determine which human feeling is absent or present in H, Q or their assembly. Only an independently admitted actual complete P/I/K plus C1 licenses the structural interpretation. Neither an exact answer nor a human-like sentence identifies full experiential type. The experience of a calculator has not been calibrated as a small number on a common richness scale, and general intelligence has not been identified with one arithmetic score.

This is consistent with UCT III but does not independently validate C1, establish a named-feeling bridge, or decide the current assistant's consciousness. It introduces no report/memory/self/integration gate for basal experience and no exclusive subject requirement.

## 7. Existing evidence and direct antecedents

Varley et al. (2005), *Agrammatic but numerate*, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0407470102, PMID 15713804: three men with extensive left perisylvian lesions showed severe grammatical impairments with preserved basic calculation and structure-sensitive mathematical operations. Author-repository and primary bibliographic abstracts were inspected. This is not evidence of absent experience or universal language dispensability.

Dehaene et al. (1998), *Imaging unconscious semantic priming*, Nature, DOI 10.1038/26967, PMID 9783584: the primary abstract reports task-related processing of masked numerical primes without awareness of those primes while participants worked on a visible target. Nonawareness of one stimulus is not absence of all experience in the participant, still less absence of UCT basal experience. No raw data or full methods were reanalysed.

Edelman & Gally (2001), *Degeneracy and complexity in biological systems*, PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.231499798: structurally different biological elements can support similar functions. Publisher abstract/opening discussion read; this is an antecedent, not our discovery. Simple copied-register redundancy does not encompass all biological degeneracy.
