## 1. Question and inherited foundation

The author asks which organization sustains a person's experience when visual/auditory input is absent, how far removal can proceed, and how this differs from a cell's experience. UCT I v1.2 P3 retains a local actual process while its constitutive dynamics, accountable boundary and lineage persist. C1/U1 applies to it and to an independently admitted actual whole. Embedding does not delete local experience; persistence does not entail unchanged content. A surviving cell does not by that fact preserve the former person's experiential organization. No unique extra owner is required before an actual whole has experience.

Four targets must be separated: nonempty experience; an independently specified experiential feature/class; complete experiential structural type; continuation of a particular actual process/person. Sensory loss need not destroy every selected experiential relation, but does not preserve all previous sensory contents. A present nonvisual experience can be a whole experience without matching an earlier visual one. 'Incomplete relative to a target' must not mean an incomplete person.

Actual organization, selected physical model, intervention evidence, capability and named feeling remain separate. No toy variable below is a measure of a human or cellular feeling. These constraints inherit UCT I v1.2 §§2.4–3.1 and TA25 D1–D5, not a new axiom.

## 2. Four thought-experiment operations

Distinguish closing a sensory input, changing an internal constitutive mechanism, cutting a relation without removing its carriers, and replacing a carrier while preserving an adequate full interface. Fix the bearer/reference, interval, initial history, comparison signature, timing and external support. Immediate excision and later adaptation are different operations. Enabling support such as physiological maintenance is not automatically a representation of every phenomenal content. The CG replacement result is a conditional route, not evidence that actual neural substitution satisfies its premises.

## 3. Preservation contract

Let V be a finite physically interpreted set of component/mechanism slots. A contract C fixes P0,V,K,I,H,B,pi,T,A_T: original reference, signature, interval, admissible histories, boundary/environment, transformation policy pi(S), independently stated target and acceptance set. For retained S subset V define F_C(S)=1 iff every h in H produces an admitted target comparison accepted by A_T. An undefined target fails this particular test; it does not establish absence of all remaining experience. When T names a feeling, its interpretation must be independently admitted; the toy examples instead use physical response relations.

For nonempty F={S:F_C(S)=1}, define M={S in F:no proper subset of S belongs to F} and K_required=intersection(F). M is a family of inclusion-minimal successful retained organizations, not minimal subjects. Inclusion-minimal, least, minimum-cardinality and single-deletion local minimum are different notions. The transformation policy may include compensation and adaptation only when explicitly declared.

## 4. General proofs

P1. Every successful S contains a minimal successful member: descend through successful proper subsets until finite descent terminates. Descent does not promise a safe one-at-a-time physical path. Further, intersection(F)=intersection(M): one inclusion uses M subset F; for the other choose a minimal member inside each S.

P2. A least successful set exists iff K_required belongs to F iff M has exactly one member. A successful intersection lies in every successful member. Conversely a least member equals the intersection; a unique minimal member lies inside each successful set by P1. The common necessary intersection can fail to be sufficient. An empty sufficient set owing to an external supplier does not identify an internal neural core.

P3. Under identical slots, transformation policy, histories and boundaries, if preservation of T2 entails preservation of T1, then F2 subset F1. Each stronger successful set contains a weaker minimal support, and for nonempty families K_required(T1) subset K_required(T2). The minimal families need not themselves be nested collections.

P4. Add the independent premise that F is upward closed. Then S succeeds iff it contains a member of M. Thus a removed D destroys success iff D intersects every M; minimal destructive D are minimal hitting sets. This is established reliability/set mathematics. Neural or logic mechanisms may violate upward closure; it must not be silently assumed.

## 5. A positive dynamic witness: input loss versus retained organization

C includes a binary latch q and return receiver/input combiner. Present relays R1/R2 copy q; absent relays supply zero. V,A are separate input ports with common inputs u,w. REP is read-only. Clock/power remain declared external.

    r1=R1*q; r2=R2*q;
    q_next=(r1 OR r2) XOR (V*u) XOR (A*w), when C exists.

Without C the chosen q target is undefined. Compare q0=0 and q0=1 under each SAME arbitrary input sequence. Their difference is preserved at every subsequent step iff C AND (R1 OR R2). Equal inputs cancel when the paired trajectories are XORed. Therefore minimal supports are {C,R1},{C,R2}; their common required {C} is insufficient. Each relay is separately removable, but both cannot be removed. Minimal destructive cuts are {C} and {R1,R2}.

Deleting V preserves the distinction-retention target but removes response to u. For retention plus that port sensitivity, minimal supports become {C,R1,V},{C,R2,V}. Deleting REP does not affect either target. This is a structural demonstration of why one input can disappear without destroying another ongoing organization. It is not a brain model identifying q with experience, and does not add a recurrence gate for basal experience.

## 6. A nonmonotone failure of 'cannot cut once more'

C contains a latch/agreement gate; installed A/B provide 1 and excised inputs become 0. With C present set q_next=q if A=B, otherwise q_next=0. For initial-distinction retention the only successful supports are {C} and {C,A,B}. Every single deletion from the full set fails, yet the smaller {C} succeeds. Single-step irreducibility does not imply inclusion-minimality. This is a finite logical counterexample, not a claim about beneficial co-lesions in people.

## 7. Same final support, different preservation history

Start the gate model at q=1,A=B=1. Deleting A/B simultaneously before the next update leaves q=1. Deleting A, permitting one update, then deleting B leaves q=0. Both end at support {C}, but different pointed states remain. The difference is physically recorded now; this is not ghost history with identical complete present organization. A support-only predicate needs a fixed temporal intervention policy. Endpoint success and preservation throughout a path are distinct.

## 8. Human and cellular scope

Under UCT, valid cell processes and human-scale processes both have nonempty experience, but need not share temporal relations, discrimination, content or self-related organization. C1 alone does not translate cellular dynamics into human feeling vocabulary. Cutting until any basal experience remains asks the wrong question about preservation of a person's experience. Requiring every former sensory channel also presupposes the answer. A substantive human application must independently identify the experiential relation/class to preserve and its physical realization; HUMAN_CORE and ACTUAL_BRIDGE remain OPEN.

Dreaming, sensory loss, infancy/development, prosthetic substitution and distributed replacement can constrain candidate contracts without imposing a language, report or unique-owner threshold. No universal anatomical core or guaranteed preserved personal identity is derived here.
