The central statistical statement is a projection of a classical binomial confidence interval under known nuisance quantities. It does not estimate those quantities, verify a shared internal sample, locate a neural process, or measure an experiential variable. It is sharp as a preimage of the specified Clopper–Pearson interval, not a claim that this interval is shortest, that the paired-yes count is sufficient for every finite-sample purpose, or that one physical paired trial identifies a mechanism.

## Mathematical checks

1. **Observation law.** With independent identically distributed pairs and fixed sample size, the indicator that both reports are yes is Bernoulli with probability `p11`; its sum is binomial even though the two reports within a pair are dependent. Within-pair dependence is the signal of interest. Dependence between pairs, time-varying parameters, or unadjusted optional stopping invalidates the stated binomial guarantee. These restrictions are explicit in the proof.

2. **Joint lapse expression.** Expanding the four combinations of independent lapse indicators gives the nonlapse Gaussian rectangle with coefficient `(1-lapse_o)*(1-lapse_s)`, two single-lapse terms equal to one half of the remaining nonlapse marginal, and a double-lapse term equal to one quarter. The implemented expression and Equation (4) of the proof agree. Known marginal lapse rates alone are insufficient; the independent indicators and fair guesses are separate premises.

3. **Absolute correlation and both covariance signs.** Centered interval events are invariant to reversing either Gaussian sign. The rectangle identifies `abs(a+c)/sqrt(v_o*v_s)`. For nonnegative `a` and `R`, a feasible negative branch requires `R+a` to satisfy the criterion bound; because `abs(R-a) <= R+a`, the positive branch also accommodates that same `a`. The continuous positive-branch projection therefore contains the entire negative branch. Taking the union over both signs does not require an unreported sign assumption.

4. **Point and interval projection.** In equal variances, solving `abs(r-a/v) <= kappa*(1-a/v)` gives the two stated endpoints. For `kappa < 1`, both endpoints are nondecreasing functions of `r`; the feasible region in normalized variance and correlation is convex. The preimage of a closed correlation interval is consequently the displayed closed interval, with every included point attainable by a positive-semidefinite Gaussian criterion covariance matrix. At `kappa = 1`, the lower endpoint is zero and the upper endpoint is `(1+r_U)/2`. This separate case is necessary; replacing a bound of one by an almost-one value is not harmless near perfect correlation.

5. **Unequal variances.** Squaring the feasible positive-branch covariance bound yields the quadratic with coefficients `1-kappa^2`, `kappa^2*(v_o+v_s)-2R`, and `R^2-kappa^2*v_o*v_s`. The criterion-radius function `sqrt((v_o-a)*(v_s-a))` is concave on the physical variance domain. Convexity supplies an interval projection. The maximum upper endpoint occurs at total covariance `min(v_o,v_s)` when it lies in the candidate strip, and otherwise at the nearest strip endpoint, as in Equation (20). The global attainable-correlation restriction is required before inversion. These operations are analytic; no finite covariance grid substitutes for the continuous projection.

6. **Coverage of the complete population set.** On the single event that the CP interval contains the true paired probability, every variance compatible with that probability has an admissible witness covariance and is therefore retained by the projected set. This gives coverage of the entire true-calibration population identified set, and hence of the actual generating variance, with at least the nominal probability. There is no additional multiplicity penalty for elements of this one set because the same probability-coverage event protects all of them. This is not a simultaneous guarantee for many separately constructed participant or condition intervals.

7. **Empty and degenerate sets.** A probability interval disjoint from the full model range has an empty preimage. Under the complete valid contract that event implies failure to cover the true probability and has probability at most alpha. It tests the combined model and calibration package; it does not reveal which premise failed. Zero halfwidth or unit independent lapse can make the probability constant, so the result must be either the full physical domain or the empty set. The final analytic full-domain guard also returns the exact input domain when the probability interval covers the complete attainable range, including no observations. These cases are handled distinctly in the code.

8. **Calibration uncertainty.** Taking the union over an externally valid calibration set and intersecting the two coverage events gives the stated `1-alpha-gamma` guarantee without an independence requirement. The validity of both marginal coverage statements is still necessary. With only an uncertain criterion-correlation bound, increasing the bound enlarges feasible sets, so its upper endpoint gives the exact continuous union. A finite list of calibration points does not automatically cover a continuous confidence region. The code and proof explicitly decline that invalid substitution. The operating-characteristic grid assumes known calibration and provides no demonstration of coverage after plug-in estimation.

## Operating-characteristic interpretation checks

The 480 scenarios contain 240 valid cases and 240 labeled assumption controls. All three profiles and every fixed generating variance enter the reported grouped summaries. The generating laws match the plan. Entire population-set coverage is calculated only for valid cases and requires both interval endpoints to be contained. A true point can be covered when the full population set is not, so point and whole-set coverage are correctly stored separately.

The runner reports unconditional width with an empty set assigned width zero, alongside empty probability and width conditional on nonemptiness. In the extreme shared-lapse case, unconditional width is about `1.10e-10` because the set is empty with probability `0.9999999972`; this is correctly described as incompatibility rather than high precision. Even valid boundary cases have nonzero empty probability, so unconditional expected confidence width need not always exceed population identified-set width.

The independent-resampling control changes the target's meaning. Its per-task sensory variance may be 0.9 while actual shared sensory covariance is zero. The published inclusion probability for 0.9 is a target-misattribution diagnostic, not failed coverage of a true shared variance of 0.9. The report, CSV, and figure caption maintain this distinction. The wrong-bound and shared-lapse controls instead have an actual shared variance but violate premises of its confidence map. The binomial probability interval remains valid across all these controls because paired trials remain independent with a fixed paired probability.

The numerical grid verifies selected operating characteristics; the proof supplies the general coverage statement under the stated law. Neither establishes the law in humans. Grid means and envelope minima are not averages over a population, power estimates, or sample-size advice. The method deliberately uses no selected participant parameter example and no new paired human data.

## Closed numerical correction and reproducibility

The theory collaborator's expanded unequal-variance checks found that an exactly full-domain result could return a slightly rounded upper endpoint and the label `informative`. The repaired analytic branch was reviewed against the set definition. It changes neither the observation model nor the fixed design. The earlier module and execution are retained.

The operating-characteristic runner was executed once against the final module to update the execution hash. `CORRECTION_REEXECUTION_RECEIPT.json` verifies that the complete CSV is byte-identical, all 124 stored NumPy arrays are exactly identical including missing-value locations, and the full summary is identical except for completion time and elapsed runtime. The old human-data fits and old 19,999 conditional simulations were not rerun.

The code uses double-precision special functions and deterministic root finding. Its numerical checks are not interval-arithmetic proof certificates. Tests extremely close to saturated probability boundaries are separately documented as numerically indeterminate rather than counted as successful direct-membership checks. This restriction is consistent with the manuscript's separation of real-arithmetic proof and numerical implementation.

## Publication boundary

No blocking mathematical or implementation inconsistency was found within this review's scope. The release may state the exact conditional coverage theorem and its fixed numerical illustration. It should retain the same-sample, Gaussian, center, independence, fixed-sample-size, and calibration assumptions; the empty-set rule; the partial-identification interpretation; the inherited statistical attribution; and the absence of a validated human paired-response measurement.
