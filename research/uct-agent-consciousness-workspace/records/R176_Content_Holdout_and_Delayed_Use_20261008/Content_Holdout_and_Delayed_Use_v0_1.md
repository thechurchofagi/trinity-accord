# Content hold-out, correspondence completion, and delayed use

R176 v0.1 — 8 October 2026. Unpublished research correction and bounded candidate comparison.

## 1. What is corrected

R175's finite-chain betweenness theorem is correct. Its characterization as an established **held-out experiential-content prediction** is too strong. There are two different tasks:

- Given complete independent structures X and E, infer an unknown correspondence f. The orientation anchors can solve this correspondence-completion task.
- Given independently identified experiential items but incomplete relations, predict a relation between those fixed items. R175 did not separate the target-bearing experiential premises from the input for this task.

A deduction is of course entailed by all its premises. Entailment is not itself data leakage. The problem is the provenance of those premises: was the target, an equivalent encoding, or target-dependent identification of its relata used to admit the structure or the map? Withholding a target from the *anchor list* is insufficient. A complete independently measured E could support a legitimate prediction of an unknown physical-to-experiential correspondence, but that is not a new prediction of E's already supplied order.

This correction does not refute C1, deny structural explanation, or prove empirical testing impossible. It prevents an unearned claim of progress on QC-10. No new phenomenal observations are available in this round.

## 2. Fixed target versus moving image labels

Use neutral labels E={a,b,c,d}; none denotes a rank. Fix one comparison episode, bearer convention, time grain and target T(E)=[b precedes c in the stipulated experienced-presentation relation]. This target concerns the presented temporal order of two identified contents, not a verbal temporal-order judgment, source timestamp, retrospective causal attribution or familiar mineness. Independent identification of b and c, and evidence for this target, remain application obligations. These names are not defined as f(x1) and f(x2) after selecting f.

Let D contain strict total orders on these four labels with a least, d greatest, and b,c strictly between a and d. The physical chain is fixed as x0<x1<x2<x3. The only shared correspondences initially supplied are x0↦a and x3↦d.

**R176-P1 (finite partial-information nonidentification).** Exactly two orders in D remain:

    E+ : a < b < c < d;     E− : a < c < b < d.

They agree on the supplied endpoint and interior facts and disagree on T. Consequently those facts do not identify T. Even requiring existence of an oriented betweenness isomorphism from X, with these endpoint anchors, does not identify T: E+ admits f+=(a,b,c,d), while E− admits f−=(a,c,b,d).

Proof: after fixing the endpoints, the two middle positions can be filled in exactly two ways. Both orders have the required interiors. The listed increasing bijections preserve and reflect every betweenness triple and satisfy both anchors. T is respectively true and false. QED.

In both models f(x1)<E f(x2) is true. This expression does not contrast the fixed pair (b,c) if f itself changes. Thus R175's image-order conclusion survives as a correspondence theorem; it is not a prediction of the same independently identified content pair across these alternatives. For n≥4 endpoint-fixed chains, the analogous partial-data class has (n−2)! orders on neutral interior labels; exchanging any two specified distinct interior labels reverses their pair order while leaving endpoint/interior facts intact. This is elementary finite-order reasoning, not a new foundational theorem.

These are logical completions of partial descriptions, **not two actual phenomenal states assigned to one identical complete K-organization**. No complete actual C1 realization pair is constructed. R175 §5's E+/E− discussion therefore supplies epistemic interpretation alternatives unless actual admissibility and independently warranted target contrast are separately established. The corrected same-theta/different-F criterion remains logically right; the physical counterexample needed to instantiate it remains open.

## 3. The missing assumption made explicit

Suppose an independent content-identity procedure fixes q(x0)=a,q(x1)=b,q(x2)=c,q(x3)=d without using their relative temporal order. Let H be the hypothesis that this **same q** preserves physical betweenness on the entire target tuple, together with its endpoint orientation. D alone admits both T values; D+H admits only E+.

This can be a genuine conditional content prediction if q, H and D were fixed without target information and target evidence is then acquired independently. It is not justified by the mere existence of *some* betweenness isomorphism: both completions have one. Nor may q be reidentified by rank after seeing the outcome. C1 supplies an actual complete-structure isomorphism, but identifying q with its appropriate restriction and identifying the relation with experienced presentation remain substantive premises.

A useful audit table is:

| Input | Contains target information? | What may be concluded? |
|---|---|---|
| Endpoint/interior facts on neutral labels | No; two target values remain | Named middle order is unresolved |
| Complete independently obtained E | Yes, its named order is already given | Unknown cross-domain mapping may be completed |
| Fixed identity q plus predeclared H | H constrains the target, which is legitimate as a hypothesis | Conditional testable named-order consequence |
| E or q chosen by physical rank after inspection | Target alignment is built in | No independent content success |

This is a dependency/provenance audit for the selected problem, not a new generic certificate research program.

## 4. Positive candidate comparison: production order versus actual use order

The correction suggests a concrete main-line comparison. Fix an action episode with a prediction marker B and a sensory feedback marker C, each with independently maintained content identity. Distinguish production occurrences Bp,Cp from later consumer-use occurrences Bu,Cu. All are inside the stipulated process/interval; their producer–trace–consumer bindings are explicit. A stored marker with no executed consumer is outside this selected comparison, without any inference about basal experience.

Let tB<tC be production times, dB,dC≥0 be actual storage/transmission delays, and uB=tB+dB, uC=tC+dC be actual first-use times at one fixed consumer. Define gap g=tC−tB>0. The elementary organizational result is

    uB > uC  iff  dB−dC > g;
    uB = uC  iff  dB−dC = g;
    uB < uC  iff  dB−dC < g.

**R176-P2 (causal delayed-use contrast).** Under these simultaneous assumptions, production order is B before C, while use order reverses exactly at the displayed strict inequality. Proof: subtract the two use-time equations, uB−uC=dB−dC−g. The equal case is explicitly excluded from strict-before claims. No probability, generic control optimization, report test or consciousness axiom enters this algebra.

A causal witness uses tB=1,tC=2,dB=2,dC=0. B is produced at 1 and retained until actual use at 3; C is produced and used at 2. No use precedes its source. Episode endpoints are 0 and 4. A baseline with dB=dC=0 preserves production and use order. Keep marker identities, source order, report script and consumer definition fixed; change only B's delay in this selected model. The complete histories differ: equal reports do not imply complete organizational equality.

Now compare only two **unvalidated selected-content bridges**, both to the same fixed target T:

- H_prod: experienced presentation order of the identified B/C contents follows their producer order.
- H_use: it follows their actual first-use order at the specified consumer.

They agree on the baseline and make opposite conditional predictions on the delay witness: H_prod predicts B before C; H_use predicts C before B. At equal use times H_use supplies neither strict direction; do not encode simultaneity as absence of experience. The candidate use bridge is motivated by actual present uptake rather than merely stored availability, but this motivation is not evidence that use time equals experienced time. Temporal reconstruction, multiple consumers, and retrospective representation could defeat either simple bridge. The domain must therefore retain one consumer and the stipulated target meaning, or decline application. A richer episode may contain both event order and represented-order relations; two different targets must not be forced into a false conflict.

This delivers a concrete explanatory choice: a retained internal action prediction and incoming bodily feedback can keep their identities and objective production order while changing their relation in current use. In UCT, independently grounded organization is transported within experience under C1. Which transported relation is *this named presentation order*, and whether it contributes to felt agency or familiar mineness, remain separate questions. The model explains a candidate organizational distinction, not an observed change of feeling.

## 5. Thought-experiment obligations

**TE176-1, sealed content labels.** Fix endpoint anchors and neutral content identities; vary only the unresolved middle order. The two completions diagnose the target/mapping ambiguity. Changing q by rank after opening the target is the explicit failed attempt.

**TE176-2, delayed prediction with unchanged report.** Fix source contents, production times, consumer and report script; vary B's actual delay from 0 to 2. Track production, retention and use separately. This separates H_prod and H_use while providing no independent observation of T. Its counterexample role is disagreement between candidate bridges, not proof that one is true.

- Formation/ancestors: neither the delay relation nor its absence creates basal experience; lack of a reporting method leaves measurement partial.
- Abacus/calculator: a list of sorted timestamps is not an executed producer–trace–consumer relation. A physically coupled implementation can instantiate that relation regardless of substrate.
- Human-realized agent: participant, implemented computation and coupled process may have different consumers and temporal grains. Apply the comparison to one declared bearer at a time; require no exclusive owner.
- Copy/rewiring/memory: copying B's value or report can leave its consumer time/provenance changed. Neither value identity nor preserved task output proves experiential identity or numerical survival.
