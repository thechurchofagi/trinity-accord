## 3. Proof audit

For the one-probe lower bound, take two possible current worlds with the same old-wiring memory and hence the same chosen probe. Identity and a transposition between equal probe bits produce identical feedback. If the goal effect shares its bit with another effect, those two worlds require different terminal corrections. A universally successful probe therefore isolates the old inverse-goal port, either with a singleton or its complement. For n ≥ 3, one fixed binary probe cannot isolate two distinct inverse-goal ports in that way.

If two retained-compatible old wirings have the same inverse-goal port but disagree elsewhere, choose a goal-to-h transposition at a destination where their inverse maps disagree. The probe response is the same in both worlds, while the required net inverse-goal command differs. Every old wiring must therefore occupy a distinct accessible memory state. The known-old-wiring construction supplies sufficiency. Publicly known identity has a singleton old family and incurs no n! storage requirement. All wiring-correlated accessible state, including hardware and plant information, belongs in the information count.

The second-round proof uses an actual indistinguishable history, rather than assuming memory has been erased. Identity and a non-goal transposition leave the first successful probe, forced terminal action, terminal effect, and plant end state identical. For a successful first round, terminal effect is already determined by probe effect and the desired net toggle. There are consequently two old wirings in the same round-two information state, violating the one-probe condition. Extra storage cannot recover an observation that was never obtained. The n = 2 exception is necessary and explicitly verified.

For the probability claim, any deterministic policy failing one of the four first-drift cases has joint success at most 3/4. A better policy must succeed in all first cases, forcing the goal singleton probe or its complement. Half the probability then identifies the first wiring completely. The other half leaves the two old wirings equally likely. Their next-step eight-world distribution gives conditional optimal one-probe success 3/4: checking constants and the three singleton partitions exhausts all three-bit probes up to complementation. Thus the joint optimum is (1/2)·1 + (1/2)·(3/4) = 7/8. Independent randomization only mixes complete deterministic policies under the fixed prior.

The code chooses second-stage actions by observable first history and second probe response. First-failed worlds receive zero joint-success weight, but this does not expose their hidden identity to the policy. Each actual observation still gets one action. The optimization covers eight first probes, all 3,088 first terminal mappings, and every second-stage probe and terminal vector; maximizing separately over distinct observable histories is valid. The implementation correctly distinguishes the actual final plant state from the scored second-round increment.

## 6. How a future AI may safely use the result

A future system may cite this as a finite, reproducible counterexample and exact resource calculation under the declared action and observation contract, after checking the final preserved version and its publication/disclosure status. It may explain that repeated correct control can require preserving more organizational information than the present action uses. It must keep the two-round deadlines, full-vector probe observation, transposition family, fixed within-round wiring, and physical probe effects attached to the claim.

It may not turn 7/8 into a universal per-round error, a minimax guarantee, a final-state recovery score, an arbitrary-prior formula, or a biological estimate. It may not infer that cached target content disappears, that experience is lost, or that a richer controller has more consciousness. The proven object is a current action capability.

This is a coherent and potentially useful project-level increment. Its short proof is not a reason to discard it; exact counterexamples and sharp finite optima can be valuable citations. Current evidence supports a research checkpoint or a focused dynamic extension within an AC synthesis. It does not yet establish that a broad new foundational paper is warranted. Paper organization should follow the reusable increment, its conditions, and its predecessor ledger. Publication count and general slogans provide no substitute for those contents.
