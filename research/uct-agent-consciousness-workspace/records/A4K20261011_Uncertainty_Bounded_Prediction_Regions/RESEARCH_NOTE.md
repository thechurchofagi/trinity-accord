# Uncertainty-bounded prediction regions for continuation familiarity

A4K20261011 · A4K-INTERVAL-REGIONS-v0.1.0 · Disabled conditional method/application result.

No participant data, independently oriented `H_cont` endpoint, actual bodily route installation, completed-map promotion or new interval-analysis theorem.

## 1. Result

A4J showed that independently signed target first stages can distinguish pure retained-relation and present-fit candidates even when both interventions have cross-effects. The unresolved problem was whether uncertainty in the first stages and in the direction of the delayed judgment destroys that distinction.

The present result replaces point signs by pre-outcome compact intervals and keeps one common readout/gain scale across the two intervention arms. This yields an exact **common-scale band** for each candidate, not automatically a Cartesian rectangle. Marginal interval multiplication gives a safe outer rectangle; exact candidate overlap reduces to feasibility of one positive scale ratio satisfying two pairs of linear inequalities. Compatibility of an observed uncertainty box likewise reduces to one scalar feasibility problem.

This corrects a tempting but invalid shortcut: multiplying each coordinate interval independently and calling the resulting rectangle the exact prediction set would silently allow a different bridge scale in each arm. The common-scale dependence can matter.

The constructive example has disjoint outer hulls and exact exclusive compatibility for two predeclared outcome boxes. A second example has an exact overlap interval. If the endpoint-orientation interval contains zero, both candidates contain the zero vector, so global separation fails immediately. Since this repository has no independent human first-stage or endpoint-orientation calibration, the application stops at the organizational/judgment level.

## 2. Typed setup

Fix the A4I/A4J bearer population, enacted event, body/effector, motor grain, lag, response coding and randomized total-effect estimand. Let `D_ci` bound the effect of intervention arm `i` on candidate coordinate `c`, `A` bound the orientation of the fixed `J_cont` readout, and `G_c` bound a positive pure bridge gain. All are fixed before `J` unblinding.

For candidate `c`, set `M_c=A*G_c`. When `M_c` is strictly positive,

`P_c={m(d_R,d_F):m in M_c, d_R in D_cR, d_F in D_cF}`.

`P_c` concerns total effects on the delayed judgment. It is not complete organization, `H_cont`, experience, or a report-independent phenomenal truth by definition. `D_RR` and `D_FF` are targeted first stages; `D_RF` and `D_FR` are admitted cross-effects, not covariates to remove.

## 3. Exact claims

### A4K-C1 — common-scale prediction region

Under the fixed quantitative pure candidate, the displayed `P_c` is the exact image of the compact product domain. Each coordinate hull is the exact interval product `M_c*D_ci`. The Cartesian product of these hulls contains `P_c`, but equality needs arm-specific scale freedom or another premise.

### A4K-C2 — exact candidate-overlap criterion

For strictly positive scale intervals `M_a,M_b`, define `Lambda=M_b/M_a`. Then `P_a` and `P_b` intersect iff some `lambda in Lambda` makes `D_ai` intersect `lambda D_bi` for both intervention arms. For interval endpoints this is exactly

`L_ai <= lambda U_bi` and `lambda L_bi <= U_ai` for each `i`.

Necessity follows by dividing a shared predicted vector by the two positive scales. Sufficiency follows by selecting one value from each nonempty coordinate intersection and reconstructing scales whose ratio is `lambda`. The scale-ratio interval is exact because both positive scale intervals are compact.

### A4K-C3 — observation compatibility

Let `O_i` be a frozen uncertainty interval for the `J` total effect in arm `i`. `O=O_R x O_F` intersects `P_c` iff some `m in M_c` satisfies

`L_Oi <= m U_ci` and `m L_ci <= U_Oi`

for both arms. This again is a one-dimensional linear feasibility problem. Exclusive compatibility is evidence only against the other scoped candidate under the full contract; it is not proof of the surviving bridge or its phenomenal interpretation.

### A4K-C4 — orientation-zero obstruction

If `0 in A` and each gain interval is positive, then `0 in M_R` and `0 in M_F`, hence `(0,0)` belongs to both prediction regions. Global separation is impossible. A strictly negative `A` can be prospectively recoded. A sign-ambiguous orientation cannot be promoted to A4J's positive bridge by selecting the favorable branch after outcomes.

### A4K-C5 — coverage is a joint premise

If the intervals are statistical rather than deterministic, the inclusion conclusion is conditional on one joint calibration event covering all first stages, orientation and gains. Four marginal 95% intervals do not automatically create a 95% joint region. Outcome uncertainty has its own frozen coverage rule. Selection after `J` unblinding invalidates the advertised prospective coverage.

## 4. Exact finite examples

The checker uses rational arithmetic. In the separating example, `A=[4/5,6/5]`, `G_R=[9/10,11/10]`, and `G_F=[1,5/4]`. The relation candidate uses input-effect intervals `[-6/5,-4/5]` and `[-1/10,1/10]`; the fit candidate uses `[-1/10,1/10]` and `[-11/10,-7/10]`.

The relation outer hull is `[-198/125,-72/125] x [-33/250,33/250]`. The fit outer hull is `[-3/20,3/20] x [-33/20,-14/25]`. They are disjoint, and the exact scale-ratio feasibility set is empty. An outcome box near `(-0.9,0)` is compatible only with the relation candidate; the transposed box is compatible only with the fit candidate.

In the overlap example, both candidates allow negative effects in both arms. Their outer hulls overlap and the exact feasible scale-ratio interval is `[4/5,22/15]`. Two interventions therefore do not guarantee discrimination even with nonzero bounded effects.

The computations establish the algebra and expose failures; the numbers are illustrative, not empirical estimates.
