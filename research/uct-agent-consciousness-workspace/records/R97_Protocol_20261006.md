# R97 protocol — learned reward ancestry and path-blocking extrapolation

Date: 2026-10-06. Continues R96/R96D. Tiny synthetic learning experiment; not externally preregistered.

Goal: test whether a learned policy recovers direct-Q, successor-O and task-G reward ancestry from bundled/joint training support, and whether held-out q_direct/o_direct/task_only contrasts expose the learned mechanism.

Feature order is (q,o,g). Training support:
q_bundled=(1,0,1), o_bundled=(0,1,1), qo_joint=(1,1,1), plus exact sign reversals.
Held-out support:
q_direct=(1,0,0), o_direct=(0,1,0), task_only=(0,0,1), plus reversals.

Four reward ancestries:
task_only=(0,0,1), direct_Q=(1,0,0), mixed_Q_task=(1,0,1), successor_only=(0,1,0).

Target choice probabilities are sigmoid(2 * theta dot x). This makes ancestry genuinely distinguishable on the training support and satisfies the R96D learning-identifiability gate.

Compare:
1. structured linear-logit policy on the declared path basis;
2. generic 3-hidden-unit tanh MLP, seeds 200..207.

Both use full-batch Adam, 5000 steps, lr=0.03. Do not replace seeds or extend failed runs. Primary diagnostics: training probability error, held-out probability error, and antisymmetric held-out effects 0.5[z(x)-z(-x)] on q/o/g.

Interpretation rule: successful in-domain fit does not establish causal-path recovery unless held-out path-blocking effects match ancestry. Do not call any path coefficient fear, valence or consciousness.
