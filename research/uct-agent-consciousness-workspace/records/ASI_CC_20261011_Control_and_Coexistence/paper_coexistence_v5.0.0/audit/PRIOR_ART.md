# PR-v5 targeted prior-art and priority audit (2026-10-11)

**The genuine next problem is not to rediscover false data injection or report redundancy.** PR-v4 already contained a false-sensor and actuator-bypass negative test. This revision makes the attack budget relative to *actual upstream control roots*, with explicit simultaneous root effects on observation, backup capacity and final actuation.

Primary research matched:

- Fawzi, Tabuada, Diggavi, *Secure Estimation and Control for Cyber-Physical Systems Under Adversarial Attacks*, IEEE TAC 59(6), 2014, DOI 10.1109/TAC.2014.2303233; https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5073 . Already analyzes attacks on sensors and actuators, secure state estimation and necessary redundancy. A root-aware correction code is not a new security-theoretic principle.
- Andersson & Dán, *Active Bayesian Inference for Robust Control under Sensor False Data Injection Attacks*, arXiv:2604.11410 (April 2026). Studies attacked sensors, active probing and recovery; our fixed pre-commit decision deliberately excludes post-commit adaptive probing.
- Terekhov et al., *Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors Subvert AI Control Protocols*, ICLR 2026. Shows monitors have adaptive attacks. Not a claim that this manuscript discovered prompt-injection failures.
- Greenblatt et al., *AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion*, ICML 2024. Foundational adversarial oversight; its success metric/task setting differs from our proof of effect-level revocation combined with maintenance of human-dependent service.
- Arike et al., *How does information access affect LLM monitors' ability to detect sabotage?*, ICML 2026. More information to an LLM monitor is not monotonically better under practical scoring, unlike *truthful refinement* of a fully faithful finite-world information partition. No contradictory claim is made.
- Muruaga, *Bounded Agents: Delegation Security for Multi-Agent AI Systems*, arXiv:2608.15888, 2026. History-bound authorization under trusted boundary assumptions; this is inherited. The reference implementation admits session-splitting limits and omitted unenumerated harmful compositions.
- UCT A4F/A4G (2026), existing internal sources: protected writer/provenance witnesses and common control ancestry. Current work makes root ancestry part of a quantitative physical/resource certificate, not an independently novel treatment of trusted witnesses.
- PR-v4.0.0 inherited covering-design and message/backup bounds, already detailed and checked. Re-running them is not new evidence.

**Increment after these precedents:** a declared 4-route/one-root adversary model in which replacing 8 role identities by 3 true fault domains is allowed, and an exact Pareto: 1 root is insufficient; 2 independently compromisable roots require 8 pre-armed universal slots; 3 or more roots require 3 slots, under one-bit/group diagnosis, up to one Byzantine root, one primary route affected, pre-commit static plan, and two complete serial revocation interlocks. Verification: direct all-16-plan oracle and an independently implemented observation-cell test both enumerate 7,380 capacity vectors; 5,432 separate capacity fault tests, 408 role-process protocol configurations (3,156 authenticated role messages). This is a new **scoped application/result construction** in this manuscript lineage. Whether its exact architecture/Pareto was published elsewhere is not globally verified. Claim only conditional exactness, not broad first priority.

**Hostile counterarguments retained:** A reliable independent downstream router or truthful bank-failure indicator can change the bound. A stronger, richer sensor symbol per root also changes the bound. Multiple route failures, unlimited root attacks, continuous time, power depletion, dynamic maintenance, or hidden physical effect routes invalidate the positive certificate unless explicitly modeled. A signing key can authenticate a false sensor message. Common administrator control of all role processes makes the experiment incapable of proving physical independence. Nothing here identifies subjective AI rights or phenomenology.
