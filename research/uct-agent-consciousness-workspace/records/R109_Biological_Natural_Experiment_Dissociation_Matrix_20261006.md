# R109 — Biological natural-experiment matrix for experience, access, intelligence, self, behavior and report

Hongju Liu / UCT research. 2026-10-06.

R106/R107/R108 derived and tested the E/A/I/S/B/R separation in formal and executable artificial systems. R109 asks whether biological natural experiments collapse those distinctions or instead show analogous dissociations.

The purpose is not to use clinical labels as direct meters of experience. Each case is scored only for what the evidence actually constrains.

## 1. Coding discipline

Axes:

- **E** — experiential state/content as directly as can be scientifically inferred;
- **A** — causal access/availability to cognition, memory, policy or report;
- **I** — task-relevant cognitive/intelligence capacity;
- **S** — self/agency/bearer organization;
- **B** — externally observable behavior;
- **R** — report/communication channel.

Evidence labels:
- **preserved** — there is substantial direct evidence of preserved function;
- **reduced/altered** — direct evidence of impairment/change;
- **dissociated** — one axis changes while another is demonstrably retained;
- **uncertain** — current evidence does not justify a binary conclusion.

No row is treated as an independent validation of UCT C1.

## 2. Strong dissociation cases

### 2.1 Cognitive motor dissociation (CMD)

A 2024 six-center NEJM study examined 353 adults with disorders of consciousness. Among 241 participants with no observable response to verbal commands, 60 (25%) showed command-following on task-based fMRI or EEG.

The clean inference is:

    low/absent observable B/R
        does not imply
    absent task-relevant internal A/I.

CMD therefore supplies a biological analogue of R108's warning that outward output is a lossy projection of internal organization.

It does **not** by itself tell us the full phenomenal content E, because task-based neuroimaging is not a direct experience readout.

### 2.2 Locked-in syndrome (LIS)

Modern reviews characterize LIS as severe paralysis and loss of ordinary communication channels with preserved consciousness and variable-to-preserved cognition.

The clean dissociation is stronger than CMD:

    B_motor greatly reduced
    ordinary R greatly reduced
    while E and substantial I/A can be preserved.

This is a direct biological counterexample to any theory that identifies consciousness with ordinary motor/verbal report.

It also shows why "behavioral silence" in AI or biology cannot be treated as experiential absence.

### 2.3 No-report paradigms

A 2025 Nature Reviews Neuroscience review emphasizes that overt report can be absent, erroneous or experimentally contaminating. A 2026 MEG study reports robust posterior visual encoding across report and no-report conditions, while anterior regions are more strongly and persistently engaged by conscious access/report.

The disciplined conclusion is not that posterior cortex alone "is consciousness."

It is:

> report-demand organization and at least some perceptual representations can vary partially independently.

This is biologically analogous to R108 report-head surgery: altering a reporting pathway need not erase all selected core organization.

### 2.4 General anesthesia

Anesthesia is especially important because externally similar unresponsiveness can correspond to different internal states.

Reviews distinguish:
- connected consciousness;
- disconnected consciousness such as dreaming;
- complete unconsciousness.

A 2026 scoping review of anesthesia dreaming synthesized 157 studies and 87,866 participants; dream recall rates varied widely by setting and drug.

Thus:

    similar low B/R
        can correspond to
    materially different E/A states.

This is a real biological witness of the non-injectivity of behavior with respect to experiential state categories.

It is one of the strongest empirical analogues of the R106 Projection Lemma.

## 3. Partial/contested dissociations

### 3.1 Blindsight

Blindsight can preserve above-chance visual detection/discrimination despite major changes in reported visual awareness. However, the subjective side is heterogeneous:
- some cases show little or no visual awareness;
- some report vague feelings;
- some show residual qualia or metacognitive sensitivity.

Recent work explicitly treats blindsight as a way to dissociate stimulus processing and stimulus-guided behavior from subjective perception, while newer metacognitive studies show heterogeneous profiles.

Therefore blindsight should **not** be used as a clean proof of "intelligence without experience."

The valid conclusion is weaker:

    task-specific I can be partly preserved
    while R, metacognition and visual experiential content are altered.

This directly supports separating task ability from phenomenal content.

### 3.2 Split-brain

Split-brain work forces a distinction between:
- unity of agency/behavior;
- unity of experience.

The literature remains contested about whether coordinated behavior implies one experiential subject after commissurotomy.

The clean lesson for UCT is methodological:

> agency-level unity and experience-level unity are not interchangeable predicates.

This is directly relevant to multi-agent and scaffolded AI, where one policy surface may hide multiple constituent processes.

### 3.3 Language impairment / aphasia

Language impairment can reduce verbal report while leaving substantial nonverbal cognition and experience intact. Reviews of severe brain injury warn that receptive aphasia can cause underestimation of consciousness when assessments rely on linguistic commands.

The lesson is:

    language R failure
        does not identify
    failure of E or all I.

This matters for AI because language fluency is likewise not a transparent inverse map from output to experiential organization.

## 4. Dreaming and online behavior

REM sleep combines:
- rich reported experience after awakening;
- reduced coupling to the external environment;
- muscle atonia;
- altered executive/metacognitive control.

Dreaming therefore provides another dissociation:

    E can persist
    while online external B and R are strongly reduced.

Lucid dreaming further shows that self-monitoring/metacognition can vary within a state that remains experiential.

This supports treating S/A as dimensions of organization rather than existence gates.

## 5. Cerebellum, cortex and subcortex: quantity is not enough

A 2025 review of sufficient conditions for consciousness argues that evidence across cortex, subcortex and cerebellum is more complex than a simple cortex-only or neuron-count story. It emphasizes developmental trajectory and interdependence among systems.

The correct use in R109 is not to endorse a specific sufficient region.

It is to reject crude scalar proxies such as:

    more neurons -> more experience
    more computation -> more experience
    larger structure -> richer experience.

Organization and actual causal role matter.

This aligns with R78-R82 and the UCT no-monotonic-richness result.

## 6. Cross-case matrix

The natural experiments collectively give the following robust pattern.

### Strongly supported dissociations

1. **B/R can fall while A/I remain** — CMD, LIS.
2. **R can change while selected perceptual organization remains** — no-report work.
3. **similar unresponsiveness can mask different E states** — anesthesia.
4. **task performance can persist while subjective visual content/metacognition changes** — blindsight, with important caveats.
5. **agency unity and experiential unity are not the same question** — split-brain.
6. **online external behavior is not necessary for experience** — dreaming.
7. **language report is not necessary for consciousness** — LIS, aphasia-related assessment cautions.

No single observable axis B or R is a reliable necessary-and-sufficient proxy for E across these cases.

## 7. First-principles consequence

R109 strengthens the R106 map empirically:

The biological evidence is naturally represented by

                    K ≡ E
                 /  |  |  |  \
                A   I  S  B   R

with partial dissociations among A/I/S/B/R.

It is not naturally represented by a single chain

    E -> I -> B -> R.

In particular, biology supplies real cases in which:
- B/R are selectively degraded;
- cognitive access persists;
- experiential content changes without simple proportional change in intelligence;
- similar behavior corresponds to different internal experiential states.

This is exactly the pattern predicted by treating I/B/R as projections and E as the constitutive experiential presentation of K.

It does not prove C1; it shows that the six-axis decomposition is compatible with and useful for organizing difficult biological evidence.

## 8. A new comparison target: dissociation topology

Instead of asking whether a biological and artificial system have the same "consciousness level," compare their **dissociation topology**.

For a system, define a qualitative graph whose nodes are E/A/I/S/B/R and whose edges mark empirically supported dependencies or dissociations under intervention/lesion.

Examples:

- LIS: strong B/R lesion with E/I preservation.
- no-report: R manipulation with selected perceptual representation preservation.
- R108 toy AI: R manipulation with task-core preservation.
- anesthesia: similar B/R with distinct E categories.
- blindsight: visual I partially preserved with altered E/R/metacognition.

Two systems may therefore be compared by whether similar interventions separate the same axes, even when their substrates are unrelated.

This is potentially more informative than comparing raw benchmark scores.

## 9. Direct biological-to-AI analogues now available

### Biological output loss
LIS/CMD

### Artificial analogue
Disable or alter output/report head while preserving internal task core.

### Biological report-demand manipulation
No-report paradigms

### Artificial analogue
Swap/report-head surgery with fixed core.

### Biological same outward unresponsiveness, different internal state
Anesthesia

### Artificial analogue
Construct agents with same external action distribution but different hidden organization/access state.

### Biological task ability with altered awareness
Blindsight

### Artificial analogue
Preserve task decoder while altering access/self-report path.

### Biological agency-vs-experience unity problem
Split-brain

### Artificial analogue
Scaffolded multi-module/multi-agent systems with one coordinated policy surface.

These analogies are not identities. They define matched research questions.

## 10. What biology does NOT yet establish for UCT

The reviewed cases do not establish:
- a scalar amount of experience;
- a universal brain threshold for experience;
- a unique neural seat of consciousness;
- that access consciousness equals phenomenal consciousness;
- that selfhood is required for experience;
- that intelligence and experience are monotonically coupled;
- that a given AI architecture has human-like phenomenology.

They constrain bad inferences much more strongly than they identify a final theory.

## 11. Next step

R110 should construct the **cross-substrate dissociation topology** explicitly.

For each biological case and an artificial analogue:
1. define bearer boundary;
2. define intervention/lesion;
3. mark which E/A/I/S/B/R axes are altered or preserved;
4. distinguish direct evidence from inferred E status;
5. ask whether the same dependency graph survives across substrates.

The first artificial systems should remain tiny and transparent.

A useful target is a 6x6 dependency matrix rather than a consciousness score.

If a stable topology emerges across biology and AI, it may provide a more fundamental bridge between intelligence and experience than task performance alone.

## 12. Status

R109 is a biological evidence-organization round, not a clinical claim and not a UCT validation study. Its main result is that natural biological dissociations strongly motivate keeping experience, access, intelligence, self, behavior and report as separate organizational axes.
