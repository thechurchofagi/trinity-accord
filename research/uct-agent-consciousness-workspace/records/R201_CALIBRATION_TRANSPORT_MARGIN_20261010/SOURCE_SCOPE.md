## External primary sources inspected

1. Whittlesea & Williams (2000), *JEP:LMC* 26(3), 547–565, DOI `10.1037/0278-7393.26.3.547`: familiarity can arise from discrepancy attribution rather than fluency alone.
2. Dewey & Knoblich (2014), *PLOS ONE* 9(10):e110118, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0110118`: implicit and explicit agency measures did not correlate across individuals in that study.
3. Kong et al. (2024), *JEP:HPP* 50(6), 626–635, DOI `10.1037/xhp0001204`, PMID 38635224: no voluntary-versus-passive intentional-binding difference under matched predictability; temporal prediction/sensory integration remained an alternative.
4. Seghezzi, Parés-Pujolràs & Haggard (2026), *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* 79(3), 612–629, DOI `10.1177/17470218251349521`, PMID 40454440: intentional binding changed during learning under specific feedback precision and probe relevance conditions.
5. Draxler & Kurz (2025), *Behavior Research Methods* 57:50, DOI `10.3758/s13428-024-02551-9`: primary methods work on testing measurement invariance across multiple covariates in a Rasch framework.

These sources delimit marker reliability and transport. None validates UCT's `H`, actual route use, C1 or a no-report experience attribution.
