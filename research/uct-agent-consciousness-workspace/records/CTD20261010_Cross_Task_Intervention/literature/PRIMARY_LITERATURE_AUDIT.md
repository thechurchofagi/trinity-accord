# Primary literature audit: sensorimotor interventions and UCT theory discrimination

Date: 2026-10-10. This is an internal research audit, not a claim of exhaustive priority search. Public primary sources were checked. No new human experiment was performed by this project. Web reference identifiers below are retrieval handles for the root agent to reopen before citing.

## 1. Decision-relevant conclusions

1. Intervening on sensorimotor organization and measuring touch, agency, or ownership is established experimental practice. Neither an active/passive effect nor a dissociation between agency and ownership would, by itself, be a new discovery.
2. The useful next contribution must specify a narrower testable constraint, identify which data can test it, and distinguish the intervention on a device from identification of the neural mediator or a particular information consumer.
3. UCT's universal-experience premise and organization/experience correspondence do not, without an additional observation bridge, give the direction or magnitude of a preselected ownership, agency, or psychophysical endpoint. A bridge can be tested; a failure of that bridge does not by itself refute UCT.
4. Broad comparator, predictive-processing, Bayesian-causal-inference, IIT, GNWT, and HOT labels do not constitute a set of mutually exclusive quantitative predictions for the same experiment. Comparison must be between explicit model instances and a shared measured endpoint.
5. D'Angelo et al. (2026) already tested cross-task covariance of individual tACS effects. A new analysis must not describe their evidence as group effects alone. They did not, in the examined main text and supplement, test equality of cross-task proportional effects or the exact multiplicative clock constraint below.

## 2. Six central primary sources

### S1. Efference copy and self-touch, 2020

Kilteni, K., Engeler, P., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2020). *Efference Copy Is Necessary for the Attenuation of Self-Generated Touch*. iScience, 23, 100843.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2020.100843
- Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6997587/
- Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004220300262
- Retrieval: `turn6search5`, `turn6search3`, `turn16search1` (PMC opens intermittently met an access challenge; indexed text and original abstract were available).

The study compared touch on the left index finger triggered by active right-finger movement, passive right-finger movement, or externally without movement. The active condition showed attenuation; passive touch was comparable to externally triggered touch. The authors interpreted this as evidence for an efference-copy contribution. It is a direct prior experiment for the proposed active/passive comparison. Do not describe its active/passive conditions as physically identical in every respect: the paper acknowledges differences in tactile feedback from the pressing finger. EMG can support the absence of overt muscle activation but is not direct recording of an efference-copy consumer. The appropriate endpoint is perceived touch intensity, not all agency or consciousness.

### S2. Ownership and agency dissociation, 2012

Kalckert, A., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2012). *Moving a Rubber Hand that Feels Like Your Own: A Dissociation of Ownership and Agency*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 6, 40.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040
- Primary full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00040/full
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3303087/
- Retrieval: `turn13view2`, `turn12search3`.

The authors manipulated movement synchrony, active/passive generation, and anatomical congruence. Passive movement reduced agency while ownership remained; anatomical incongruence reduced ownership while agency remained possible. Questionnaires and proprioceptive localization supplied different measurements. This establishes that the two endpoints should not be collapsed into a single self score. Their externally moved fingers were mechanically coupled, but this was not a direct selective lesion of a known neural information route. The result constrains particular accounts of bodily self attribution, not the existence of experience.

### S3. Baseline-dependent interpretations of attenuation, 2023

Job, X., & Kilteni, K. (2023). *Action does not enhance but attenuates predicted touch*. eLife, 12, e90912.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.90912
- Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10723797/
- Publisher: https://elifesciences.org/articles/90912
- Data/registrations: https://osf.io/9jkqt ; https://osf.io/hs8au ; https://osf.io/pxy87
- Retrieval: `turn10view1`, `turn11view5`.

Three preregistered experiments compared attenuation and enhancement interpretations. Including a no-action external-touch baseline was decisive: a difference between two action conditions need not imply enhancement above ordinary perception. Their results supported attenuation under the tested self-touch conditions. This is an exact precedent for guarding against an inadequate reference condition. Reproducing its PSE/JND effects is a replication. The paper is also evidence that physical contact, prediction, touch intensity, and discrimination precision must be distinguished.

### S4. Neural stimulation, ownership, simultaneity, and BCI, 2026

D'Angelo, M., Lanfranco, R. C., Chancel, M., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2026). *Parietal alpha frequency shapes own-body perception by modulating the temporal integration of bodily signals*. Nature Communications, 17, 53. Published 12 January 2026 (the DOI contains 2025).

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67657-w
- Main: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67657-w
- PDF: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67657-w.pdf
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12796323/
- Supplement: https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-025-67657-w/MediaObjects/41467_2025_67657_MOESM1_ESM.pdf
- Data: https://osf.io/ytga5 ; modeling code: https://osf.io/s5p4v
- Retrieval: `turn10view3`, `turn11view2`, `turn21view0`, `turn30view0`, `turn30view1`, `turn31view0`, `turn32view0`.

The within-person stimulation experiment used three sessions, low alpha (8 Hz), high alpha (13 Hz), and sham, and both ownership and simultaneity judgments. Thirty illusion-susceptible participants were analyzed. The authors found wider temporal binding windows under low-alpha stimulation and narrower windows under high-alpha stimulation. They compared a BCI model with varying sensory uncertainty against one with varying common-cause priors and preferred the former. The supplement also correlated individual intervention effects across tasks. These are existing results. The reanalysis must identify its additional constraint explicitly.

### S5. A precise competing agency model, 2019

Legaspi, R., & Toyoizumi, T. (2019). *A Bayesian psychophysics model of sense of agency*. Nature Communications, 10, 4250.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12170-0
- Main: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12170-0
- Accessible primary PDF: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12170-0.pdf
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6751175/
- Retrieval: `turn26view0`, `turn20search1`.

This is a computational paper explaining existing action/outcome timing results, not a new human intervention dataset. It models sensory timing uncertainty, a causal prior, and the consistency of the action-outcome interval with a causal model. It explicitly allows agency for unintended actions when the relevant cues are precise. Its proposed confidence-in-causal-estimate quantity is an additional interpretation of feeling of agency. A self-touch attenuation result cannot directly test that interpretation, because the endpoint differs. Even within the model, causal prior and causal jitter can have partly overlapping explanatory roles.

### S6. Adversarial consciousness testing, 2025

Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025). *Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness*. Nature, 642, 133-142.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1
- Primary: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137136/
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/
- Retrieval: `turn18view3`, `turn19view4`, `turn21view3`.

This collaboration preregistered experiment-specific divergent predictions and their interpretation with theory proponents. It measured neural activity while participants saw suprathreshold images of varying durations. Some registered predictions were supported and others challenged. Its methodological relevance is the advance agreement on model-to-measurement bridges. It does not provide a prediction about active/passive self-touch, a multiplicative ownership clock, or UCT; none should be imported by analogy alone.

## 3. Exact novelty boundary for the D'Angelo reanalysis

### Already done by the original authors

- Separate effects of stimulation on both tasks' temporal binding windows and sensitivities.
- Comparison of uncertainty-change and prior-change BCI models.
- Between-task TBW and sensitivity correlations within each stimulation condition.
- Individual low-alpha effect correlation across tasks: reported as the correlation of sham-minus-low TBW differences, Pearson r = .728, p < .001.
- Individual high-alpha effect correlation across tasks: high-minus-sham differences, Pearson r = .547, p = .002.
- The exact preceding definitions and tests occur in Supplementary Results, pages 16-17, Figure S7. The signs chosen for subtraction do not affect the cross-task correlation when used consistently.
- Task-related IAF differences were explored; the classic peak method produced task differences, but the FOOOF analysis did not confirm them. The authors explicitly caution about this discrepancy.

### A potentially new, more restrictive test

Define a **strict shared multiplicative clock** bridge, as a new hypothesis of this project rather than an assertion the original authors necessarily made:

`w[i,t,f] = c[i,t] * tau[i,f]`, with positive widths and task factors `c[i,t]` that do not change with stimulation.

For each person it entails:

`log(w[i,O,8] / w[i,O,13]) - log(w[i,S,8] / w[i,S,13]) = 0`.

Equivalently, `log(w[i,O,f]) - log(w[i,S,f])` is invariant across stimulation conditions. Two tasks and three stimulation conditions yield two independent constraints per participant. The 8-versus-13 contrast is one necessary constraint. Cross-task correlations of absolute differences, including S7, neither entail nor test the exact equality of proportional effects.

No test of this exact log-ratio/equality constraint was located in the main text or 24-page supplement. Targeted public searches on ownership, simultaneity, multiplicative/shared clock, proportional effects, and log effects did not reveal the same analysis; that limited search is not proof of global priority.

### Interpretation cautions

- Equal average log contrasts are weaker than equality in each person. Opposing individual departures can cancel.
- Estimated widths carry psychometric fitting error. Raw-count likelihood or a bootstrap through the fitting pipeline is preferable to treating all six widths as error-free.
- Failure to reject is not evidence of exact equality. Any equivalence margin should have an independently motivated interpretation and must not be chosen after viewing the result.
- Rejecting the strict bridge does not imply distinct neural clocks. A common latent mechanism followed by task-specific nonlinear or additive transformations can produce different proportional widths.
- The original BCI model has a nonlinear mapping from uncertainty and priors to response probabilities. A common sigma therefore does not generally imply proportional Gaussian fitted widths. Rejecting the strict clock is not automatically rejecting BCI_sigma.
- The data identify an intervention effect of stimulation condition under the design assumptions. They do not directly read out a specific efference-copy/proprioceptive consumer, and they do not identify a neural mediator from the stimulation effect alone.
- Present the study as retrospective secondary analysis with an analysis plan fixed before the new analysis, if that occurred. It is not a new preregistered human experiment.

## 4. Theory comparison matrix

| Exact theoretical object | Additional assumptions required | Falsifiable prediction for a compatible endpoint | What would count against it | Inference ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peripheral-feedback-only model for self-touch intensity | All relevant afferent distributions, stimulus histories, and report functions are matched; no motor-dependent term | Active/passive manipulation does not change the intensity-response distribution | A reliable effect after valid equivalence checks and controls | Rejects the specified input-only restriction; does not alone isolate an efference copy |
| Forward-model attenuation instance | Identified motor prediction reaches the relevant consumer before feedback; body-state inputs, precision, and learning are specified | Removing that prediction reduces its predicted attenuation under the specified task | Preserved attenuation with valid selective route removal and adequate precision, or opposite held-out prediction | Tests this attenuation mechanism, not all comparator or predictive accounts |
| Match-only agency sufficiency model | Agency endpoint is a fixed function of prediction/feedback agreement alone | Equal agreement implies equal agency predictions regardless of source | Different agency with matched agreement and validated measurement | This restricted model is already conceptually criticized in prior literature; it is not a fair stand-in for all comparator models |
| Legaspi-Toyoizumi agency model | Fixed timing-noise, prior, causal-jitter, and CCE/endpoint mapping | Cue reliability and causal prior jointly determine timing/agency; intention need not be necessary | Registered joint predictions fail on held-out matched-endpoint trials | Does not make a direct prediction about tactile PSE or total experience |
| D'Angelo BCI_sigma instance | Task-specific priors remain stable; sensory-uncertainty changes are the relevant intervention | Joint response curves under stimulation follow its specified nonlinear mapping | Better out-of-sample predictions from a competing specified model or invariant-parameter failure | Fits a computational mediator; does not uniquely establish its neural implementation |
| Strict shared multiplicative clock, introduced for this reanalysis | `w_itf = c_it tau_if`, task factor invariant across intervention; width estimation valid | Within-person cross-task log stimulation contrasts are equal | Credible departure beyond estimation uncertainty | Rejects this exact bridge only; shared neural influence can survive |
| UCT universal experience plus structural correspondence | Needs a specified physical/phenomenal equivalence relation and an independent endpoint bridge | No unique signed numeric agency/ownership/PSE prediction follows as stated | A contradiction can only target a further explicit bridge/submodel in these data | Compatibility is not comparative confirmation; subject existence is not measured |
| IIT, GNWT, HOT at broad family level | Need implementations, selected system/grain/time scale, neural variables, and measurement mapping for this task | No warranted numeric prediction for the present motor-route/width contrast | No result in these data alone adjudicates the entire family | Mark as not tested, rather than assigning invented expected effects |

## 5. Additional exact precedents and warnings

- Zaadnoordijk, L., Besold, T. R., & Hunnius, S. (2019), *A match does not make a sense: on the sufficiency of the comparator model for explaining the sense of agency*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niz006 ; https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2019/1/niz006/5488527 ; retrieval `turn12search10`. Its criticism targets the sufficiency of mere matching, not every comparator implementation. This limits priority claims for the earlier UCT method paper.
- Rohde, M., Di Luca, M., & Ernst, M. O. (2011), *The Rubber Hand Illusion: Feeling of Ownership and Proprioceptive Drift Do Not Go Hand in Hand*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0021659 ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0021659 ; retrieval `turn23view3`. It experimentally dissociates drift from ownership ratings and shows why drift is not a universal ownership readout.
- Dewey, J. A., & Knoblich, G. (2014), *Do Implicit and Explicit Measures of the Sense of Agency Measure the Same Thing?*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0110118 ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0110118 ; retrieval `turn23view2`. Their attenuation, temporal binding, and explicit agency measures did not significantly correlate. This is evidence against treating those assays as interchangeable, not proof that they can never share any cause.
- Pyasik et al. (2021), *I'm a believer: Illusory self-generated touch elicits sensory attenuation and somatosensory evoked potentials similar to the real self-touch*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117727 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33434613/ ; author-institution record https://researchportal.rma.ac.be/de/publications/im-a-believer-illusory-self-generated-touch-elicits-sensory-atten/ ; retrieval `turn18search7`, `turn18search0`. Their embodied-fake-hand condition elicited attenuation-like signatures. It warns against extending S1 to an absolute necessity claim for every paradigm; methods and endpoint differences need examination before declaring a contradiction.
- Valè et al. (2025), *Divisive attenuation based on noisy sensorimotor predictions accounts for excess variability in self-touch*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00055.2025 ; author PDF https://www.paulbays.com/pdf/ValTomGir25.pdf ; data/code https://doi.org/10.17863/CAM.113018 ; retrieval `turn18view2`. Quantitative comparisons between attenuation mechanisms and gain manipulations already exist. Do not advertise model comparison of sensory attenuation as an untouched field.

## 6. Suggested conservative contribution sentence

"We derive and retrospectively test a cross-task invariance constraint for a strictly shared multiplicative temporal mechanism, distinguishing evidence that a brain intervention affects two perceptual judgments from evidence that it affects them through the same invariant quantitative bridge."

This sentence claims a new specific test and application. It does not claim a new neural experiment, identification of efference-copy routing in humans, proof of experience, or a decisive comparison of whole consciousness theories.
