# Exact finite proof

## A4Z-T1 — coverage is compatible with nine relation families

With two named routes and two named targets, each target column may contain route 1 only, route 2 only, or both routes. Coverage excludes only the empty column. The two columns are chosen independently, so there are \(3^2=9\) covered matrices.

Exactly four matrices have one route per target: each of two targets chooses either route 1 or route 2, hence \(2^2=4\). Exactly two of these have the same singleton route in both columns. Therefore seven of the nine covered matrices lack a global single owner, and two of the four locally unique matrices still lack one global owner. Coverage and target-local uniqueness each fail to entail global ownership.

## A4Z-T2 — five structural orbits under route renaming

The nontrivial route-label swap exchanges singleton states \(\{r_1\}\) and \(\{r_2\}\) and fixes \(\{r_1,r_2\}\). Among the nine matrices, only the all-both matrix is fixed by the swap. Burnside's lemma gives

\[
\frac{9+1}{2}=5
\]

orbits. This is standard finite group-action mathematics. The project-specific use is diagnostic: a unique-owner label is presentation-dependent, coverage collapses all five orbits, and a relational family preserves them without postulating an exclusive owner.

## A4Z-C1 — what the count does not prove

The enumeration is a complete proof for the declared finite model. It does not establish that any matrix is physically installed, that \(\Pi\) belongs to a complete \(K\), that C1 is empirically true, that two distinct orbits have different named phenomenal qualities, or that an experience has one, two, or any particular number of subjects. Those require separate premises and evidence.
