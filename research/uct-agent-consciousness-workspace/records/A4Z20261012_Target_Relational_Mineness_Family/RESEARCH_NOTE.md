## 2. Three candidate bridges

The **unique-owner bridge** chooses one route label for all targets. It is too strong: A4W permits multiple overlapping routes, and a route-name permutation changes the label without changing structure.

The **coverage bridge** records only that every target has some route. It is too weak: it discards whether a target uses route 1, route 2 or both, and whether two targets share or distribute their centres.

The **target-indexed relational family** retains the nonempty set of participating routes for each target, modulo mere route renaming. It neither requires nor forbids local uniqueness. It permits joint centring, distributed centring and mixed cases in one experience-internal organization.

This is a positive explanation at the structural level, not a proof that the ordinary-language term “mine” uniquely denotes \(\Pi\). The open bridge B_pc^family still has to explain and test that interpretation against independently fixed phenomenal targets.

## 3. Exact finite result

For two routes and two targets, each target has three nonempty route sets. There are nine covered relation matrices. Four have exactly one route per target. Only two have one and the same route across both targets. Thus coverage is compatible with seven non-global-owner worlds, while even target-local uniqueness is compatible with two distributed-centre worlds.

Under route-label swap, only the all-both matrix is fixed. Burnside's lemma yields five structural orbits. Coverage collapses all five to one value; a global-owner label distinguishes presentation rather than structure; \(\Pi\) preserves the target-relative organization while quotienting mere naming.

The mathematics is elementary and not claimed as novel. The reusable increment is the exact comparison of the three bridge forms, its placement after A4Y's stop, and the typed separation of mineness, conceptual self and report.

## 5. Counterexamples and thought experiments

**Two hands, one cursor versus two cursors.** Fix two live hand-centred routes, success and the sentence “I moved it.” In one case both routes jointly enter one cursor target; in another each route enters a different cursor target. Coverage and report match; \(\Pi\) differs.

**Route-label swap.** Rename the two routes while transporting all edges. The unique-owner label changes, but the orbit of \(\Pi\) does not. Labels cannot carry the phenomenal burden.

**Memory/report swap.** Hold current \(\Pi\) fixed and replace the autobiographical sentence about who acted. Conceptual self and report change; current target participation need not.

**Abacus/calculator and human-realized agent.** Hold answer and transcript fixed while varying whether finger-, gaze-, key- or participant-centred routes jointly or distributively enter the target. Intelligence/output does not identify the relation family, and nested participants need no unique extra owner.

**Ancestor/formation and copy/rewiring.** Gradually form a second participating route without an experience-onset threshold; alternatively copy route values while removing current lineage/participation. Equal values do not install the relation, and new relations need not erase older local experience.
