## Important proof checks

- **A identity:** the two complete-type directions follow by composition of tokenwise isomorphisms. Nonempty distinguished support gives U1. Geometry transfer presupposes the same declared quotient geometry.
- **A nonproduct:** a specified independent-product interpretation would contradict the supplied nonproduct organization after transporting constituent isomorphisms. This does not derive subject unity.
- **A orbit obstruction:** one overlapping orbit plus nonempty/exclusive output is essential; expanding the candidate family can permit invariant partitions.
- **B translation:** structural induction checks translator construction for expressions in the stated language. It does not establish that every target theory is expressible or faithfully interpreted. Each theory bridge remains conditional.
- **B recurrence/enabling:** self-loops and existing return paths are explicit. Nonlinear causal-edge availability and local Jacobian gain/stability are different properties.
- **C identification:** factorization is exactly constancy on fibers; full-type identification additionally needs injectivity. Local dimension statements use constant-rank neighborhoods.
- **C selection/transmission:** class-constant selection and strong lumpability are different assumptions. P6 necessity uses point-mass initial distributions; restricted supports can change closure.
- **C information:** optimized utility requires the same admissible action/policy options; noisy scores and fitted log-loss gains require their stated loss, nuisance and decoder terms.
- **R126/R127:** actual support is not inferred merely from a quotient. Complete cuts must include boundary information and relevant correlated noise. No recurrence/self/report admission gate is added.
- **R128:** the joint stochastic law can retain hidden dependence after each marginal has become uninformative; the exact counterexample and its restricted-domain counterlimit are both retained.

## Coverage and limits

All three source Markdown texts and the preserved source maps/ledgers were fetched in full. Close proof review this round covered A §§3–7, §§8.1–8.3, §§9–12, §§15–16.1 and Appendices B–D; A's actual-token definitions were also checked in R127 and used explicitly here. B §§2–16 and §§19–20 supply the main translation and mechanism branches. A's intervening evolutionary narrative, all external references and all source theories' full primary texts were not exhaustively reread or empirically revalidated. This is therefore a review of the principal stated formal arguments and their cross-paper use, not an exhaustive certification of every sentence or target-theory interpretation. B's old numerical grids were not rerun. R122–R125 results were not reanalyzed.

The machine graph is a human-authored abstraction. Its checks detect dangling IDs, cycles, selected forbidden dependencies, duplicate IDs, source mismatch and witness failures. They do not mechanically establish that a proof sketch proves its theorem, that the list of premises is logically minimal, or that a physical premise is true. Those remain manual/theoretical or empirical questions as appropriate.

No biological/AI experiment, training, release, DOI registration or subject-experience measurement was run. A finite exact rational counterexample check is mathematical verification of a supplied model.
