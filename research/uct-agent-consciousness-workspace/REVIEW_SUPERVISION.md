### Thought experiment TE-QC-01 — component invariants under an unverified connection

Role: a minimal counterexample to a scope-transfer shortcut, using elementary invariant reasoning already known; no originality claim.

Fix two abstract components with Boolean states x_i, initial state 0, and update x_i'=u_i. Each standalone certificate admits only u_i=0 and proves S_i={0} invariant. Now preserve both components and their local update rules but connect the inputs by u_1=1-x_2 and u_2=1-x_1. From (0,0), the joint next state is (1,1).

The standalone certificates remain valid under their own input contracts. They do not certify the coupled instance because the connection violates those contracts. The lesson is to expose the interface/admitted-input premise, not to infer that composition is always impossible. Stronger composition requires compatible environments, shared-variable agreement and a proof that every admitted joint transition preserves the joint invariant. Local safety closure is still not complete organization or experiential unity.

Acceptance: write an actual interface contract or a precise countermodel, identify the omitted premise, and state exactly how this limits the next body/agent argument. Do not spend further rounds enumerating this two-bit example.
