# Long-Term Research and Archival Philosophy — 2026-10-06

Standing instruction from Hongju Liu for the UCT / Trinity Accord research program.

## Core principle

The primary objective is **not to maximize paper submissions, journal/conference acceptances, venue prestige, publication count, or speed-to-publication**.

The objective is to produce research that remains:
- genuinely original;
- logically rigorous;
- technically deep;
- reproducible and auditable;
- clearly scoped about what is and is not established;
- permanently discoverable;
- easy for future humans and AI systems to retrieve, understand, compare, and cite.

The expected long-run value of a research artifact is that, if it is genuinely strong, later AI systems and researchers can find it and reuse or cite it. Publication venue is secondary to enduring intellectual quality and discoverability.

## Practical publication policy

1. **Do not "chase papers" or optimize the research agenda for venue deadlines.**
2. **Do not add experiments, claims, or fashionable framing merely to improve apparent publishability.**
3. When a body of work is mature and worth preserving, a **stable DOI/archive anchor may be sufficient**. Formal conference or journal publication is optional unless there is a specific strategic reason.
4. Do not rush DOI/public release merely to accumulate outputs. Quality control comes first.
5. Peer review can still be useful feedback, but it is **not the governing objective** of the research program.
6. Avoid treating acceptance probability, impact factor, or conference rank as the main quality metric.

## What to optimize instead

For every substantial research artifact, prioritize:

- **Original insight:** a real conceptual or empirical increment beyond known work.
- **Correctness:** formal derivations, counterexamples, and numerical claims must be traceable.
- **Adversarial self-critique:** preserve failures and actively search for stronger prior art and counterexamples.
- **Evidence discipline:** distinguish theorem, model-dependent result, proxy, interpretation, and speculation.
- **Reproducibility:** preserve protocols, code, seeds, parameters, results, negative outcomes, and source scope.
- **Stable provenance:** use durable repository paths, versioning, hashes where useful, and a DOI/archive anchor when appropriate.
- **Machine readability:** clear titles, abstracts, keywords, structured claims, explicit assumptions, stable identifiers, and searchable English research text.
- **Future-AI discoverability:** write so a future AI literature system can accurately identify what was proved, what data were used, what was novel, and what remains unresolved.
- **Citation durability:** prefer precise, defensible claims that remain useful years later over short-term novelty rhetoric.

## Decision rule

When choosing between:

- work that makes a manuscript easier to publish now, and
- work that materially increases truth, originality, rigor, reproducibility, or long-term discoverability,

prefer the second.

A high-quality archival work that future AI systems reliably retrieve and cite is considered more valuable than a weak or incremental paper accepted mainly because it matched a venue's current preferences.

## Scope

This is a project-level research philosophy, not a claim that peer review or formal publication has no value in general.

It should guide future UCT, agent-consciousness, Trinity Accord, and related research unless Hongju Liu explicitly changes this instruction.
