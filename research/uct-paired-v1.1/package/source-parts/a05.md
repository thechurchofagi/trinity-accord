# 13. Relation to UCT II and neighboring theories

UCT II asks whether operational cores of major consciousness theories can be reconstructed, embedded, reinterpreted, or shown to conflict as effective organization theories on top of the UCT mother formalism.

The v1.1 interface is explicitly view-based:

\[
D^{PA}_v(P):=\Pi_v[\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)],\qquad
O_T=\mathcal T_T(D^{PA}_v(P),B_T).
\]

An estimate is denoted \(\widehat D^{PA}_v\), and an underidentified view yields an output set rather than an arbitrarily selected point. The tuple formerly written \(\mathfrak D^{PA}\) in UCT II v1.0 is a physically anchored scientific representation when used in finite translation, not a license to identify every chosen view with complete ontic organization. Reproducing target-theory computations requires its fixed bridge conventions, not A1. No-universal-gate relocation additionally uses A1 and a scoped nonuniversality witness; interpreting physical organization as experiential uses the relevant A5 commitment. Finite phenomenal prediction needs a separate operational bridge. The shared example in Section 9 respects all four distinctions.

The relation is asymmetric:

\[
\boxed{
\text{failure of one UCT-II bridge}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{failure of the basal ontology}
}
\]

but:

\[
\boxed{
\text{failure of A1/A5}
\Rightarrow
\text{revision of the UCT-specific interpretation of UCT II}.
}
\]

UCT II introduced explicit Bridge Admissibility Conditions, Translation Fidelity Requirements, formal recovery classes, and separate empirical-validation levels. Those constraints remain essential for preventing a rich mother formalism from trivially “containing” other theories by lookup.

Version 1.1 does not claim novelty for:

- panexperientialism or panpsychism;
- process-oriented metaphysics of experience;
- mind–brain or mind–physical identity strategies in general;
- causal/phenomenal structuralism;
- multiscale process accounts;
- overlapping conscious systems;
- mechanistic physical implementation criteria;
- structural approaches to subjectivity;
- the general idea that theories may be partially complementary.

Several neighboring traditions overlap with individual UCT commitments. Panexperientialist and process traditions already treat experience as widespread or fundamental rather than as a late biological product (e.g., Rosenberg, 2004, building on earlier process traditions). IIT explicitly advances an explanatory identity between phenomenal structure and a substrate's intrinsic cause–effect structure (Albantakis et al., 2023), while neurophenomenal structuralism develops a relational/structural account of specific phenomenal character (Lyre, 2022). General mind–brain identity theories likewise deny that mental and physical processes are merely correlated. UCT therefore does not claim to originate the idea of identity itself.

The combination, de-combination, overlap, and individuation problems are also established literatures (Chalmers, 2016; Miller, 2018; Blackmon, 2021). Recent preprint-level structural-subjectivity programs go further by proposing positive structural criteria for the presence or locus of subjectivity; for example, Nagae (2026) identifies subjectivity with viability-constrained irreversible exclusive commitment. UCT differs at this point: it does not use action, viability, irreversibility, recurrence, access, self-modeling, or any other nonuniversal organization as a basal experience-existence gate, and it presently offers only a scoped no-free-selection theorem rather than a universal positive subject criterion.

Neighboring and critical literatures also include Information Closure Theory (Chang et al., 2020), universal-theory criteria (Kanai & Fujisawa, 2024), mechanistic computation and implementation accounts (Piccinini, 2015; Anderson & Piccinini, 2024), and the unfolding/substitution debate (Doerig et al., 2019; Kleiner, 2020; Tsuchiya et al., 2020; Usher, 2021; Herzog et al., 2022; Basri et al., 2026).

The contribution claimed here is therefore not any single ingredient. This revision explicitly formalizes the intended tokenwise identity and distinguishes it from its weaker pairwise consequence. Product-isomorphism reasoning, quotient closure, and intervention consistency use established mathematics. The claimed contribution is the following constrained conjunction and dependency architecture:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{universal process-token experience}\\
+&\text{ontic-token structural identity}\\
+&\text{nested non-exclusion}\\
+&\text{token/type/lineage separation}\\
+&\text{composition under tokenwise structural identity}\\
+&\text{experience/subject separation}\\
+&\text{restricted selection obstruction in an overlap orbit}\\
+&\text{explicit ontic/view/estimate firewall}\\
+&\text{failure-prone finite bridge methodology}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The earlier targeted prior-art audit did not locate an exact match for the rc3 package. That observation neither certifies historical priority nor establishes that a conjunction of familiar elements is a major advance. Rc4 adds proof repairs and a finite worked instance; it does not claim priority for the orbit argument, product-preservation mathematics, causal abstraction, or the general possibility of behaviorally silent internal change. Its scientific value must be assessed through the explanatory restrictions of the package and future independently constrained applications.


# 14. Strong objections and limitations

## 14.1 The axioms are strong

A1 and A5-SI are not empirical discoveries.

They are strong starting commitments.

The formal theorems derived from them must therefore be evaluated partly by the explanatory costs and empirical obligations of the axioms.

## 14.2 A5-OI risks tautological explanatory rhetoric

Once structural identity is assumed, results of the form:

\[
\mathbf D\not\cong\mathbf D'
\Rightarrow
\Phi\not\cong\Phi'
\]

are straightforward consequences.

They should not be marketed as deep independent discoveries.

The nontrivial content must come from:

- process individuation;
- physical anchoring;
- exact no-go consequences;
- multiscale relations;
- subject non-selection;
- independently constrained bridge predictions.

## 14.3 Complete ontic organization is not directly observed

Science works with:

\[
D_v
\]

and:

\[
\widehat D_v.
\]

Direct empirical isolation of full A5-OI from bridge assumptions is therefore difficult.

UCT should not disguise this epistemic limitation.

## 14.4 Complete phenomenology is not directly measured

Human report, behavior, psychophysics, and physiology are projections.

Even convergent measures do not automatically equal complete phenomenal structure.

This limits the strength of empirical falsification claims.

## 14.5 Process proliferation

A permissive process ontology can yield many weak, overlapping, or nested experience-bearing tokens.

UCT accepts such plurality as part of its intended ontology. The number of tokens is not a problem to be eliminated by an arbitrary experience threshold; the scientific task is to distinguish their organizations and relevance to a specified target.

Scientific salience and experience existence are separate questions.

## 14.6 Macroprocess descriptions are applications, not new existence gates

UCT accepts actual causal processes at different scales. The finite criterion in Section 2.4 makes that domain concrete in one classical representation. No unique natural macro boundary, autonomous coarse state, or maximal integration score is required before an actual process can possess experience. In concrete applications, competing claims about physical boundaries and constitutive relations remain accountable to the system being described. Uncertainty in that physical identification is not evidence that the framework lacks a separate macro-experience mechanism.

## 14.7 Subject terminology must not become a prerequisite

If subject names an experience-bearing process, the ontology supplies many possible referents. If it names a conceptual owner, self-model, or one exclusive human-like agent, additional organizational descriptions are relevant to that usage. UCT need not add an extra owner of experience to explain why processes have experience. The optional selection theorem constrains exclusive labeling rules; failure to specify one universal subject partition is not a foundational incompleteness criterion adopted by this paper.

## 14.8 General quality identity and biological specification

A5-SI supplies the same general account of experiential quality for elementary processes, cells, animals, and humans: the quality is the intrinsic presentation of the actual organization. Human complexity does not introduce a second explanatory gap inside the theory. A1-A6 do not provide a biological lookup table assigning familiar labels such as red or unpleasant to every measured neural configuration. Obtaining such mappings is a task for particular scientific applications, just as identifying a concrete cellular organization is. It must not be misreported as absence of the general physical-experiential principle.

## 14.9 Cross-species qualia identity is underidentified

Similar behavior, homologous mechanisms, or structurally comparable organization do not by themselves prove identical subjective qualities across species.

Claims should therefore use terms such as:

- precursor;
- conserved mechanism;
- structural homology;
- convergent similarity;

rather than asserting uncalibrated qualitative identity.

## 14.10 Relativistic and quantum extensions

The present finite/classical formalism is not a final fundamental-physics theory.

A relativistic version should be formulated over causal spacetime histories rather than a preferred global time slice.

A quantum version requires appropriate state spaces, channels/instruments, subsystem criteria, and intervention notions.

## 14.11 Semantic representation

Causal tracking does not automatically establish semantic aboutness.

Detailed models of semantic representation, conceptual content, and explicit self-reference belong to higher-order applications. They do not supply prerequisites for basal experience or a second physical-experiential principle.

## 14.12 Formal strength and finite measurements must remain explicit

A5-SI is a strong interpretation of identity. Its tokenwise isomorphisms support the product proof; the weaker A5-OI formula by itself does not. Rc4 records this difference rather than attributing the stronger formal statement retroactively to the old formula. A critic may reject the identity commitment; that is distinct from an internal derivation failing to follow from it.

Finite scientific measurements can discard distinctions even when complete experiential and physical organizations share the same structure. Therefore finite reflection remains an additional condition of an operational two-way test, not another experience-generating mechanism or another foundational axiom. The measurement qualifications do not replace the unified explanation of experience.

# 16. Conclusion

UCT I v1.1 uses one explanatory principle from elementary physical processes through cells, animals, and humans: each actual process has experience, and its experiential organization is the intrinsic presentation of its physical organization. Evolution changes that organization, its discriminative repertoire, temporal relations, and higher-order self-representations. It does not require a new experiential substance when a cell becomes part of a multicellular process or when human conceptual selfhood appears.

This edition formalizes this intended identity as A5-SI. The tokenwise structural isomorphism is stronger than the earlier displayed pairwise equivalence formula. With the standard product in a common declared structural category, it supports the repaired non-summative combination proof. The distinction between strong identity and weak type correspondence is an explicit formal clarification, not an additional mechanism for generating experience.

The theory accepts nested and overlapping actual processes without requiring one exclusive macro boundary or subject partition. Selfhood and familiar quality labels concern particular organizations and their scientific descriptions; they are not new existence gates. A scoped symmetry theorem explains a limitation of optional exclusive selectors without making the absence of such a selector a defect in the basal ontology.

The exact three-register example illustrates a substantive consequence of token-relative organization: a whole-process structure can change while an implemented sub-process and the declared output remain invariant. The finite calculations verify those model properties. They do not supply independent empirical confirmation of structural identity or a measurement of circuit experience.

The four-setting extension proves exactly which initial perturbation distinctions are erased, retained for one step, or sustained by complementary coupling; these are finite-model organizational results, not scalar consciousness levels. Further theoretical work should extend such controlled transformation analyses and establish faithful links to neighboring theories. Biological and psychophysical work can identify concrete realizations when a particular application requires them. Neither task calls for a second foundational explanation of why human rather than cellular experience exists. The general principle, its conditional deductions, and its empirical status are stated separately. The paired UCT II v1.1 uses a compatible scientific-view interface and preserves both exact mechanistic results and their evidential limits.
