# 8. From physical process to conceptual self: a structural-evolutionary synthesis

This section is explanatory rather than foundational.

It asks:

> If A1 and the intended identity A5-SI are granted, can one describe a continuous and branching path from elementary physical processes to human conceptual selfhood without introducing successive new phenomenal substances?

UCT can represent such a path coherently as a structural synthesis. This does not establish that the historical transitions occurred in exactly the sequence listed below, nor does it provide independent evidence for A1 or A5-SI. The relevant theoretical claim is narrower: no additional phenomenal substance is required by the UCT formalism when these organizational transformations are described.

Evolution supplies the organizing rationale for continuity across these cases. UCT takes universal experience and structural identity as its starting commitments; it does not repeatedly reopen their derivation inside each biological application. Comparative evolutionary findings illustrate changes of organization. Accepting biological evolution by itself need not force another theorist to adopt UCT's axioms; that external disagreement is distinct from whether UCT's internal explanations consistently use them.

## 8.1 Elementary physical processes

At the most elementary level considered by UCT, an actual interaction process possesses whatever relational structure is physically instantiated.

UCT does not require:

- a nervous system;
- a sensor;
- a memory;
- a self-model;
- a report channel.

Under A1, the experience-existence predicate is already nonzero. A5-SI identifies this process's experiential organization with its actual organization. The pairwise A5-OI formula is a consequence of that stronger statement.

UCT therefore refuses to project human terms such as “seeing,” “wanting,” or “suffering” onto elementary interactions. The primitive claim is structural, not anthropomorphic.

## 8.2 From stable chemistry to self-maintaining organization

The transition from nonliving chemistry to the first cells is an origin-of-life problem, not a solved Darwinian lineage. UCT does not claim to reconstruct the historical path.

The theoretical point is narrower. If physical processes develop persistent boundaries, recurrent chemical organization, and endogenous regulation, then new relations appear inside the process organization:

\[
\text{internal state}
\leftrightarrow
\text{boundary}
\leftrightarrow
\text{environmental perturbation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{regulatory response}.
\]

These relations are not the origin of experience under A1. They are transformations of experiential organization.

## 8.3 Single-cell and non-neural learning: temporal depth

A decisive organizational transition occurs when past events leave present physical traces that modify current response:

\[
History
\to
m_t
\to
Response_t.
\]

This is the minimal form of temporal depth required by UCT's memory analysis.

The framework does not require memory to create experience. Memory changes current organization.

Non-neural learning illustrates the physical possibility of history-dependent behavior without a brain. Habituation has been experimentally demonstrated in the slime mould *Physarum polycephalum* (Boisseau, Vogel, & Dussutour, 2016). Such evidence does not establish phenomenology in the organism; it demonstrates that learning-like temporal organization need not await centralized nervous systems.

If all physical consequences of a history are erased, No Ghost History predicts no remaining current phenomenal-type difference attributable solely to the past.

## 8.4 Multicellularity: from juxtaposition to organism-scale relations

Consider independent cells:

\[
C_1,\ldots,C_n.
\]

If their joint organization remains a product, there is no new irreducible cross-cell relation merely because the cells are spatially adjacent.

If, however, real chemical, electrical, mechanical, developmental, or other causal relations generate an actual multicellular process \(M\) with:

\[
\mathbf D(M)
\not\cong
\bigotimes_i
\mathbf D(C_i),
\]

then the structure-for-structure identity A5-SI assigns \(M\) an experiential organization that is nonproduct relative to that constituent comparison, by Theorem 6.3. The multicellular case uses the same identity principle as the cellular case; no new experiential substance or threshold is introduced.

The significance of multicellularity in UCT is therefore not cell count. It is the formation of new actual macroprocess organization.

No privileged signaling medium is required. Mechanical, chemical, electrical, and neural relations can all contribute when they are physically constitutive.

## 8.5 Nervous systems: routing and specialization, not an experience switch

The evolution of neurons adds high-speed, long-range, selectively routed, and plastic coordination.

Under UCT:

\[
\boxed{
\text{neuron}
\neq
\text{experience ignition}.
}
\]

Neurons transform organization. They can increase spatial discrimination, routing, temporal coordination, learning capacity, and context sensitivity.

Associative learning in the box jellyfish *Tripedalia cystophora*, a cnidarian without a vertebrate-style centralized brain, provides an empirical illustration that nontrivial learning need not wait for vertebrate-like centralization (Bielecki et al., 2023).

This finding is not evidence for UCT's A1. It illustrates the danger of equating a particular level of centralized neural architecture with the first appearance of complex learning.

## 8.6 Damage regulation, nociception, and affect

The pain lineage is particularly useful because it exposes the mistake of projecting a fully human quale backward.

Comparative biology indicates that damage detection and adaptive cellular stress responses predate nervous systems, while specialized nociceptors, sensitization, central aversive states, and human pain involve progressively richer mechanisms (Walters, 2025).

A schematic structural trajectory proceeds through:

1. cellular damage regulation;
2. multicellular injury coordination;
3. specialized nociception and sensitization;
4. aversive learning and multidimensional affective pain;
5. human suffering.

UCT does not identify the first term with human pain.

It instead asks which physical relations constituting later pain are already present in simpler systems.

Human pain also illustrates why one scalar valence variable is inadequate. Clinical and conceptual literatures distinguish sensory-discriminative from affective-motivational dimensions of pain, and pain-asymbolia cases have been interpreted as evidence for partial dissociation between them (Auvray, Myin, & Spence, 2010). That interpretation is not uncontested: recent philosophical reviews emphasize that asymbolia may reflect strongly reduced rather than literally absent negative affect, and that the taxonomy of the residual state remains debated (Coninx, 2024). The conservative UCT conclusion is therefore:

**Nociceptive discrimination, unpleasantness, and suffering should not be collapsed into one primitive scalar variable.**

Under A5-SI, unpleasantness belongs to the intrinsic organization of the relevant actual process, just as any cellular quality does. Identifying which concrete human mechanisms instantiate a specified pain experience is an application-level biological question; it does not require another foundational account of why human experience exists.


### One explanatory principle across experiential qualities

Let \(P_c\) be a cellular process and \(P_h\) a human process. UCT applies the same relation \(\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)\) in each appropriately declared comparison. The organizations differ in their realized distinctions and relations; the explanatory principle does not change. Human red, pain, and bodily ownership can occupy richly differentiated positions within a process's experiential organization without requiring that a cell possess those same human discriminations or linguistic labels.

In a continuously comparable transformation family, A2 supplies experiential continuity and A5 supplies structural identity. Added, removed, or reorganized causal relations correspond to changes in the intrinsic organization itself. The theory need not posit a point where an otherwise nonexperiential organization suddenly acquires a new phenomenal substance. Nor does it follow that evolution must monotonically increase one scalar amount of experience.

At this explanatory level, asking for a second mechanism connecting a fully specified actual organization to its experience would reopen what A5 fixes as the identity principle. A separate question is which actual organization realizes a particular familiar human experience. Investigating that empirical instance can refine an application without constituting a missing axiom of the general theory.

## 8.7 Association, relational abstraction, and reasoning

Cognitive evolution can likewise be represented as transformations of organization rather than the sudden arrival of a new phenomenal substance.

A simplified comparative sequence runs from state-dependent response through habituation and sensitization, association, relational abstraction, relations over relations, and symbolic or compositional reasoning.

The sequence is not a literal phylogenetic ladder.

Honeybees can learn and transfer same/different relational rules to novel stimuli (Giurfa et al., 2001), supporting the narrower claim that transferable relational discrimination need not require a human-sized brain.

Cuttlefish have shown what-where-when behavioral organization meeting standard criteria for episodic-like memory (Jozet-Alves, Bertin, & Clayton, 2013), and later work demonstrated substantial delayed control in a delay-of-gratification task (Schnell et al., 2021).

These examples support the narrower conclusion that component capacities relevant to human cognition occur in distant lineages and can be realized by substantially different nervous-system architectures. They do not establish a single linear ancestry of cognition, nor do they demonstrate that nonhuman animals execute human formal logic or possess phenomenology identical to human cognition.

## 8.8 Physical witness and semantic inflation

A danger in comparative cognition is to redescribe simple behavior with sophisticated human concepts.

UCT therefore introduces a Physical Witness Criterion:

> A claimed higher-order relation belongs to the actual constitutive organization only if the physical process itself distinguishes that relational configuration under controlled, physically admissible perturbation.

For example, “same/different” should not be attributed merely because an analyst can describe stimuli relationally. Transfer to novel physical stimuli provides stronger evidence that the system is sensitive to an invariant relation, though behavioral transfer alone still need not reveal the complete ontic mechanism.

The criterion blocks:

\[
\text{observer's description}
\Rightarrow
\text{invented phenomenal dimension}.
\]

## 8.9 Body-centered organization

With increasingly coordinated sensorimotor organization, a system can physically encode relations involving:

- internal/external boundary;
- body position;
- sensor position;
- action state;
- consequences of self-generated action;
- persistent internal variables.

Write this only as a projection of already existing process organization:

\[
\mathcal S(M)
=
\Pi_{sys}
[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)
].
\]

This is system-relative organization, not a new observer inside the system.

It can support increasingly rich body-centered and agency-related experience without being the origin of experience itself.

## 8.10 Self-model

A self-model is a high-level physical organization in which some internal states track, predict, compress, or regulate aspects of the system's own organization across time.

Possible ingredients include:

- body state;
- action consequences;
- memory;
- prediction;
- social information;
- control;
- metacognitive access.

UCT does not define the self-model as the subject, and it does not make self-modeling necessary for basal experience.

## 8.11 The conceptual “I”

Human conceptual selfhood adds language, autobiographical structure, explicit temporal narration, symbolic categorization, social cognition, metacognition, and culturally transmitted conceptual tools.

A system can then instantiate structures corresponding to propositions such as:

> I am the one undergoing this experience.

or:

> I remember being the same individual who experienced that event.

Within UCT, this conceptual “I” is a late, high-dimensional organization inside experience, not the entity that creates experience.

The proposed structural sequence is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{elementary actual physical process}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{persistent / self-maintaining organization}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{history-dependent regulation}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{multicellular macroprocess relations}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{specialized neural routing}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{multimodal memory / prediction / affect}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{body-centered control organization}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{self-model}\\
\downarrow\\
\text{conceptual autobiographical “I”}
\end{array}
}
\]

The arrows mean structural transformation, not one linear species ancestry.

The comparative cases in this section are therefore evidential anchors for organizational possibilities and intermediate capacities. They are not empirical confirmation of universal experience, structural identity, or cross-species quale identity.

## 8.12 No universal consciousness ladder

Evolution can:

- add relations;
- delete relations;
- reweight couplings;
- prune structure;
- specialize;
- compress irrelevant details;
- introduce new temporal dependencies;
- bifurcate dynamically;
- lose capacities;
- converge on similar functions by different architectures.

Therefore UCT rejects:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dC}{dt}>0
}
\]

for a universal scalar amount of consciousness.

The more general representation is:

\[
\boxed{
\lambda
\mapsto
\mathbf D^{ontic}_\lambda(P_\lambda)
\mapsto
\Phi_\lambda(P_\lambda).
}
\]

Evolution is a path through a branching space of experiential organization, not a staircase from unconscious matter to conscious humans.
