# 4. Foundational commitments

## A1 — Universal Experience

For every actual valid process token:

\[
\boxed{
\forall P\in\mathcal P,
\qquad
\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing.
}
\]

A1 is not derived from evolution, neuroscience, intelligence, report, complexity, or biology.

The weaker equivalence formulas A5-W and A5-OI do not themselves establish nonemptiness for every actual token. Under a strong structure-for-structure representation, nonemptiness may also follow mathematically from nonempty physical structure. A1 is retained to state the intended ontological commitment and to prevent actual-process definitions from quietly excluding simple cases.

## A2 — Scoped Structural Continuity

For an explicitly declared continuously comparable family \(P(\lambda)\), if the relevant physical organization varies continuously in a specified structural topology, then experiential organization varies continuously:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D(P_\lambda)\text{ continuous}
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P_\lambda)\text{ continuous}.
}
\]

A2 is a schema, not a universal scalar continuity law. Quantitative use requires a declared metric/topology. A derived categorical observable can undergo a threshold transition even when underlying structure varies continuously.

## A3 — Persistence Under Embedding

If an actual process \(P\) remains physically realized while embedded in a larger actual process \(Q\), embedding alone does not terminate \(P\):

\[
\boxed{
P\triangleleft Q
\not\Rightarrow
\text{cessation of }P.
}
\]

The distinctive content of A3 is process ontological. Once the lower token remains actual, A1 supplies nonempty lower-scale experience.

A3 does not imply that a process continues after its constitutive dynamics cease.

## A4 — Selfhood Non-Prerequisite

Human-like conceptual selfhood is not required for basal experience:

\[
\boxed{
\neg Self_I(P)
\not\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)=\varnothing.
}
\]

The positive mechanisms by which body models, agency, autobiographical memory, metacognition, and a conceptual “I” form are not axioms. They belong to higher-order application/bridge layers.

## A5 - Experience-Structure Identity

UCT's intended claim is that experience is the intrinsic presentation of an actual physical process's own organization. Cellular and human experience instantiate this same identity principle; richer organization does not require a second kind of experiential ingredient.

### A5-SI - explicit structure-for-structure identity

Fix a common structural signature \(\mathcal K\) for the comparison: the relevant state distinctions, causal and temporal relations, and constituent organization must be specified. Write \(\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)\) and \(\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\) for the complete physical and experiential organization of the same actual token in this signature. The strong intended identity is expressed by

\[
h_P:\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)
\;\cong\;\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P).
\]

The two expressions describe the same organization in physical and experiential terms. The isomorphism is a formal expression of that commitment, not an empirically fitted correspondence, an extra causal force, or an experience-generating mechanism. Structural relations must be fixed by the declared signature; they cannot be freely relabeled to fit a desired phenomenal result. When an independent product is used, it is the ordinary product in the common comparison category on both sides. Section 6 makes this typing explicit.

**Version boundary.** This is stronger than the pairwise equivalence formula displayed in rc3. The rc4 development revision adopted it to formalize the intended identity interpretation; it does not claim to have deduced it from the weaker formula or to have established it empirically. The published v1.0 and the original rc3 remain unchanged.

### A5-OI - pairwise type equivalence as a consequence

Within that common signature, composing the tokenwise isomorphisms gives

\[
\Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q)
\iff
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q).
\]

The subscripts are suppressed when the scope is clear. This consequence preserves and reflects equivalence between actual token types, but if written alone it is weaker than A5-SI: an arbitrary permutation of type labels can satisfy it without preserving each token's organization. Section 6 supplies an explicit counterexample to that weaker reading.

### A5-W - one-way phenomenal completeness

The one-way consequence inherited from the explicitly stated v1.0 formula is

\[
\mathbf D(P)\cong\mathbf D(Q)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q).
\]

It excludes a freely varying hidden phenomenal variable when complete constitutive physical organization is fixed. No product conclusion is attributed to this one-way formula alone. In the rest of the paper, A5 denotes the intended strong identity, with A5-W or A5-OI cited when a weaker consequence already suffices.

## A6 — Token / Type / Lineage Distinction

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D(P)\cong\mathbf D(Q)
\not\Rightarrow
P=Q.
}
\]

Structural equivalence does not imply numerical identity.


# 5. Formal consequences

## 5.1 Organization-Gate Impossibility

Define an experience-existence predicate:

\[
E(P)=\mathbf 1[\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing].
\]

By A1:

\[
E(P)=1
\qquad
\forall P\in\mathcal P.
\]

Let \(F(P)\) be any organization descriptor and \(S\) a threshold/target region. If:

\[
\exists Q\in\mathcal P:
F(Q)\notin S,
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf 1[F(P)\in S]
\not\equiv
E(P).
}
\]

Hence, within UCT, no nonuniversal threshold of:

- integration;
- recurrence;
- global availability;
- explicit memory;
- higher-order representation;
- predictive modeling;
- autonomy;
- entropy;
- generic biological complexity;

can equal the universal experience-existence predicate.

This theorem does not make those mechanisms irrelevant. It relocates them from experience existence to experience organization.

## 5.2 No Hidden Quale

From A5-W:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q)
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)\cong
\Phi(Q).
\]

UCT therefore rejects an additional hidden phenomenal degree of freedom that varies independently while complete token-relative physical organization is fixed.

Within the intended structuralist interpretation, words such as “red” and “pain” label experiential organizations rather than additional freely varying quale atoms. A5-W alone does not determine the physical organization corresponding to either label or establish the complete structuralist interpretation.

## 5.3 Probe Invariance

Let \(\mathcal I^{exp}\) denote an experimenter-selected probe family. Merely changing this choice, without changing the complete constitutive physical organization, cannot alter phenomenology under A5-W:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I^{exp}_1&\ne\mathcal I^{exp}_2,\\
\mathbf D^{ontic}_1(P)&\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_2(P)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Phi_1(P)\cong\Phi_2(P).
\end{aligned}
\]

The completeness qualifier matters. Holding only one causal graph or one response descriptor fixed is insufficient if other constitutive state distinctions change. A probe that physically intervenes on the process can change its experience by changing its organization; the invariant claim concerns a merely external change of description or choice.


## 5.4 No Future Contamination

If:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(Q),
\]

then a future external divergence that is not physically instantiated at time \(t\) cannot imply a present phenomenal difference:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(P)\cong\Phi_t(Q).
}
\]

Present causal dispositions are part of the present organization; actual future facts are not.

## 5.5 Causally Screened History / No Ghost History

Suppose:

\[
History(P)\neq History(Q)
\]

but all current constitutive physical consequences of those histories have converged:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(Q).
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(P)\cong\Phi_t(Q).
}
\]

A6 still permits the tokens to remain numerically distinct because lineage can differ even when current structural type is the same.

## 5.6 External reward-label non-identity

Changing an external reward sign, semantic label, or experimenter convention without changing intrinsic constitutive organization cannot by itself alter phenomenology under A5-W.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reward sign}
\neq
\text{phenomenal valence sign}.
}
\]


# 6. Factorization, macroprocesses, and combination

## 6.1 Independent products and comparison objects

Fix structural signatures and a constituent split before comparing a whole with its parts. The physical product \(X_1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_n\) represents independent juxtaposition relative to that split and a declared boundary convention. In a transition model, component updates must not depend on the other component states once the declared boundary is fixed. A shared drive can create statistical correlations without creating the internal causal dependence at issue. Product factorization is consequently not synonymous with unconditional statistical independence.

For this product comparison, constituents occupy nonoverlapping slots of the specified physical split. A product of overlapping actual tokens would require a separately justified comparison construction; general gluing of overlapping processes is not established here. An abstract product is initially a **comparison object**. It need not itself be an actual causally continuous token; A1 is not automatically applied to it. Unrestricted mixing of physically distinct intervention ports must not be mistaken for a mere relabeling that preserves a particular constituent split. The phenomenal product must also be specified as the intended independent juxtaposition, not retrospectively defined to make a desired conclusion true.

## 6.2 Why A5-OI alone is insufficient

A5-OI constrains equivalence of structural types. It does not state how products are transported. The distinction is visible in a finite-set countermodel. Take isomorphism classes indexed by positive cardinality and the bijection

\[
f(6)=7,\qquad f(7)=6,\qquad f(n)=n\quad(n\ne6,7).
\]

Let physical constituent types have sizes 2 and 3, and a whole type have size 7. Map phenomenal types using \(f\). The equivalence biconditional holds because \(f\) is injective. Nevertheless,

\[
7\ne2\cdot3,\qquad f(7)=6=f(2)\cdot f(3).
\]

Thus a nonproduct physical type can correspond to the product of the constituent phenomenal types while the bare A5-OI equivalence formula is satisfied. This is an abstract countermodel to an implication of the formula, not a claim that unstructured finite sets are adequate physical or phenomenal models. It pinpoints the missing algebraic commitment. Additional physical restrictions may exclude it, but those restrictions must be stated.

## 6.3 Non-summative combination from the intended identity

Use the strong tokenwise identity A5-SI in a common comparison category with the finite products needed for the argument. Independent juxtaposition on each side is represented by this same standard product. Ordinary products preserve isomorphisms: if each \(h_i:X_i\cong Y_i\), then

\[
\bigotimes_i h_i:\bigotimes_i X_i\cong\bigotimes_i Y_i.
\]

This is the relevant mathematical consequence of expressing the same constituent organizations in two descriptions. It is not another physical mechanism of experiential combination. The use of a common signature and product must be declared; the weak type-equivalence formula alone cannot supply it.

**Theorem 6.3 (non-summative combination in the declared product comparison).** Let \(M\) be an actual whole process and \(P_i\) its actual constituents in the nonoverlapping slots of the specified comparison. If

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)\not\cong
\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

then A5-SI implies

\[
\Phi(M)\not\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i).
\]

**Proof.** Let \(h_M\) and \(h_i\) be the tokenwise isomorphisms supplied by A5-SI. If \(\Phi(M)\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)\), compose that isomorphism with \(h_M\) and the inverse of the product of the \(h_i\). This yields

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)
\cong\Phi(M)
\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)
\cong\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

contradicting the physical premise. The abstract comparison product need not be an actual token, so no extension of A1 to imaginary processes is used. \(\square\)

The result says that actual additional constitutive relations are not erased in the experiential description. It does not add together subjective substances. Its nonproduct conclusion is relative to the specified constituent product, not every possible decomposition. Continuing constituent processes remain experience-bearing by A1 and A3. Neither the theorem nor the theory requires a unique numerical subject count before admitting the whole's experiential organization.

If identity is deliberately weakened back to the pairwise A5-OI formula alone, the countermodel in Section 6.2 still applies and this proof is unavailable. The repair therefore makes the intended premise explicit rather than silently inferring a stronger premise from a weaker formula.

## 6.4 Macro invariance with micro change

Suppose actual macroprocess tokens obey

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_A)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_B),
\]

while an independently identified pair of lower-scale actual tokens obeys

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^A)\not\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^B).
\]

A5-W suffices for \(\Phi(M_A)\cong\Phi(M_B)\). The converse component of A5-OI additionally yields \(\Phi(p^A)\not\cong\Phi(p^B)\). Only the stated pairwise consequences are needed for these claims; the product construction is not involved.

The result is conditional: it does not show that any specified material replacement actually preserves complete macro organization. Nor can one establish it merely by observing the same coarse statistic of the same token. The macro token must have an independently supported realization and boundary; lower-scale physical differences must not be constitutive differences of that macro token. Appendix B demonstrates the corresponding distinction between a fixed whole-process token and an invariant implemented sub-process in an idealized finite model. It is not a general biological replacement theorem.


# 7. Subject selection under symmetry: an orbit obstruction

UCT distinguishes experience, structural unity, macroprocess tokenhood, subjecthood, and self-modeling. The result below concerns a proposed selection rule. It neither derives subjecthood from A1 nor treats every experience-bearing process as one canonical subject.

## 7.1 Equivariant selection is a union of candidate orbits

Let \(W\) be a physical structure and \(\Gamma\) a group of its structure-preserving automorphisms. Let \(\mathcal C(W)\) be a candidate family closed under this group. A deterministic selector returns \(S(W)\subseteq\mathcal C(W)\) and is equivariant:

\[
S(gW)=gS(W).
\]

Since \(gW=W\) for \(g\in\Gamma\), the selected family is invariant under \(\Gamma\).

**Orbit lemma.** If a candidate \(P\) is selected, its entire orbit \(\Gamma P\) is selected.

**Proof.** For each \(g\in\Gamma\), invariance gives \(gP\in gS(W)=S(W)\). Thus a selected family is a union of full orbits. \(\square\)

**Corollary 7.1 (restricted exclusive-selection obstruction).** Suppose the admissible candidates are one orbit \(O\) containing an overlapping pair. No deterministic equivariant selector can return a nonempty, pairwise disjoint subfamily of \(O\).

**Proof.** A nonempty selection contains one candidate and hence all of \(O\). It then contains the overlapping pair, contrary to pairwise disjointness. \(\square\)

This corollary requires the stated restriction of the candidate family and a nonempty-output requirement. These are selection requirements, not consequences of A1-A6.

## 7.2 The three-part example and its counterlimits

For a reflection-symmetric world with parts \(a,b,c\), let the reflection exchange \(a\) and \(c\), and let the candidate orbit be

\[
O=\bigl\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\}\bigr\}.
\]

The two invariant selected subfamilies are the empty family and the whole orbit. The first violates nonemptiness; the second violates exclusivity. An unbiased random rule could choose either member with equal probability, but it would not determine one unique subject from \(W\); a realized random seed would add selection data.

The theorem does **not** rule out the invariant partitions

\[
\bigl\{\{a,b,c\}\bigr\},\qquad
\bigl\{\{a\},\{b\},\{c\}\bigr\}.
\]

If a positive subject theory admits one of these, it needs its own physical justification. Neither is excluded by the orbit lemma. The correct conclusion is a scoped obstruction to selecting nonempty exclusive candidates from an overlapping symmetry orbit, not a general theorem that intrinsic structure cannot determine any unique partition. A universal positive subject criterion is not imposed by UCT. Any additional exclusive selector must justify its own scope and purpose.
