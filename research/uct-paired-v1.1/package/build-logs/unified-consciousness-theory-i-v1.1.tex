\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}}

Unified Consciousness Theory (UCT) adopts nonempty experience for every
actual valid physical process token and treats that experience as the
intrinsic presentation of the process's own organization. A cell and a
human are interpreted by the same principle. Evolutionary change
concerns the relations, discriminations, temporal organization, and
self-representations instantiated by processes, rather than the arrival
of a new experiential substance at a privileged biological stage.

This revision makes the intended structure-for-structure identity
explicit. For each actual process \(P\), its complete physical and
experiential organizations, expressed in a declared common structural
signature \(\mathcal K\), are isomorphic:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P).
\]

The statement, denoted A5-SI, formalizes one organization under physical
description and intrinsic experiential presentation. It is stronger than
the pairwise type-equivalence formula displayed in rc3; that weaker
formula is now a consequence. A finite countermodel explains why the
distinction matters. Under A5-SI, standard products of constituent
isomorphisms yield a corrected non-summative combination theorem without
adding an independent experience-generating mechanism. Nonproduct
organization concerns a specified constituent comparison, not a
numerical subject count.

Actual process plurality is accepted at all relevant physical scales.
Macroprocess analysis describes realized causal organization; it does
not introduce another experience-existence gate. A unique universal
partition into subjects is not a prerequisite of the theory. A scoped
symmetry-orbit result constrains optional exclusive subject selectors
while retaining possible invariant global partitions. Human conceptual
selfhood is treated as higher-order organization within experience.

A three-register thought experiment has identical declared input-output
behavior in two circuit settings while its complete modeled transition
maps are nonisomorphic. An implemented sub-process retains its
organization. This exactly checked case distinguishes whole-process
change from sub-process preservation without changing the comparison
token after an outcome. An extension with separately controlled
cross-links proves three exact internal-retention regimes: immediate
erasure, one-step retention, and sustained distinguishability, despite
unchanged declared output. The mathematical models supply no independent
phenomenal measurements.

The framework separates ontic organization from finite scientific views
and estimates. For later empirical applications, physical sufficiency
for a measured target and reflection of all relevant structural
differences are distinct bridge requirements. The remaining research
program is to derive more specific structural consequences and faithful
comparisons with neighboring theories. It does not require a second
account of why human rather than cellular experience exists. The paper
presents a unified explanatory principle with conditional formal
consequences; empirical confirmation and complete intertheory recovery
are separate claims.

\textbf{Keywords:} consciousness; panexperientialism; process ontology;
structural identity; composition; evolution; experiential structure;
self-model; scientific views

\hypertarget{the-target-problem}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{1. The target problem}\label{the-target-problem}}

Contemporary consciousness theories assign central roles to integrated
causal structure, recurrent processing, global availability,
higher-order representation, predictive modeling, information closure,
temporal organization, and related mechanisms (Seth \& Bayne, 2022;
Albantakis et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2020; Kanai \& Fujisawa, 2024).
Much of the competition among theories concerns whether one of these
mechanisms constitutes, enables, or marks consciousness.

UCT begins by separating two questions:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{\text{Does experience exist?}}
\]

and:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{\text{What is the organization of that experience?}}
\]

UCT fixes the first question axiomatically on its declared domain of
actual process tokens. It therefore relocates the nontrivial universal
problem to experiential structure.

Universal experience and structural identity are the adopted explanatory
starting points. Internal derivations are assessed by whether they
follow from those commitments. The substantive comparative task is to
constrain structured differences, multiscale relations, and
transformations; empirical applications additionally require declared
bridge predictions. Human and cellular experience do not require
different existence mechanisms within this framework.

The principal clarification of v1.1 is that the identity claim concerns
the actual physical organization of a process token, not a
representation chosen by an analyst. The second is that finite
experiments do not directly observe complete ontic organization or
complete phenomenology. Operational tests therefore target explicitly
declared finite bridge hypotheses.

\hypertarget{ontological-domain-actual-process-tokens}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{2. Ontological domain: actual process
tokens}\label{ontological-domain-actual-process-tokens}}

\hypertarget{actuality-and-tokenhood}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{2.1 Actuality and tokenhood}\label{actuality-and-tokenhood}}

Let:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathcal P
\]

be the domain of actual valid physical process tokens. An element:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
P\in\mathcal P
\]

is an actual causally continuous physical subhistory, not an arbitrary
set of variables selected after the fact.

A valid token must satisfy, at minimum:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{actual support} --- the relevant states/events are physically
  instantiated;
\item
  \textbf{induced-relation fidelity} --- attributed internal relations
  are inherited from physical organization rather than invented by the
  modeller;
\item
  \textbf{causal continuity} --- the token has a physically traceable
  causal history over the interval of interest;
\item
  \textbf{boundary accountability} --- materially relevant
  cross-boundary influences are represented rather than silently erased;
\item
  \textbf{lineage traceability} --- continuation, replacement,
  branching, merger, and cessation are physically trackable;
\item
  \textbf{description invariance} --- invertible coordinate or label
  changes do not create new tokens.
\end{enumerate}

UCT is process-token pluralist. Actual tokens may:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  overlap;
\item
  nest;
\item
  branch;
\item
  merge;
\item
  persist through replacement;
\item
  exist at multiple physical scales.
\end{itemize}

No maximality or exclusion rule is imposed at the basal level.

\hypertarget{multiscale-token-plurality}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{2.2 Multiscale token
plurality}\label{multiscale-token-plurality}}

A critical distinction in v1.1 is:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\text{actual macroprocess token}
\neq
\text{arbitrary coarse description}.
}
\]

Molecular, cellular, circuit-level, neural-system, organism-level, and
other macroprocesses may all be actual process tokens when the relevant
organization is physically instantiated. A scientist does not create a
macroprocess merely by averaging microscopic variables.

Macroprocess is a scale-relative description of actual organized
physical activity. Its experience does not wait for a new threshold of
autonomy, recurrence, closure, complexity, or subjecthood. Many
overlapping and nested processes may qualify; UCT does not require a
unique privileged macro boundary. In an application, one must specify
which actual causal organization is being described, just as one must
specify which physical process a model concerns. That identification
task should not be confused with a missing mechanism that turns
macroprocesses into experience.

\hypertarget{token-type-and-lineage}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{2.3 Token, type, and lineage}\label{token-type-and-lineage}}

UCT distinguishes:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\text{structural type}
\neq
\text{numerical token}
\neq
\text{causal lineage}.
}
\]

Two process tokens can have isomorphic organization without being
numerically identical:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D(P)\cong \mathbf D(Q)
\not\Rightarrow
P=Q.
\]

Lineage is a directed continuation relation rather than numerical
identity. A process may branch:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
P\leadsto Q_1,
\qquad
P\leadsto Q_2,
\qquad
Q_1\neq Q_2,
\]

or contribute to a merger:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
P\leadsto M,
\qquad
Q\leadsto M.
\]

Nothing in these relations alone determines a unique subject count.

\hypertarget{a-concrete-finite-process-criterion}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{2.4 A concrete finite process
criterion}\label{a-concrete-finite-process-criterion}}

In a finite classical description, let \(G\) represent physically
instantiated events and their causal relations. An admissible process
subhistory \(H\) consists of actual events, inherits their physical
relations, is connected through the inherited causal links over the
declared interval, and records materially relevant links across its
boundary. Connectivity here does not require mutual influence between
every pair, maximality, an autonomous Markov state, or a nervous system.
A numerical renaming of \(H\) does not create another physical
occurrence.

This is a concrete expression of Section 2.1's process domain, not an
independent consciousness detector. Whenever such a process is actual,
A1 applies. Different subhistories can overlap and nest; selecting all
admissible processes does not demand an exclusive partition. Greater
integration, temporal depth, differentiation, or self-maintenance then
characterizes differences of organization within the domain.

The representation \(G\) must itself be grounded in the physical system.
A convenient calculated feature may describe an existing process without
creating an additional token. In contrast, an actual causal relation
remains relevant even when it crosses an observer's preferred anatomical
or computational boundary. Applications may investigate a process's
current state carriers, dynamical contributors, enabling conditions, and
output recipients separately. Those roles do not determine whether some
underlying actual local process has experience.

A cell, a multicellular organism, and an extended interaction can
therefore each instantiate experience-bearing organization without
passing three different ontological tests. An entire organism need not
be one indivisible subject for its many realized processes to fall
within UCT. Appendix D gives optional procedures for checking finite
coarse descriptions; their predictive-closure conditions are not
required by the process criterion or by A1.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{3. Ontic organization, views, estimates, and
subjects}\label{ontic-organization-views-estimates-and-subjects}}

The central formal firewall of v1.1 is:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\text{ontic process token}
\neq
\text{physical view}
\neq
\text{scientific estimate}
\neq
\text{subject}.
}
\]

\hypertarget{full-token-relative-ontic-organization}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{3.1 Full token-relative ontic
organization}\label{full-token-relative-ontic-organization}}

For an actual process token \(P\), define:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
}
\]

as the complete currently instantiated physical organization
constitutive of that token.

``Complete'' is token-relative. It means that every physical distinction
or relation constitutive of \(P\)'s own organization is included. It
does not mean that every microscopic fact anywhere in the realizing
substrate or surrounding universe is part of the same token.

This distinction permits fine and coarse actual process tokens to
coexist without making every microscopic fluctuation a mandatory
difference in a human-scale macroexperience.

\hypertarget{physical-scientific-views}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{3.2 Physical scientific
views}\label{physical-scientific-views}}

Science does not ordinarily access \(\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\) directly.
Instead, it constructs physically anchored views indexed by:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
v=(t,\tau,g,\mathcal I,\mathcal B,\ldots),
\]

where \(t\) is time, \(\tau\) a horizon, \(g\) a
measurement/representation grain, \(\mathcal I\) a physically admissible
intervention family, and \(\mathcal B\) a declared boundary
representation.

Write:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
D_v(P)
=
\Pi_v[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)
].
\]

Different views need not form one total hierarchy. Some are refinements
or coarse projections of others; some are incomparable.

Where two routes claim to construct the same target physical view, local
consistency requires approximate or exact commutation:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
R_{v\to z}
\cong
R_{w\to z}\circ R_{v\to w}.
\]

\hypertarget{scientific-estimates}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{3.3 Scientific estimates}\label{scientific-estimates}}

Actual data yield:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\widehat D_v(P\mid\mathcal E,\mathcal M),
\]

an estimate conditioned on evidence \(\mathcal E\) and model class
\(\mathcal M\).

If evidence underdetermines the physical organization, the appropriate
scientific output is an identified set:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathfrak I(P)
=
\left\{
D:
D\text{ remains compatible with the available evidence}
\right\},
\]

rather than an ontological point estimate chosen for convenience.

\hypertarget{subjecthood}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{3.4 Subjecthood}\label{subjecthood}}

A subject is not defined merely by being a process token, a scientific
view, or a macroprocess.

UCT therefore maintains:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\text{experience-bearing process}
\neq
\text{canonically individuated subject}.
}
\]

A universal positive subject selector is not required for this basal
theory. Where a study uses subject to mean an experience-bearing
process, the process domain supplies its referent. Where it means a
human-like owner or conceptual self, the relevant self-organizing and
self-representational relations belong to higher-order organization.
Neither usage licenses imposing one exclusive subject partition on all
actual processes.

\hypertarget{a-pointed-port-aware-comparison-signature}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{3.5 A pointed, port-aware comparison
signature}\label{a-pointed-port-aware-comparison-signature}}

The word \emph{structure} must not conceal what an isomorphism is
required to preserve. In the finite controlled models below, the
declared signature has sorts for states, boundary inputs, intervention
ports, outputs, constituent slots, and time; operation symbols for
update, reset, readout, and constituent projection; a distinguished
current-state constant; and the declared temporal and incidence
relations. A model in that signature can be written
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathcal M_v=(X,x_\bullet,U,J,Y,T,I,o,\mathscr C,\preceq).
\]

The tuple is a model \textbf{in} the signature, not the signature
itself. Here \(T_u:X\to X\) is an update at fixed constitutive
mechanism, \(I_j:X\to X\) a permitted state reset, and \(o:X\to Y\) a
declared output. \(\mathscr C\) specifies constituent projections and
physically identified ports. Current-state pointing is essential: two
occurrences of the same transition law at inequivalent current states
need not have the same complete organization.

An admissible isomorphism contains bijections \(f_X,f_U,f_J,f_Y\) that
satisfy
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
f_X(x_\bullet)=x'_\bullet,\qquad
f_XT_u=T'_{f_U(u)}f_X,\qquad
f_XI_j=I'_{f_J(j)}f_X,\qquad
f_Yo=o'f_X,
\]

and preserve the declared temporal relations and constituent incidence.
A port permutation is allowed only when it is declared as a symmetry of
the comparison; an arbitrary mixture of physically distinct ports is not
a relabeling of a fixed constituent split. Changing a mechanism,
removing an intervention, or reversing a directed temporal relation
generally changes the comparison rather than supplying an isomorphism. A
probe family chosen by the analyst remains part of a scientific view,
whereas a process's actual causal dispositions are ontic.

For the product comparison in Section 6, hold the common boundary
convention fixed, use the product state space with its pointed state,
and retain the specified component-local update and reset operations.
These declarations identify the ordinary independent product used on
both sides of A5-SI. They do not impose autonomy, reciprocity,
maximality, or a new threshold for experience.

The finite signature supplies an explicit example, not a claim that a
finite model captures all constitutive properties of real hardware or a
brain. A restricted signature or measured view can omit distinctions.
Completeness relative to an actual token is therefore not inferred from
success in this finite representation. In biological applications the
relevant state carriers, interfaces, and time relations must be
justified independently; the same foundational identity principle is
used.

\hypertarget{foundational-commitments}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{4. Foundational commitments}\label{foundational-commitments}}

\hypertarget{a1-universal-experience}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{A1 --- Universal Experience}\label{a1-universal-experience}}

For every actual valid process token:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\forall P\in\mathcal P,
\qquad
\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing.
}
\]

A1 is not derived from evolution, neuroscience, intelligence, report,
complexity, or biology.

The weaker equivalence formulas A5-W and A5-OI do not themselves
establish nonemptiness for every actual token. Under a strong
structure-for-structure representation, nonemptiness may also follow
mathematically from nonempty physical structure. A1 is retained to state
the intended ontological commitment and to prevent actual-process
definitions from quietly excluding simple cases.

\hypertarget{a2-scoped-structural-continuity}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{A2 --- Scoped Structural
Continuity}\label{a2-scoped-structural-continuity}}

For an explicitly declared continuously comparable family
\(P(\lambda)\), if the relevant physical organization varies
continuously in a specified structural topology, then experiential
organization varies continuously:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D(P_\lambda)\text{ continuous}
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P_\lambda)\text{ continuous}.
}
\]

A2 is a schema, not a universal scalar continuity law. Quantitative use
requires a declared metric/topology. A derived categorical observable
can undergo a threshold transition even when underlying structure varies
continuously.

\hypertarget{a3-persistence-under-embedding}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{A3 --- Persistence Under
Embedding}\label{a3-persistence-under-embedding}}

If an actual process \(P\) remains physically realized while embedded in
a larger actual process \(Q\), embedding alone does not terminate \(P\):
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
P\triangleleft Q
\not\Rightarrow
\text{cessation of }P.
}
\]

The distinctive content of A3 is process ontological. Once the lower
token remains actual, A1 supplies nonempty lower-scale experience.

A3 does not imply that a process continues after its constitutive
dynamics cease.

\hypertarget{a4-selfhood-non-prerequisite}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{A4 --- Selfhood
Non-Prerequisite}\label{a4-selfhood-non-prerequisite}}

Human-like conceptual selfhood is not required for basal experience:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\neg Self_I(P)
\not\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)=\varnothing.
}
\]

The positive mechanisms by which body models, agency, autobiographical
memory, metacognition, and a conceptual ``I'' form are not axioms. They
belong to higher-order application/bridge layers.

\hypertarget{a5---experience-structure-identity}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{A5 - Experience-Structure
Identity}\label{a5---experience-structure-identity}}

UCT's intended claim is that experience is the intrinsic presentation of
an actual physical process's own organization. Cellular and human
experience instantiate this same identity principle; richer organization
does not require a second kind of experiential ingredient.

\hypertarget{a5-si---explicit-structure-for-structure-identity}{%
\subsubsection{A5-SI - explicit structure-for-structure
identity}\label{a5-si---explicit-structure-for-structure-identity}}

Fix a common structural signature \(\mathcal K\) for the comparison: the
relevant state distinctions, causal and temporal relations, and
constituent organization must be specified. Write
\(\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)\) and \(\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\) for
the complete physical and experiential organization of the same actual
token in this signature. The strong intended identity is expressed by
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
h_P:\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)
\;\cong\;\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P).
\]

The two expressions describe the same organization in physical and
experiential terms. The isomorphism is a formal expression of that
commitment, not an empirically fitted correspondence, an extra causal
force, or an experience-generating mechanism. Structural relations must
be fixed by the declared signature; they cannot be freely relabeled to
fit a desired phenomenal result. When an independent product is used, it
is the ordinary product in the common comparison category on both sides.
Section 6 makes this typing explicit.

\textbf{Version boundary.} This is stronger than the pairwise
equivalence formula displayed in rc3. The rc4 development revision
adopted it to formalize the intended identity interpretation; it does
not claim to have deduced it from the weaker formula or to have
established it empirically. The published v1.0 and the original rc3
remain unchanged.

\hypertarget{a5-oi---pairwise-type-equivalence-as-a-consequence}{%
\subsubsection{A5-OI - pairwise type equivalence as a
consequence}\label{a5-oi---pairwise-type-equivalence-as-a-consequence}}

Within that common signature, composing the tokenwise isomorphisms gives
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q)
\iff
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q).
\]

The subscripts are suppressed when the scope is clear. This consequence
preserves and reflects equivalence between actual token types, but if
written alone it is weaker than A5-SI: an arbitrary permutation of type
labels can satisfy it without preserving each token's organization.
Section 6 supplies an explicit counterexample to that weaker reading.

\hypertarget{a5-w---one-way-phenomenal-completeness}{%
\subsubsection{A5-W - one-way phenomenal
completeness}\label{a5-w---one-way-phenomenal-completeness}}

The one-way consequence inherited from the explicitly stated v1.0
formula is
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D(P)\cong\mathbf D(Q)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q).
\]

It excludes a freely varying hidden phenomenal variable when complete
constitutive physical organization is fixed. No product conclusion is
attributed to this one-way formula alone. In the rest of the paper, A5
denotes the intended strong identity, with A5-W or A5-OI cited when a
weaker consequence already suffices.

\hypertarget{a6-token-type-lineage-distinction}{%

Distinction}\label{a6-token-type-lineage-distinction}}
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D(P)\cong\mathbf D(Q)
\not\Rightarrow
P=Q.
}
\]

Structural equivalence does not imply numerical identity.

\hypertarget{formal-consequences}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{5. Formal consequences}\label{formal-consequences}}

\hypertarget{organization-gate-impossibility}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{5.1 Organization-Gate
Impossibility}\label{organization-gate-impossibility}}

Define an experience-existence predicate:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
E(P)=\mathbf 1[\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing].
\]

By A1:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
E(P)=1
\qquad
\forall P\in\mathcal P.
\]

Let \(F(P)\) be any organization descriptor and \(S\) a threshold/target
region. If:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\exists Q\in\mathcal P:
F(Q)\notin S,
\]

then:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf 1[F(P)\in S]
\not\equiv
E(P).
}
\]

Hence, within UCT, no nonuniversal threshold of:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  integration;
\item
  recurrence;
\item
  global availability;
\item
  explicit memory;
\item
  higher-order representation;
\item
  predictive modeling;
\item
  autonomy;
\item
  entropy;
\item
  generic biological complexity;
\end{itemize}

can equal the universal experience-existence predicate.

This theorem does not make those mechanisms irrelevant. It relocates
them from experience existence to experience organization.

\hypertarget{no-hidden-quale}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{5.2 No Hidden Quale}\label{no-hidden-quale}}

From A5-W:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q)
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)\cong
\Phi(Q).
\]

UCT therefore rejects an additional hidden phenomenal degree of freedom
that varies independently while complete token-relative physical
organization is fixed.

Within the intended structuralist interpretation, words such as ``red''
and ``pain'' label experiential organizations rather than additional
freely varying quale atoms. A5-W alone does not determine the physical
organization corresponding to either label or establish the complete
structuralist interpretation.

\hypertarget{probe-invariance}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{5.3 Probe Invariance}\label{probe-invariance}}

Let \(\mathcal I^{exp}\) denote an experimenter-selected probe family.
Merely changing this choice, without changing the complete constitutive
physical organization, cannot alter phenomenology under A5-W:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I^{exp}_1&\ne\mathcal I^{exp}_2,\\
\mathbf D^{ontic}_1(P)&\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_2(P)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Phi_1(P)\cong\Phi_2(P).
\end{aligned}
\]

The completeness qualifier matters. Holding only one causal graph or one
response descriptor fixed is insufficient if other constitutive state
distinctions change. A probe that physically intervenes on the process
can change its experience by changing its organization; the invariant
claim concerns a merely external change of description or choice.

\hypertarget{no-future-contamination}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{5.4 No Future Contamination}\label{no-future-contamination}}

If:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(Q),
\]

then a future external divergence that is not physically instantiated at
time \(t\) cannot imply a present phenomenal difference:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(P)\cong\Phi_t(Q).
}
\]

Present causal dispositions are part of the present organization; actual
future facts are not.

\hypertarget{factorization-macroprocesses-and-combination}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{6. Factorization, macroprocesses, and
combination}\label{factorization-macroprocesses-and-combination}}

\hypertarget{independent-products-and-comparison-objects}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{6.1 Independent products and comparison
objects}\label{independent-products-and-comparison-objects}}

Fix structural signatures and a constituent split before comparing a
whole with its parts. The physical product
\(X_1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_n\) represents independent juxtaposition
relative to that split and a declared boundary convention. In a
transition model, component updates must not depend on the other
component states once the declared boundary is fixed. A shared drive can
create statistical correlations without creating the internal causal
dependence at issue. Product factorization is consequently not
synonymous with unconditional statistical independence.

For this product comparison, constituents occupy nonoverlapping slots of
the specified physical split. A product of overlapping actual tokens
would require a separately justified comparison construction; general
gluing of overlapping processes is not established here. An abstract
product is initially a \textbf{comparison object}. It need not itself be
an actual causally continuous token; A1 is not automatically applied to
it. Unrestricted mixing of physically distinct intervention ports must
not be mistaken for a mere relabeling that preserves a particular
constituent split. The phenomenal product must also be specified as the
intended independent juxtaposition, not retrospectively defined to make
a desired conclusion true.

\hypertarget{why-a5-oi-alone-is-insufficient}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{6.2 Why A5-OI alone is
insufficient}\label{why-a5-oi-alone-is-insufficient}}

A5-OI constrains equivalence of structural types. It does not state how
products are transported. The distinction is visible in a finite-set
countermodel. Take isomorphism classes indexed by positive cardinality
and the bijection
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
f(6)=7,\qquad f(7)=6,\qquad f(n)=n\quad(n\ne6,7).
\]

Let physical constituent types have sizes 2 and 3, and a whole type have
size 7. Map phenomenal types using \(f\). The equivalence biconditional
holds because \(f\) is injective. Nevertheless,
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
7\ne2\cdot3,\qquad f(7)=6=f(2)\cdot f(3).
\]

Thus a nonproduct physical type can correspond to the product of the
constituent phenomenal types while the bare A5-OI equivalence formula is
satisfied. This is an abstract countermodel to an implication of the
formula, not a claim that unstructured finite sets are adequate physical
or phenomenal models. It pinpoints the missing algebraic commitment.
Additional physical restrictions may exclude it, but those restrictions
must be stated.

\hypertarget{non-summative-combination-from-the-intended-identity}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{6.3 Non-summative combination from the intended
identity}\label{non-summative-combination-from-the-intended-identity}}

Use the strong tokenwise identity A5-SI in a common comparison category
with the finite products needed for the argument. Independent
juxtaposition on each side is represented by this same standard product.
Ordinary products preserve isomorphisms: if each \(h_i:X_i\cong Y_i\),
then
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\bigotimes_i h_i:\bigotimes_i X_i\cong\bigotimes_i Y_i.
\]

This is the relevant mathematical consequence of expressing the same
constituent organizations in two descriptions. It is not another
physical mechanism of experiential combination. The use of a common
signature and product must be declared; the weak type-equivalence
formula alone cannot supply it.

\textbf{Theorem 6.3 (non-summative combination in the declared product
comparison).} Let \(M\) be an actual whole process and \(P_i\) its
actual constituents in the nonoverlapping slots of the specified
comparison. If
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)\not\cong
\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

then A5-SI implies
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\Phi(M)\not\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i).
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Let \(h_M\) and \(h_i\) be the tokenwise isomorphisms
supplied by A5-SI. If \(\Phi(M)\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)\), compose
that isomorphism with \(h_M\) and the inverse of the product of the
\(h_i\). This yields
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)
\cong\Phi(M)
\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)
\cong\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

contradicting the physical premise. The abstract comparison product need
not be an actual token, so no extension of A1 to imaginary processes is
used. \(\square\)

The result says that actual additional constitutive relations are not
erased in the experiential description. It does not add together
subjective substances. Its nonproduct conclusion is relative to the
specified constituent product, not every possible decomposition.
Continuing constituent processes remain experience-bearing by A1 and A3.
Neither the theorem nor the theory requires a unique numerical subject
count before admitting the whole's experiential organization.

If identity is deliberately weakened back to the pairwise A5-OI formula
alone, the countermodel in Section 6.2 still applies and this proof is
unavailable. The repair therefore makes the intended premise explicit
rather than silently inferring a stronger premise from a weaker formula.

\hypertarget{macro-invariance-with-micro-change}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{6.4 Macro invariance with micro
change}\label{macro-invariance-with-micro-change}}

Suppose actual macroprocess tokens obey
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_A)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_B),
\]

while an independently identified pair of lower-scale actual tokens
obeys
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^A)\not\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^B).
\]

A5-W suffices for \(\Phi(M_A)\cong\Phi(M_B)\). The converse component of
A5-OI additionally yields \(\Phi(p^A)\not\cong\Phi(p^B)\). Only the
stated pairwise consequences are needed for these claims; the product
construction is not involved.

The result is conditional: it does not show that any specified material
replacement actually preserves complete macro organization. Nor can one
establish it merely by observing the same coarse statistic of the same
token. The macro token must have an independently supported realization
and boundary; lower-scale physical differences must not be constitutive
differences of that macro token. Appendix B demonstrates the
corresponding distinction between a fixed whole-process token and an
invariant implemented sub-process in an idealized finite model. It is
not a general biological replacement theorem.

\hypertarget{subject-selection-under-symmetry-an-orbit-obstruction}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{7. Subject selection under symmetry: an orbit
obstruction}\label{subject-selection-under-symmetry-an-orbit-obstruction}}

UCT distinguishes experience, structural unity, macroprocess tokenhood,
subjecthood, and self-modeling. The result below concerns a proposed
selection rule. It neither derives subjecthood from A1 nor treats every
experience-bearing process as one canonical subject.

\hypertarget{equivariant-selection-is-a-union-of-candidate-orbits}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{7.1 Equivariant selection is a union of candidate
orbits}\label{equivariant-selection-is-a-union-of-candidate-orbits}}

Let \(W\) be a physical structure and \(\Gamma\) a group of its
structure-preserving automorphisms. Let \(\mathcal C(W)\) be a candidate
family closed under this group. A deterministic selector returns
\(S(W)\subseteq\mathcal C(W)\) and is equivariant:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
S(gW)=gS(W).
\]

Since \(gW=W\) for \(g\in\Gamma\), the selected family is invariant
under \(\Gamma\).

\textbf{Orbit lemma.} If a candidate \(P\) is selected, its entire orbit
\(\Gamma P\) is selected.

\textbf{Proof.} For each \(g\in\Gamma\), invariance gives
\(gP\in gS(W)=S(W)\). Thus a selected family is a union of full orbits.
\(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary 7.1 (restricted exclusive-selection obstruction).}
Suppose the admissible candidates are one orbit \(O\) containing an
overlapping pair. No deterministic equivariant selector can return a
nonempty, pairwise disjoint subfamily of \(O\).

\textbf{Proof.} A nonempty selection contains one candidate and hence
all of \(O\). It then contains the overlapping pair, contrary to
pairwise disjointness. \(\square\)

This corollary requires the stated restriction of the candidate family
and a nonempty-output requirement. These are selection requirements, not
consequences of A1-A6.

\hypertarget{the-three-part-example-and-its-counterlimits}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{7.2 The three-part example and its
counterlimits}\label{the-three-part-example-and-its-counterlimits}}

For a reflection-symmetric world with parts \(a,b,c\), let the
reflection exchange \(a\) and \(c\), and let the candidate orbit be
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
O=\bigl\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\}\bigr\}.
\]

The two invariant selected subfamilies are the empty family and the
whole orbit. The first violates nonemptiness; the second violates
exclusivity. An unbiased random rule could choose either member with
equal probability, but it would not determine one unique subject from
\(W\); a realized random seed would add selection data.

The theorem does \textbf{not} rule out the invariant partitions
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\bigl\{\{a,b,c\}\bigr\},\qquad
\bigl\{\{a\},\{b\},\{c\}\bigr\}.
\]

If a positive subject theory admits one of these, it needs its own
physical justification. Neither is excluded by the orbit lemma. The
correct conclusion is a scoped obstruction to selecting nonempty
exclusive candidates from an overlapping symmetry orbit, not a general
theorem that intrinsic structure cannot determine any unique partition.
A universal positive subject criterion is not imposed by UCT. Any
additional exclusive selector must justify its own scope and purpose.

\hypertarget{from-physical-process-to-conceptual-self-a-structural-evolutionary-synthesis}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{8. From physical process to conceptual self: a
structural-evolutionary
synthesis}\label{from-physical-process-to-conceptual-self-a-structural-evolutionary-synthesis}}

This section is explanatory rather than foundational.

It asks:

\begin{quote}
If A1 and the intended identity A5-SI are granted, can one describe a
continuous and branching path from elementary physical processes to
human conceptual selfhood without introducing successive new phenomenal
substances?
\end{quote}

UCT can represent such a path coherently as a structural synthesis. This
does not establish that the historical transitions occurred in exactly
the sequence listed below, nor does it provide independent evidence for
A1 or A5-SI. The relevant theoretical claim is narrower: no additional
phenomenal substance is required by the UCT formalism when these
organizational transformations are described.

Evolution supplies the organizing rationale for continuity across these
cases. UCT takes universal experience and structural identity as its
starting commitments; it does not repeatedly reopen their derivation
inside each biological application. Comparative evolutionary findings
illustrate changes of organization. Accepting biological evolution by
itself need not force another theorist to adopt UCT's axioms; that
external disagreement is distinct from whether UCT's internal
explanations consistently use them.

\hypertarget{elementary-physical-processes}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.1 Elementary physical
processes}\label{elementary-physical-processes}}

At the most elementary level considered by UCT, an actual interaction
process possesses whatever relational structure is physically
instantiated.

UCT does not require:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  a nervous system;
\item
  a sensor;
\item
  a memory;
\item
  a self-model;
\item
  a report channel.
\end{itemize}

Under A1, the experience-existence predicate is already nonzero. A5-SI
identifies this process's experiential organization with its actual
organization. The pairwise A5-OI formula is a consequence of that
stronger statement.

UCT therefore refuses to project human terms such as ``seeing,''
``wanting,'' or ``suffering'' onto elementary interactions. The
primitive claim is structural, not anthropomorphic.

\hypertarget{from-stable-chemistry-to-self-maintaining-organization}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.2 From stable chemistry to self-maintaining
organization}\label{from-stable-chemistry-to-self-maintaining-organization}}

The transition from nonliving chemistry to the first cells is an
origin-of-life problem, not a solved Darwinian lineage. UCT does not
claim to reconstruct the historical path.

The theoretical point is narrower. If physical processes develop
persistent boundaries, recurrent chemical organization, and endogenous
regulation, then new relations appear inside the process organization:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\text{internal state}
\leftrightarrow
\text{boundary}
\leftrightarrow
\text{environmental perturbation}
\leftrightarrow
\text{regulatory response}.
\]

These relations are not the origin of experience under A1. They are
transformations of experiential organization.

\hypertarget{single-cell-and-non-neural-learning-temporal-depth}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.3 Single-cell and non-neural learning: temporal
depth}\label{single-cell-and-non-neural-learning-temporal-depth}}

A decisive organizational transition occurs when past events leave
present physical traces that modify current response:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
History
\to
m_t
\to
Response_t.
\]

This is the minimal form of temporal depth required by UCT's memory
analysis.

The framework does not require memory to create experience. Memory
changes current organization.

Non-neural learning illustrates the physical possibility of
history-dependent behavior without a brain. Habituation has been
experimentally demonstrated in the slime mould \emph{Physarum
polycephalum} (Boisseau, Vogel, \& Dussutour, 2016). Such evidence does
not establish phenomenology in the organism; it demonstrates that
learning-like temporal organization need not await centralized nervous
systems.

If all physical consequences of a history are erased, No Ghost History
predicts no remaining current phenomenal-type difference attributable
solely to the past.

\hypertarget{multicellularity-from-juxtaposition-to-organism-scale-relations}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.4 Multicellularity: from juxtaposition to organism-scale
relations}\label{multicellularity-from-juxtaposition-to-organism-scale-relations}}

Consider independent cells:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
C_1,\ldots,C_n.
\]

If their joint organization remains a product, there is no new
irreducible cross-cell relation merely because the cells are spatially
adjacent.

If, however, real chemical, electrical, mechanical, developmental, or
other causal relations generate an actual multicellular process \(M\)
with:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D(M)
\not\cong
\bigotimes_i
\mathbf D(C_i),
\]

then the structure-for-structure identity A5-SI assigns \(M\) an
experiential organization that is nonproduct relative to that
constituent comparison, by Theorem 6.3. The multicellular case uses the
same identity principle as the cellular case; no new experiential
substance or threshold is introduced.

The significance of multicellularity in UCT is therefore not cell count.
It is the formation of new actual macroprocess organization.

No privileged signaling medium is required. Mechanical, chemical,
electrical, and neural relations can all contribute when they are
physically constitutive.

\hypertarget{nervous-systems-routing-and-specialization-not-an-experience-switch}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.5 Nervous systems: routing and specialization, not an
experience
switch}\label{nervous-systems-routing-and-specialization-not-an-experience-switch}}

The evolution of neurons adds high-speed, long-range, selectively
routed, and plastic coordination.

Under UCT:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\boxed{
\text{neuron}
\neq
\text{experience ignition}.
}
\]

Neurons transform organization. They can increase spatial
discrimination, routing, temporal coordination, learning capacity, and
context sensitivity.

Associative learning in the box jellyfish \emph{Tripedalia cystophora},
a cnidarian without a vertebrate-style centralized brain, provides an
empirical illustration that nontrivial learning need not wait for
vertebrate-like centralization (Bielecki et al., 2023).

This finding is not evidence for UCT's A1. It illustrates the danger of
equating a particular level of centralized neural architecture with the
first appearance of complex learning.

\hypertarget{damage-regulation-nociception-and-affect}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.6 Damage regulation, nociception, and
affect}\label{damage-regulation-nociception-and-affect}}

The pain lineage is particularly useful because it exposes the mistake
of projecting a fully human quale backward.

Comparative biology indicates that damage detection and adaptive
cellular stress responses predate nervous systems, while specialized
nociceptors, sensitization, central aversive states, and human pain
involve progressively richer mechanisms (Walters, 2025).

A schematic structural trajectory proceeds through:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  cellular damage regulation;
\item
  multicellular injury coordination;
\item
  specialized nociception and sensitization;
\item
  aversive learning and multidimensional affective pain;
\item
  human suffering.
\end{enumerate}

UCT does not identify the first term with human pain.

It instead asks which physical relations constituting later pain are
already present in simpler systems.

Human pain also illustrates why one scalar valence variable is
inadequate. Clinical and conceptual literatures distinguish
sensory-discriminative from affective-motivational dimensions of pain,
and pain-asymbolia cases have been interpreted as evidence for partial
dissociation between them (Auvray, Myin, \& Spence, 2010). That
interpretation is not uncontested: recent philosophical reviews
emphasize that asymbolia may reflect strongly reduced rather than
literally absent negative affect, and that the taxonomy of the residual
state remains debated (Coninx, 2024). The conservative UCT conclusion is
therefore:

\textbf{Nociceptive discrimination, unpleasantness, and suffering should
not be collapsed into one primitive scalar variable.}

Under A5-SI, unpleasantness belongs to the intrinsic organization of the
relevant actual process, just as any cellular quality does. Identifying
which concrete human mechanisms instantiate a specified pain experience
is an application-level biological question; it does not require another
foundational account of why human experience exists.

\hypertarget{one-explanatory-principle-across-experiential-qualities}{%
\subsubsection{One explanatory principle across experiential
qualities}\label{one-explanatory-principle-across-experiential-qualities}}

Let \(P_c\) be a cellular process and \(P_h\) a human process. UCT
applies the same relation
\(\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)\) in each
appropriately declared comparison. The organizations differ in their
realized distinctions and relations; the explanatory principle does not
change. Human red, pain, and bodily ownership can occupy richly
differentiated positions within a process's experiential organization
without requiring that a cell possess those same human discriminations
or linguistic labels.

In a continuously comparable transformation family, A2 supplies
experiential continuity and A5 supplies structural identity. Added,
removed, or reorganized causal relations correspond to changes in the
intrinsic organization itself. The theory need not posit a point where
an otherwise nonexperiential organization suddenly acquires a new
phenomenal substance. Nor does it follow that evolution must
monotonically increase one scalar amount of experience.

At this explanatory level, asking for a second mechanism connecting a
fully specified actual organization to its experience would reopen what
A5 fixes as the identity principle. A separate question is which actual
organization realizes a particular familiar human experience.
Investigating that empirical instance can refine an application without
constituting a missing axiom of the general theory.

\hypertarget{association-relational-abstraction-and-reasoning}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.7 Association, relational abstraction, and
reasoning}\label{association-relational-abstraction-and-reasoning}}

Cognitive evolution can likewise be represented as transformations of
organization rather than the sudden arrival of a new phenomenal
substance.

A simplified comparative sequence runs from state-dependent response
through habituation and sensitization, association, relational
abstraction, relations over relations, and symbolic or compositional
reasoning.

The sequence is not a literal phylogenetic ladder.

Honeybees can learn and transfer same/different relational rules to
novel stimuli (Giurfa et al., 2001), supporting the narrower claim that
transferable relational discrimination need not require a human-sized
brain.

Cuttlefish have shown what-where-when behavioral organization meeting
standard criteria for episodic-like memory (Jozet-Alves, Bertin, \&
Clayton, 2013), and later work demonstrated substantial delayed control
in a delay-of-gratification task (Schnell et al., 2021).

These examples support the narrower conclusion that component capacities
relevant to human cognition occur in distant lineages and can be
realized by substantially different nervous-system architectures. They
do not establish a single linear ancestry of cognition, nor do they
demonstrate that nonhuman animals execute human formal logic or possess
phenomenology identical to human cognition.

\hypertarget{physical-witness-and-semantic-inflation}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.8 Physical witness and semantic
inflation}\label{physical-witness-and-semantic-inflation}}

A danger in comparative cognition is to redescribe simple behavior with
sophisticated human concepts.

UCT therefore introduces a Physical Witness Criterion:

\begin{quote}
A claimed higher-order relation belongs to the actual constitutive
organization only if the physical process itself distinguishes that
relational configuration under controlled, physically admissible
perturbation.
\end{quote}

For example, ``same/different'' should not be attributed merely because
an analyst can describe stimuli relationally. Transfer to novel physical
stimuli provides stronger evidence that the system is sensitive to an
invariant relation, though behavioral transfer alone still need not
reveal the complete ontic mechanism.

The criterion blocks:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\text{observer's description}
\Rightarrow
\text{invented phenomenal dimension}.
\]

\hypertarget{body-centered-organization}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{8.9 Body-centered
organization}\label{body-centered-organization}}

With increasingly coordinated sensorimotor organization, a system can
physically encode relations involving:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  internal/external boundary;
\item
  body position;
\item
  sensor position;
\item
  action state;
\item
  consequences of self-generated action;
\item
  persistent internal variables.
\end{itemize}

Write this only as a projection of already existing process
organization:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathcal S(M)
=

A concrete consequence should identify which relation changes, rather
than merely restating that different complete structures have different
experiential types. Extend Appendix B by controlling the two cross-links
separately:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
s^+=s\oplus u,\qquad
a^+=s\oplus(\alpha b),\qquad
b^+=s\oplus(\beta a),\qquad y=s,
\quad (\alpha,\beta)\in\{0,1\}^2.
\]

The fixed comparison contains the whole network \(W_{\alpha\beta}\), the
implemented \(s\)-process, and the boundary-conditioned \(a\mid b\)
constituent split. Buffers, the clock, state-reset ports, and idealized
completeness are as in Appendix B. The settings are distinct mechanisms,
not one continuous A2 interpolation. Conditional UCT statements concern
their actual realizations, not uninstantiated equations.

For common \(s_0\) and input string, all four settings have the same
declared output. To measure retention of an \emph{initial internal
perturbation}, fix the common boundary trajectory \(s_t\), allow all
four initial pair states \(z_0=(a_0,b_0)^T\), and apply no further pair
resets. Differences between two pair trajectories obey linear dynamics
over \(\mathbb F_2\):
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\delta z_n=A_{\alpha\beta}^{\,n}\delta z_0,\qquad
A_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\\\beta&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The identical boundary drive cancels from the difference equation.
Define the retained distinguishability at horizon \(n\geq1\) by the
number of different pair states reachable from the four initial pair
states under that fixed drive:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
N_n=|\{z_n:z_0\in\mathbb F_2^2\}|=2^{\operatorname{rank}(A_{\alpha\beta}^{\,n})}.
\]

This is a specific response-partition invariant. It is not the number of
experiences, a scalar amount of consciousness, a familiar quale label,
or a full phenomenal metric.

different properties, not interchangeable labels.

diagram; the noise model and stability would have to be analyzed.

\hypertarget{sufficiency-and-reflection-are-different-bridge-hypotheses}{%

\subsection{10.2 Sufficiency and reflection are different bridge
hypotheses}\label{sufficiency-and-reflection-are-different-bridge-hypotheses}}

Let \(D_v\) be a frozen finite physical view and \(\Psi_v\) a declared
finite phenomenal target. A \textbf{sufficiency bridge} \(B_v^{\to}\)
asserts
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
D_v(A)\cong D_v(B)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \Psi_v(A)\cong\Psi_v(B).
\]

Equivalently, on the compared classes, the target factors through a map
\(f_v\) from the physical view. This does not require that \(f_v\) be
injective. To assert a biconditional one must separately impose a
\textbf{reflection bridge} \(B_v^{\leftarrow}\):
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\Psi_v(A)\cong\Psi_v(B)
\ \Longrightarrow\ D_v(A)\cong D_v(B).
\]

Together these bridges say that the physical view and the finite target
distinguish the same classes within the declared scope. A5-OI for
complete structures does not imply reflection after either side has
undergone a lossy projection. For example, even an identity
correspondence between two full physical and phenomenal states can have
an observed target that assigns both states the same response. The
finite target then remains sufficient as a function of the physical
state but fails reflection.

\hypertarget{operational-structural-correspondence}{%

Correspondence}\label{operational-structural-correspondence}}

Two packages should therefore be distinguished:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\begin{aligned}
OSC_v^{\to}&=A5\;+\;B_v^{\to}\;+\;\text{measurement assumptions},\\
OSC_v^{\leftrightarrow}&=A5\;+\;B_v^{\to}\;+\;B_v^{\leftarrow}
\;+\;\text{measurement assumptions}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The notation denotes packages of assumptions, not numeric addition. The
operational implications are supplied by the bridge conditions;
attaching A5 explains their theoretical interpretation and does not
constitute a separate empirical derivation of those conditions. The
one-way package predicts target equivalence for equivalent frozen
physical views. The two-way package additionally predicts target
non-equivalence for non-equivalent views. Reflection must be defended
for the specific target; it cannot be supplied merely by requesting more
confidence in the same coarse report.

Approximate applications must declare physical and phenomenal distance
functions, equivalence tolerances, and error models. Failure to reject a
measured difference is not evidence of equivalence without a suitable
equivalence-testing design and adequate precision. The exact finite
examples below do not solve this measurement problem.

A finite empirical failure challenges the operational package. It does
not automatically isolate A5 from either bridge direction or from
measurement assumptions.

A countermodel explains the distinction: take two different complete

underdescription alone can produce this pattern.

A challenge specifically to A5-W would instead require independently
supported equivalence of the \textbf{complete constitutive} physical
organizations and a valid phenomenal observation that cannot differ
between isomorphic full experiential structures. Those are stronger
premises than agreement on a frozen coarse view. They must not be
smuggled in by renaming the failed finite prediction ``completeness.''

independently supported complete physical nonisomorphism \textbf{and}
complete experiential equivalence, not merely equivalent reports. This
paper does not claim that routine measurements supply those premises.

A5 is not needed to derive operational predictions that have already
been stipulated in a bridge. A successful bridge test supports that
fixed bridge/model against specified alternatives; it is not, solely for
that reason, differential evidence for A5 over every other
interpretation using the same operational law. Any stronger model
comparison must expose independently motivated constraints that generate
different likelihoods or predictions. This is a statement about

experience.

claim to originate the idea of identity itself.

The combination, de-combination, overlap, and individuation problems are
also established literatures (Chalmers, 2016; Miller, 2018; Blackmon,
2021). Recent preprint-level structural-subjectivity programs go further
by proposing positive structural criteria for the presence or locus of
subjectivity; for example, Nagae (2026) identifies subjectivity with
viability-constrained irreversible exclusive commitment. UCT differs at
this point: it does not use action, viability, irreversibility,
recurrence, access, self-modeling, or any other nonuniversal
organization as a basal experience-existence gate, and it presently
offers only a scoped no-free-selection theorem rather than a universal
positive subject criterion.

Neighboring and critical literatures also include Information Closure
Theory (Chang et al., 2020), universal-theory criteria (Kanai \&
Fujisawa, 2024), mechanistic computation and implementation accounts
(Piccinini, 2015; Anderson \& Piccinini, 2024), and the
unfolding/substitution debate (Doerig et al., 2019; Kleiner, 2020;
Tsuchiya et al., 2020; Usher, 2021; Herzog et al., 2022; Basri et al.,
2026).

This revision explicitly formalizes the intended tokenwise identity and
distinguishes it from its weaker pairwise consequence.

consistency use established mathematics. The claimed contribution is the

\end{aligned}

The earlier targeted prior-art audit did not locate an exact match for

advance. Rc4 adds proof repairs and a finite worked instance; it does
not claim priority for the orbit argument, product-preservation
mathematics, causal abstraction, or the general possibility of
behaviorally silent internal change. Its scientific value must be
assessed through the explanatory restrictions of the package and future
independently constrained applications.

\hypertarget{strong-objections-and-limitations}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{14. Strong objections and
limitations}\label{strong-objections-and-limitations}}

\hypertarget{the-axioms-are-strong}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.1 The axioms are strong}\label{the-axioms-are-strong}}

A1 and A5-SI are not empirical discoveries.

They are strong starting commitments.

The formal theorems derived from them must therefore be evaluated partly
by the explanatory costs and empirical obligations of the axioms.

\hypertarget{a5-oi-risks-tautological-explanatory-rhetoric}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.2 A5-OI risks tautological explanatory
rhetoric}\label{a5-oi-risks-tautological-explanatory-rhetoric}}

Once structural identity is assumed, results of the form:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\mathbf D\not\cong\mathbf D'
\Rightarrow
\Phi\not\cong\Phi'
\]

are straightforward consequences.

They should not be marketed as deep independent discoveries.

The nontrivial content must come from:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  process individuation;
\item
  physical anchoring;
\item
  exact no-go consequences;
\item
  multiscale relations;
\item
  subject non-selection;
\item
  independently constrained bridge predictions.
\end{itemize}

\hypertarget{complete-ontic-organization-is-not-directly-observed}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.3 Complete ontic organization is not directly
observed}\label{complete-ontic-organization-is-not-directly-observed}}

Science works with:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
D_v
\]

and:
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\widehat D_v.
\]

Direct empirical isolation of full A5-OI from bridge assumptions is
therefore difficult.

UCT should not disguise this epistemic limitation.

\hypertarget{complete-phenomenology-is-not-directly-measured}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.4 Complete phenomenology is not directly
measured}\label{complete-phenomenology-is-not-directly-measured}}

Human report, behavior, psychophysics, and physiology are projections.

Even convergent measures do not automatically equal complete phenomenal
structure.

This limits the strength of empirical falsification claims.

\hypertarget{process-proliferation}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.5 Process proliferation}\label{process-proliferation}}

A permissive process ontology can yield many weak, overlapping, or
nested experience-bearing tokens.

UCT accepts such plurality as part of its intended ontology. The number
of tokens is not a problem to be eliminated by an arbitrary experience
threshold; the scientific task is to distinguish their organizations and
relevance to a specified target.

Scientific salience and experience existence are separate questions.

\hypertarget{macroprocess-descriptions-are-applications-not-new-existence-gates}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.6 Macroprocess descriptions are applications, not new
existence
gates}\label{macroprocess-descriptions-are-applications-not-new-existence-gates}}

UCT accepts actual causal processes at different scales. The finite
criterion in Section 2.4 makes that domain concrete in one classical
representation. No unique natural macro boundary, autonomous coarse
state, or maximal integration score is required before an actual process
can possess experience. In concrete applications, competing claims about
physical boundaries and constitutive relations remain accountable to the
system being described. Uncertainty in that physical identification is
not evidence that the framework lacks a separate macro-experience
mechanism.

\hypertarget{subject-terminology-must-not-become-a-prerequisite}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.7 Subject terminology must not become a
prerequisite}\label{subject-terminology-must-not-become-a-prerequisite}}

If subject names an experience-bearing process, the ontology supplies
many possible referents. If it names a conceptual owner, self-model, or
one exclusive human-like agent, additional organizational descriptions
are relevant to that usage. UCT need not add an extra owner of
experience to explain why processes have experience. The optional
selection theorem constrains exclusive labeling rules; failure to
specify one universal subject partition is not a foundational
incompleteness criterion adopted by this paper.

\hypertarget{general-quality-identity-and-biological-specification}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.8 General quality identity and biological
specification}\label{general-quality-identity-and-biological-specification}}

A5-SI supplies the same general account of experiential quality for
elementary processes, cells, animals, and humans: the quality is the
intrinsic presentation of the actual organization. Human complexity does
not introduce a second explanatory gap inside the theory. A1-A6 do not
provide a biological lookup table assigning familiar labels such as red
or unpleasant to every measured neural configuration. Obtaining such
mappings is a task for particular scientific applications, just as
identifying a concrete cellular organization is. It must not be
misreported as absence of the general physical-experiential principle.

\hypertarget{cross-species-qualia-identity-is-underidentified}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.9 Cross-species qualia identity is
underidentified}\label{cross-species-qualia-identity-is-underidentified}}

Similar behavior, homologous mechanisms, or structurally comparable
organization do not by themselves prove identical subjective qualities
across species.

Claims should therefore use terms such as:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  precursor;
\item
  conserved mechanism;
\item
  structural homology;
\item
  convergent similarity;
\end{itemize}

rather than asserting uncalibrated qualitative identity.

\hypertarget{relativistic-and-quantum-extensions}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.10 Relativistic and quantum
extensions}\label{relativistic-and-quantum-extensions}}

The present finite/classical formalism is not a final
fundamental-physics theory.

A relativistic version should be formulated over causal spacetime
histories rather than a preferred global time slice.

A quantum version requires appropriate state spaces,
channels/instruments, subsystem criteria, and intervention notions.

\hypertarget{semantic-representation}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.11 Semantic
representation}\label{semantic-representation}}

Causal tracking does not automatically establish semantic aboutness.

Detailed models of semantic representation, conceptual content, and
explicit self-reference belong to higher-order applications. They do not
supply prerequisites for basal experience or a second
physical-experiential principle.

\hypertarget{formal-strength-and-finite-measurements-must-remain-explicit}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{14.12 Formal strength and finite measurements must remain
explicit}\label{formal-strength-and-finite-measurements-must-remain-explicit}}

A5-SI is a strong interpretation of identity. Its tokenwise isomorphisms
support the product proof; the weaker A5-OI formula by itself does not.
Rc4 records this difference rather than attributing the stronger formal
statement retroactively to the old formula. A critic may reject the
identity commitment; that is distinct from an internal derivation
failing to follow from it.

Finite scientific measurements can discard distinctions even when
complete experiential and physical organizations share the same
structure. Therefore finite reflection remains an additional condition
of an operational two-way test, not another experience-generating
mechanism or another foundational axiom. The measurement qualifications
do not replace the unified explanation of experience.

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
\section{16. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

UCT I v1.1 uses one explanatory principle from elementary physical
processes through cells, animals, and humans: each actual process has
experience, and its experiential organization is the intrinsic
presentation of its physical organization. Evolution changes that
organization, its discriminative repertoire, temporal relations, and
higher-order self-representations. It does not require a new
experiential substance when a cell becomes part of a multicellular
process or when human conceptual selfhood appears.

This edition formalizes this intended identity as A5-SI. The tokenwise
structural isomorphism is stronger than the earlier displayed pairwise
equivalence formula. With the standard product in a common declared
structural category, it supports the repaired non-summative combination
proof. The distinction between strong identity and weak type
correspondence is an explicit formal clarification, not an additional
mechanism for generating experience.

The theory accepts nested and overlapping actual processes without
requiring one exclusive macro boundary or subject partition. Selfhood
and familiar quality labels concern particular organizations and their
scientific descriptions; they are not new existence gates. A scoped
symmetry theorem explains a limitation of optional exclusive selectors
without making the absence of such a selector a defect in the basal
ontology.

The exact three-register example illustrates a substantive consequence
of token-relative organization: a whole-process structure can change
while an implemented sub-process and the declared output remain
invariant. The finite calculations verify those model properties. They
do not supply independent empirical confirmation of structural identity
or a measurement of circuit experience.

The four-setting extension proves exactly which initial perturbation
distinctions are erased, retained for one step, or sustained by
complementary coupling; these are finite-model organizational results,
not scalar consciousness levels. Further theoretical work should extend
such controlled transformation analyses and establish faithful links to
neighboring theories. Biological and psychophysical work can identify
concrete realizations when a particular application requires them.
Neither task calls for a second foundational explanation of why human
rather than cellular experience exists. The general principle, its
conditional deductions, and its empirical status are stated separately.
The paired UCT II v1.1 uses a compatible scientific-view interface and
preserves both exact mechanistic results and their evidential limits.

Before calculating any output, specify two token hypotheses: the whole
implemented network \(W_\lambda\), and the continuing implemented

clock, and declared boundary \(u\). The whole is causally connected
through the outgoing \(s\) links in both settings. The cross-coupled
\(a,b\) pair has boundary \(s\). These are explicit component/boundary
choices, not subject assignments. A1 applies conditionally if the
stipulated processes are actually instantiated and meet the token
requirements; a mathematical state table is not itself proof of that
instantiation.

The intervention family comprises all 27 coordinate clamps immediately
before an update: each register is either unchanged, set to 0, or set to
1. The boundary input takes both bit values. The switch settings are
separately compared mechanisms; they are not assumed to constitute a
continuous A2 path. This is a worked theoretical specification, not a
preregistered empirical study.

\hypertarget{b.2-exact-closure-of-an-implemented-sub-process}{%

\subsection{B.2 Exact closure of an implemented
sub-process}\label{b.2-exact-closure-of-an-implemented-sub-process}}

For \(\pi(s,a,b)=s\), the macro update is \(\bar T_u(s)=s\oplus u\).
Every two physical states with the same \(s\) have the same next
projected state. Projected coordinate clamps act as the same clamp on
\(s\) if it is targeted and as the identity otherwise. Hence
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\pi(T_u^\lambda(Ix))
=\bar T_u(\omega(I)\pi(x))
\]

for every declared clamp, input, setting, and state. The implementation
of \(s\) is part of the physical specification, not inferred from
closure alone. The present state and future update of this sub-process
do not depend on \(a\) or \(b\), even though it causally drives them.
The case separates physical downstream connection from participation in
this particular process's state and update.

By induction on time, for the same \(s_0\) and any input string,
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
y_t=s_0\oplus u_0\oplus\cdots\oplus u_{t-1}
\quad(t\ge1)
\]

in both settings, independently of initial \(a,b\). This is equality of
the declared output behavior, not equivalence under every conceivable
additional physical experiment.

\hypertarget{b.3-full-structural-difference-is-invariant-to-state-relabeling}{%
\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\subsection{B.3 Full structural difference is invariant to state
relabeling}\label{b.3-full-structural-difference-is-invariant-to-state-relabeling}}

Fix either input value. At \(\lambda=0\), both downstream next states
equal \(s\), so the eight-state transition map has exactly two images.
At \(\lambda=1\), the map is bijective: from \((s',a',b')\), recover
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
s=s'\oplus u,\qquad a=b'\oplus s,\qquad b=a'\oplus s.
\]

It therefore has eight images. Conjugating a transition map by a
bijective state relabeling preserves its image cardinality. Consequently
the two whole-network transition structures are not isomorphic, even
before requiring isomorphisms to preserve the intervention ports.

At a fixed boundary \(s\), the \(a\mid b\) split has independent
component updates at \(\lambda=0\). At \(\lambda=1\), changing \(b\)
changes \(a^+\) and changing \(a\) changes \(b^+\); no product of
component-local update maps reproduces this behavior relative to these
physical ports. This is a statement about the specified split, not about
every conceivable factorization or about one phenomenal subject. Because
the boundary is explicitly conditioned, correlation from the common
\(s\) drive is not mistaken for cross-coupling.

\hypertarget{appendix-d.-optional-checks-on-a-finite-coarse-description}{%
\clearpage
\section{Appendix D. Optional checks on a finite coarse
description}\label{appendix-d.-optional-checks-on-a-finite-coarse-description}}

These checks concern a proposed scientific model, not the existence of
experience. Actual processes need not admit autonomous, closed coarse
descriptions. The finite process criterion in Section 2.4 does not
depend on the checks below.

A useful model dossier identifies the actual referent and its physical
state carriers or distributed organization; declares the time horizon,
boundary, and omitted feedback; and distinguishes implemented relations
from a calculated summary. Predictive success alone does not create a
new token. A literal register is not required for a distributed
realization.

For a deterministic lower-level update \(x^+=T_e(x)\) and a surjective
coarse map \(\pi:X\to M\), a closed deterministic update \(\bar T_e\)
exists exactly when
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\pi(x)=\pi(x')\ \Longrightarrow\
\pi(T_e x)=\pi(T_e x').
\]

\textbf{Proof.} If \(\pi T_e=\bar T_e\pi\), equal coarse states have
equal coarse successors. Conversely, define \(\bar T_e(m)=\pi(T_e x)\)
using any representative of \(m\). The displayed condition makes the
definition representative-independent, and surjectivity supplies a
representative. \(\square\)

For a declared state-reset intervention \(I\), an additional
intervention correspondence \(\omega\) can be checked by
\nopagebreak[4]
\[
\pi(T_e(Ix))=\bar T_e(\omega(I)\pi(x)).
\]

The reset is applied before the transition. Mechanism-changing
interventions require separately indexed update maps; the reset equation
does not automatically cover them. Approximate, probabilistic, or
history-dependent models need their own error and sufficiency
conditions. Failure of a closed deterministic description is a failure
of that model, not absence of experience in the actual process.

This mathematics belongs to established lumpability and
causal-abstraction methodology (Buchholz, 1994; Rubenstein et al., 2017;
Beckers \& Halpern, 2019). UCT claims no mathematical priority. Appendix
B supplies both an implemented closed process and examples illustrating
why a closed calculated feature is not automatically another token, and
why an arbitrary feature can fail closure. These distinctions keep
optional modeling requirements separate from the common explanatory
principle for elementary, cellular, and human experience.
