## Scientific result

The central object is a query-relative preservation contract for a plural civilizational witness. It distinguishes situated testimony, individual final-text adoption, legitimate collective procedure, and current authorization. Six substantive content obligations address lived experience, knowledge and interpretive context, dissent and omissions, failures and responsibilities, aspirations with their status attached, and open questions/revision routes. The archive must not substitute for living communities.

For n respondents and m recorded binary clauses, marginal counts k_j identify the number J endorsing every clause only within the sharp integer interval:

L = max(0, sum_j k_j - (m-1)n), U = min_j k_j.

With a known target population N and unrestricted nonrespondents, the interval is [L, U + N - n]. These are classical intersection bounds applied to the witness problem, not newly invented probability theorems. Joint endorsement of individually recorded clauses is NOT evidence of adoption of a later edited package or implementation permission. A valid majority procedure is not automatically invalid merely because individual views differ.

For unrestricted binary states and exact fixed-length coding with query definitions already known, minimum bits for one fixed nonempty bundle, all marginal counts, all anonymous bundle counts, and all person-clause pairs are respectively:

ceil(log2(n+1)); ceil(log2((n+1)^m)); ceil(log2(binomial(n+2^m-1,2^m-1))); nm.

All bundle counts invert to the anonymous endorsement-pattern histogram by classical Boolean-lattice Mobius inversion. The first two query families need not be nested. Costs exclude text, names, signatures, and real archival infrastructure. Anonymous joint statistics are not an automatic privacy guarantee.

Partial entries use unknown rather than invented assent/refusal. The sound threshold reader takes the minimum/maximum over compatible completions. A separate sealed-past/current-permission argument is inherited indistinguishability reasoning, not a new revocation protocol.
