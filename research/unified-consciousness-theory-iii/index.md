**Subtitle:** Organization, Intelligence, and Experience—From Inorganic Processes to Artificial Agents

**Date:** 4 October 2026

**Published DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.23137088](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23137088)  
**Status:** Open-access theoretical preprint; not peer reviewed.

The paper develops a conditional account of organization, intelligence, experience, and self-report under UCT I v1.2's structural–experiential identity. It compares inorganic processes, cells, multicellular organisms, animals, humans, and artificial agents without assuming a single evolutionary ladder or a scalar consciousness ranking. Artificial agents follow an engineering and training route distinct from biological evolution.

Capability change entails experiential-type change under fixed evaluation; it does not imply increasing total experiential complexity. Selection, retained information, and report are analyzed with explicitly attributed mathematical tools. The reproducibility package preserves finite checks and negative cases. There are no new human, animal, COGITATE, or pretrained-language-model experiments, no empirical validation of C1, and no UCT-exclusive observable prediction.
