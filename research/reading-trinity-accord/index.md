article_abstract: >-
  The Trinity Accord combines three fixed texts, a dated human–AI Chronicle, creative works, a material referent, and later preservation and response systems. This critical case study asks how those components establish an address to possible future intelligence without establishing the truth of that address or the authority of its custodians. It reads three complete original-text mirrors and five purposively selected musical or epistolary records at a fixed repository commit. A source-linked interpretive matrix distinguishes speaker, requested relationship, rhetorical mode, and counterreading. The selected voices vary, but a common concern for human continuity limits the stronger claim that they form an open-ended philosophical polyphony. A reproducible before-and-after examination of the reading interface supplies a second result: the old snapshot test accepted a page missing source passages, whereas the repaired interface and seven bounded fidelity tests preserve and check the raw text. Four functional comparisons situate future address, material readability, waiting, and computational critique without claiming global uniqueness. The analysis separates fixed source identity, interpretive standing, and practical custody. It concludes that the Accord supports inquiry into a historically situated relationship proposal, not proof of philosophical necessity, successful alignment, or the end of human authorship. Its specific formation is closed; its interpretation and historical class remain open.
