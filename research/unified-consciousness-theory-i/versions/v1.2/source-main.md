## Abstract

Unified Consciousness Theory (UCT) is reformulated around one consciousness-specific commitment: for every actual valid physical process token, its complete token-relative physical organization and its intrinsic experiential organization are the same structure under a declared common signature. Process ontology remains separate and explicit, covering actual support, causal continuity, boundary accountability, persistence, lineage, and token/type distinctions.

This architecture makes several v1.1 commitments derivable rather than independent. Universal nonempty experience (U1) follows from the nonempty constitutive organization of an actual token together with tokenwise Structural–Experiential Identity (C1). Structural continuity (U2) follows when physical and experiential descriptions are represented in the same isomorphism-class space with a predeclared invariant topology or pseudometric. Selfhood Non-Prerequisite becomes a corollary given an actual non-self token witness, while persistence under embedding and token/type/lineage are treated as process-ontological rules.

The reformulation preserves the main v1.1 consequences concerning organization-gate impossibility, hidden-quale exclusion under fixed complete structure, probe invariance, no future contamination, causally screened history, non-summative combination, subject-selection symmetry, structural transformation, and failure-prone finite bridge methodology. It also retains the distinction between complete ontic organization, finite physical views, scientific estimates, and subject individuation.

A central evolutionary consequence is explicit: within UCT there is no first conscious ancestor. U1 assigns nonempty experience to every actual valid process token, including the simplest actual physical interaction processes. Evolution may introduce thresholds for memory, recurrence, global access, self-modeling, language, metacognition, and conceptual selfhood, but these are transitions within experiential organization rather than an ignition of experience itself. U2 transfers strict continuity when a physical structural path is continuous, while a fine-chain corollary handles arbitrarily gradual change without requiring a perfectly smooth global trajectory. Evolutionary continuity is therefore an application and explanatory pressure test, not an independent proof of C1.

The revision reduces logical redundancy but does not reduce the empirical burden. C1 remains a strong metaphysical identity commitment; finite experiments test explicitly declared bridge packages rather than complete ontic identity directly.

**Keywords:** consciousness; process ontology; structural identity; axiom minimization; continuity; evolution; experiential structure; self-model; scientific views

# 1. The target problem

Contemporary consciousness theories assign central roles to integrated causal structure, recurrent processing, global availability, higher-order representation, predictive modeling, information closure, temporal organization, and related mechanisms (Seth & Bayne, 2022; Albantakis et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2020; Kanai & Fujisawa, 2024). Much of the competition among theories concerns whether one of these mechanisms constitutes, enables, or marks consciousness.

UCT begins by separating two questions:

\[
\boxed{\text{Does experience exist?}}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{\text{What is the organization of that experience?}}
\]

UCT no longer treats the first question as an independent axiom. Instead, both existence and organization are placed under one tokenwise structural–experiential identity commitment. Given an actual token with nonempty physical organization, nonempty experience follows as a theorem. The nontrivial scientific problem is then to identify and compare organization without confusing finite views with complete ontic structure.

The single consciousness-specific starting point is tokenwise structural–experiential identity. Universal experience is a direct internal consequence, while process identity and persistence are handled in the process ontology. Internal derivations are assessed by whether they follow from those commitments. The substantive comparative task is to constrain structured differences, multiscale relations, and transformations; empirical applications additionally require declared bridge predictions. Human and cellular experience do not require different existence mechanisms within this framework.

The principal clarification of version 1.2 is that the strong identity commitment makes part of the v1.1 axiom list logically redundant. The second is that continuity must be stated in a common structural-type space, not by silently assigning unrelated topologies to physical and experiential descriptions. A central evolutionary consequence is then explicit: within UCT there is no first conscious ancestor. Thresholds may occur for particular forms of organization, but not for the existence of experience itself. Finite experiments still do not directly observe complete ontic organization or complete phenomenology; operational tests therefore target explicitly declared finite bridge hypotheses.


# 2. Ontological domain: actual process tokens

## 2.1 Actuality and tokenhood

Let:

\[
\mathcal P
\]

be the domain of actual valid physical process tokens. An element:

\[
P\in\mathcal P
\]

is an actual causally continuous physical subhistory, not an arbitrary set of variables selected after the fact.

A valid token must satisfy, at minimum:

1. **actual support** — the relevant states/events are physically instantiated;
2. **induced-relation fidelity** — attributed internal relations are inherited from physical organization rather than invented by the modeller;
3. **causal continuity** — the token has a physically traceable causal history over the interval of interest;
4. **boundary accountability** — materially relevant cross-boundary influences are represented rather than silently erased;
5. **lineage traceability** — continuation, replacement, branching, merger, and cessation are physically trackable;
6. **description invariance** — invertible coordinate or label changes do not create new tokens.

UCT is process-token pluralist. Actual tokens may:

- overlap;
- nest;
- branch;
- merge;
- persist through replacement;
- exist at multiple physical scales.

No maximality or exclusion rule is imposed at the basal level.

## 2.2 Multiscale token plurality

A critical distinction in v1.1 is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{actual macroprocess token}
\neq
\text{arbitrary coarse description}.
}
\]

Molecular, cellular, circuit-level, neural-system, organism-level, and other macroprocesses may all be actual process tokens when the relevant organization is physically instantiated. A scientist does not create a macroprocess merely by averaging microscopic variables.

Macroprocess is a scale-relative description of actual organized physical activity. Its experience does not wait for a new threshold of autonomy, recurrence, closure, complexity, or subjecthood. Many overlapping and nested processes may qualify; UCT does not require a unique privileged macro boundary. In an application, one must specify which actual causal organization is being described, just as one must specify which physical process a model concerns. That identification task should not be confused with a missing mechanism that turns macroprocesses into experience.

## 2.3 P6 — Token, type, and lineage

UCT distinguishes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{structural type}
\neq
\text{numerical token}
\neq
\text{causal lineage}.
}
\]

Two process tokens can have isomorphic organization without being numerically identical:

\[
\mathbf D(P)\cong \mathbf D(Q)
\not\Rightarrow
P=Q.
\]

Lineage is a directed continuation relation rather than numerical identity. A process may branch:

\[
P\leadsto Q_1,
\qquad
P\leadsto Q_2,
\qquad
Q_1\neq Q_2,
\]

or contribute to a merger:

\[
P\leadsto M,
\qquad
Q\leadsto M.
\]

Nothing in these relations alone determines a unique subject count.


## 2.4 P3 — Persistence, cessation, and embedding

Embedding is not by itself a termination event. If a process \(P\) continues to satisfy the actual-token criterion over interval \(I\) while participating in a larger actual process \(Q\), then:

\[
\boxed{
Persist(P,I)\land Embed(P,Q)
\Rightarrow
P\in\mathcal P\text{ throughout }I.
}
\]

Whether persistence actually holds is a physical question concerning support, constitutive dynamics, boundary dependence, and lineage. This rule does not assert that a process survives after its constitutive dynamics cease.

The distinctive role formerly assigned to A3 is therefore process ontological. Once persistence establishes that \(P\) remains an actual token, the experience conclusion follows from Theorem U1 below.

## 2.5 A concrete finite process criterion

In a finite classical description, let \(G\) represent physically instantiated events and their causal relations. An admissible process subhistory \(H\) consists of actual events, inherits their physical relations, is connected through the inherited causal links over the declared interval, and records materially relevant links across its boundary. Connectivity here does not require mutual influence between every pair, maximality, an autonomous Markov state, or a nervous system. A numerical renaming of \(H\) does not create another physical occurrence.

This is a concrete expression of Section 2.1's process domain, not an independent consciousness detector. Whenever such a process is actual, U1 applies. Different subhistories can overlap and nest; selecting all admissible processes does not demand an exclusive partition. Greater integration, temporal depth, differentiation, or self-maintenance then characterizes differences of organization within the domain.

The representation \(G\) must itself be grounded in the physical system. A convenient calculated feature may describe an existing process without creating an additional token. In contrast, an actual causal relation remains relevant even when it crosses an observer's preferred anatomical or computational boundary. Applications may investigate a process's current state carriers, dynamical contributors, enabling conditions, and output recipients separately. Those roles do not determine whether some underlying actual local process has experience.

A cell, a multicellular organism, and an extended interaction can therefore each instantiate experience-bearing organization without passing three different ontological tests. An entire organism need not be one indivisible subject for its many realized processes to fall within UCT. Appendix D gives optional procedures for checking finite coarse descriptions; their predictive-closure conditions are not required by the process criterion or by U1.

## 3.1 Full token-relative ontic organization

For an actual process token \(P\), define:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
}
\]

as the complete currently instantiated physical organization constitutive of that token.

“Complete” is token-relative. It means that every physical distinction or relation constitutive of \(P\)'s own organization is included. It does not mean that every microscopic fact anywhere in the realizing substrate or surrounding universe is part of the same token.

This distinction permits fine and coarse actual process tokens to coexist without making every microscopic fluctuation a mandatory difference in a human-scale macroexperience.

## 3.2 Physical scientific views

Science does not ordinarily access \(\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\) directly. Instead, it constructs physically anchored views indexed by:

\[
v=(t,\tau,g,\mathcal I,\mathcal B,\ldots),
\]

where \(t\) is time, \(\tau\) a horizon, \(g\) a measurement/representation grain, \(\mathcal I\) a physically admissible intervention family, and \(\mathcal B\) a declared boundary representation.

Write:

\[
D_v(P)
=
\Pi_v[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)
].
\]

Different views need not form one total hierarchy. Some are refinements or coarse projections of others; some are incomparable.

Where two routes claim to construct the same target physical view, local consistency requires approximate or exact commutation:

\[
R_{v\to z}
\cong
R_{w\to z}\circ R_{v\to w}.
\]

## 3.3 Scientific estimates

Actual data yield:

\[
\widehat D_v(P\mid\mathcal E,\mathcal M),
\]

an estimate conditioned on evidence \(\mathcal E\) and model class \(\mathcal M\).

If evidence underdetermines the physical organization, the appropriate scientific output is an identified set:

\[
\mathfrak I(P)
=
\left\{
D:
D\text{ remains compatible with the available evidence}
\right\},
\]

rather than an ontological point estimate chosen for convenience.

## 3.4 Subjecthood

A subject is not defined merely by being a process token, a scientific view, or a macroprocess.

UCT therefore maintains:

\[
\boxed{
\text{experience-bearing process}
\neq
\text{canonically individuated subject}.
}
\]

A universal positive subject selector is not required for this basal theory. Where a study uses subject to mean an experience-bearing process, the process domain supplies its referent. Where it means a human-like owner or conceptual self, the relevant self-organizing and self-representational relations belong to higher-order organization. Neither usage licenses imposing one exclusive subject partition on all actual processes.


## 3.5 A pointed, port-aware comparison signature

The word *structure* must not conceal what an isomorphism is required to preserve. In the finite controlled models below, the declared signature has sorts for states, boundary inputs, intervention ports, outputs, constituent slots, and time; operation symbols for update, reset, readout, and constituent projection; a distinguished current-state constant; and the declared temporal and incidence relations. A model in that signature can be written:

\[
\mathcal M_v=(X,x_\bullet,U,J,Y,T,I,o,\mathscr C,\preceq).
\]

The tuple is a model **in** the signature, not the signature itself. Here \(T_u:X\to X\) is an update at fixed constitutive mechanism, \(I_j:X\to X\) a permitted state reset, and \(o:X\to Y\) a declared output. \(\mathscr C\) specifies constituent projections and physically identified ports. Current-state pointing is essential: two occurrences of the same transition law at inequivalent current states need not have the same complete organization.

An admissible isomorphism contains bijections \(f_X,f_U,f_J,f_Y\) that satisfy

\[
f_X(x_\bullet)=x'_\bullet,\qquad
f_XT_u=T'_{f_U(u)}f_X,\qquad
f_XI_j=I'_{f_J(j)}f_X,\qquad
f_Yo=o'f_X,
\]

and preserve the declared temporal relations and constituent incidence. A port permutation is allowed only when it is declared as a symmetry of the comparison; an arbitrary mixture of physically distinct ports is not a relabeling of a fixed constituent split. Changing a mechanism, removing an intervention, or reversing a directed temporal relation generally changes the comparison rather than supplying an isomorphism. A probe family chosen by the analyst remains part of a scientific view, whereas a process's actual causal dispositions are ontic.

For the product comparison in Section 6, hold the common boundary convention fixed, use the product state space with its pointed state, and retain the specified component-local update and reset operations. These declarations identify the ordinary independent product used on both sides of C1. They do not impose autonomy, reciprocity, maximality, or a new threshold for experience.

The finite signature supplies an explicit example, not a claim that a finite model captures all constitutive properties of real hardware or a brain. A restricted signature or measured view can omit distinctions. Completeness relative to an actual token is therefore not inferred from success in this finite representation. In biological applications the relevant state carriers, interfaces, and time relations must be justified independently; the same core identity principle is used.

## 3.6 Structural-type space and invariant topology

Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal K}\) be the class/category of admissible \(\mathcal K\)-structures for a declared comparison. Define the structural-type space:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak S_{\mathcal K}
=
\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal C_{\mathcal K})/\cong.
}
\]

Write:

\[
q_{\mathcal K}(X)=[X]_{\cong}.
\]

For continuity and approximate-structure questions, a topology \(\tau_{\mathcal K}\), or an isomorphism-invariant pseudometric \(\delta_{\mathcal K}\), may be fixed on \(\mathfrak S_{\mathcal K}\). It must be declared on structural grounds rather than chosen after observing a desired phenomenal outcome.

Two choices must be separated. First, quotienting by structural isomorphism removes representational relabelings that should not count as new structural types. Second, the topology or pseudometric determines which *different* structural types count as nearby. C1 supplies neither a unique topology nor a unique metric. Consequently, Theorem U2 transfers continuity **within a predeclared structural comparison geometry**; it does not by itself prove that an evolutionary or developmental trajectory is continuous in that geometry.

The use of equivalence classes or quotient spaces is not claimed as new mathematics or as new to consciousness science. Kleiner (2020a) uses automorphism-equivalence classes to characterize intersubjectively well-defined aspects of experience; Kleiner and Ludwig (2024) develop a general framework for mathematical structures of conscious experience, including topological examples; and Oizumi, Lim, and Kanai (2026) use symmetry orbits and a quotient space in a principal-bundle model of perceptual qualia. The present quotient serves a different, narrower role: it identifies **isomorphism classes of the declared physical/experiential structural models** so that the two descriptions in C1 can be compared in one type space. It is not a claim that the present quotient geometry uniquely characterizes qualia.

This quotient construction does not certify that any finite representation is ontically complete. It supplies the common comparison space required by Theorem U2: physical and experiential organizations named by C1 occupy the same structural type rather than two independently topologized spaces.

# 4. One consciousness-specific core commitment

The v1.1 list A1–A6 is not treated here as an independent axiom basis. P3 and P6 have moved to process ontology; Universal Experience, Structural Continuity, and Selfhood Non-Prerequisite are derived below. The sole consciousness-specific core axiom is the strong tokenwise identity already intended by v1.1.

## C1 — Structural–Experiential Identity

For every actual valid process token \(P\), there exists an experiential organization in the same declared structural signature and an isomorphism:

\[
\boxed{
\forall P\in\mathcal P,\quad
\exists\,\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P),\,h_P:
\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P)
\overset{h_P}{\cong}
\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P).
}
\]

The two expressions describe the same token-relative organization in physical description and intrinsic experiential presentation. The isomorphism is not an empirically fitted correlation, an extra causal force, a report-generating mechanism, a scalar consciousness score, or a subject-selection rule.

The explicit existential form prevents a notational ambiguity: the theory is not first assuming an already-defined \(\Phi(P)\) and then using isomorphism to prove that experience exists. C1 itself is the metaphysical identity commitment from which the existence theorem follows.

This reformulation does not eliminate the substantive universal-experience commitment; it absorbs that commitment into the stronger universal tokenwise identity. A smaller independent axiom list is therefore a reduction in logical redundancy, not an empirical proof from nonphenomenal premises.

## Lemma C1-OI — Pairwise type equivalence

Composing tokenwise isomorphisms gives:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q)
\iff
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q).
}
\]

This pairwise equivalence is weaker than C1 written alone: an arbitrary permutation of type labels could preserve a biconditional without preserving each token's internal organization.

## Lemma C1-W — One-way phenomenal completeness

The one-way consequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf D(P)\cong\mathbf D(Q)
\Longrightarrow
\Phi(P)\cong\Phi(Q).
}
\]

C1-W suffices for No Hidden Quale, Probe Invariance, No Future Contamination, and Causally Screened History.

## Theorem U1 — Universal Nonempty Experience

For every actual valid process token:

\[
\boxed{
P\in\mathcal P
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing.
}
\]

**Proof.** By the process-token criterion, an actual token contains at least one physically instantiated event/state in the constitutive carrier sort. Hence the corresponding ontic \(\mathcal K\)-structure is nonempty in that distinguished sort. C1 supplies a sort-preserving isomorphism to \(\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P)\). A bijection from a nonempty carrier cannot have an empty codomain carrier. Therefore \(\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing\). \(\square\)

The distinguished-carrier qualification matters in a many-sorted signature: auxiliary sorts may legitimately be empty, whereas an actual token cannot have an empty constitutive event/state carrier.

U1 replaces v1.1 A1 as an independent axiom. Universal experience remains part of UCT's substantive content because C1 is itself universal over actual tokens.

## Theorem U2 — Structural Continuity

Let \(\Lambda\) be a parameter space and \(P_\lambda\) an explicitly comparable family of actual process tokens. Define:

\[
d(\lambda)
=
q_{\mathcal K}
\left(
\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(P_\lambda)
\right),
\qquad
\phi(\lambda)
=
q_{\mathcal K}
\left(
\Phi_{\mathcal K}(P_\lambda)
\right).
\]

By C1:

\[
\boxed{
d(\lambda)=\phi(\lambda)
\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda.
}
\]

Therefore, for any predeclared topology \(\tau_{\mathcal K}\) on the common structural-type space:

\[
\boxed{
d\text{ continuous}
\iff
\phi\text{ continuous}.
}
\]

For any predeclared isomorphism-invariant pseudometric:

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\mathcal K}(d(\lambda),d(\mu))
=
\delta_{\mathcal K}(\phi(\lambda),\phi(\mu)).
}
\]

**Proof.** C1 places the physical and experiential organizations of each token in the same isomorphism class. Hence the quotient-valued maps \(d\) and \(\phi\) are pointwise identical. Identical functions have the same topological and metric regularity properties. \(\square\)

U2 concerns structural continuity. It does not imply a universal scalar amount of consciousness, integer subject-count continuity, reportability continuity, conceptual-self continuity, or continuity of externally chosen human qualitative labels.

U2 is also a **transfer theorem**, not a proof that nature supplies a continuous path. Establishing that a biological, developmental, artificial, or replacement trajectory \(\lambda\mapsto P_\lambda\) is continuous in the chosen \((\mathfrak S_{\mathcal K},\tau_{\mathcal K})\) remains a physical/modeling premise. Choosing a topology after seeing a desired experiential conclusion would violate the same no-post-hoc discipline used elsewhere in the paper.

## Corollary U3 — Selfhood Non-Prerequisite

Suppose there exists an actual token \(P_0\) lacking human-like conceptual selfhood:

\[
P_0\in\mathcal P,
\qquad
\neg Self_I(P_0).
\]

By U1:

\[
\Phi(P_0)\neq\varnothing.
\]

Therefore human-like conceptual selfhood is not a necessary condition for basal experience.

The logical form is explicitly witness-dependent. U3 requires at least one actual token for which the chosen operational/structural definition of human-like conceptual selfhood is absent. The existence and correct classification of such a witness are empirical/semantic premises; they are not supplied by the theorem itself.

The positive mechanisms by which body models, agency, autobiographical memory, metacognition, and a conceptual “I” form remain higher-order application/bridge questions.

# 5. Formal consequences

## 5.1 Organization-Gate Impossibility

Define an experience-existence predicate:

\[
E(P)=\mathbf 1[\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing].
\]

By Theorem U1:

\[
E(P)=1
\qquad
\forall P\in\mathcal P.
\]

Let \(F(P)\) be any organization descriptor and \(S\) a threshold/target region. If:

\[
\exists Q\in\mathcal P:
F(Q)\notin S,
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf 1[F(P)\in S]
\not\equiv
E(P).
}
\]

Hence, within UCT, no nonuniversal threshold of:

- integration;
- recurrence;
- global availability;
- explicit memory;
- higher-order representation;
- predictive modeling;
- autonomy;
- entropy;
- generic biological complexity;

can equal the universal experience-existence predicate.

This theorem does not make those mechanisms irrelevant. It relocates them from experience existence to experience organization.

## 5.2 No Hidden Quale

From C1-W:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(P)\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}(Q)
\Rightarrow
\Phi(P)\cong
\Phi(Q).
\]

UCT therefore rejects an additional hidden phenomenal degree of freedom that varies independently while complete token-relative physical organization is fixed.

Within the intended structuralist interpretation, words such as “red” and “pain” label experiential organizations rather than additional freely varying quale atoms. C1-W alone does not determine the physical organization corresponding to either label or establish the complete structuralist interpretation.

## 5.3 Probe Invariance

Let \(\mathcal I^{exp}\) denote an experimenter-selected probe family. Merely changing this choice, without changing the complete constitutive physical organization, cannot alter phenomenology under C1-W:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I^{exp}_1&\ne\mathcal I^{exp}_2,\\
\mathbf D^{ontic}_1(P)&\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}_2(P)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Phi_1(P)\cong\Phi_2(P).
\end{aligned}
\]

The completeness qualifier matters. Holding only one causal graph or one response descriptor fixed is insufficient if other constitutive state distinctions change. A probe that physically intervenes on the process can change its experience by changing its organization; the invariant claim concerns a merely external change of description or choice.


## 5.4 No Future Contamination

If:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(Q),
\]

then a future external divergence that is not physically instantiated at time \(t\) cannot imply a present phenomenal difference:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(P)\cong\Phi_t(Q).
}
\]

Present causal dispositions are part of the present organization; actual future facts are not.

## 5.5 Causally Screened History / No Ghost History

Suppose:

\[
History(P)\neq History(Q)
\]

but all current constitutive physical consequences of those histories have converged:

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(P)
\cong
\mathbf D^{ontic}_t(Q).
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_t(P)\cong\Phi_t(Q).
}
\]

P6 still permits the tokens to remain numerically distinct because lineage can differ even when current structural type is the same.

## 5.6 External reward-label non-identity

Changing an external reward sign, semantic label, or experimenter convention without changing intrinsic constitutive organization cannot by itself alter phenomenology under C1-W.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reward sign}
\neq
\text{phenomenal valence sign}.
}
\]


# 6. Factorization, macroprocesses, and combination

## 6.1 Independent products and comparison objects

Fix structural signatures and a constituent split before comparing a whole with its parts. The physical product \(X_1\otimes\cdots\otimes X_n\) represents independent juxtaposition relative to that split and a declared boundary convention. In a transition model, component updates must not depend on the other component states once the declared boundary is fixed. A shared drive can create statistical correlations without creating the internal causal dependence at issue. Product factorization is consequently not synonymous with unconditional statistical independence.

For this product comparison, constituents occupy nonoverlapping slots of the specified physical split. A product of overlapping actual tokens would require a separately justified comparison construction; general gluing of overlapping processes is not established here. An abstract product is initially a **comparison object**. It need not itself be an actual causally continuous token; U1 is not automatically applied to it. Unrestricted mixing of physically distinct intervention ports must not be mistaken for a mere relabeling that preserves a particular constituent split. The phenomenal product must also be specified as the intended independent juxtaposition, not retrospectively defined to make a desired conclusion true.

## 6.2 Why C1-OI alone is insufficient

C1-OI constrains equivalence of structural types. It does not state how products are transported. The distinction is visible in a finite-set countermodel. Take isomorphism classes indexed by positive cardinality and the bijection

\[
f(6)=7,\qquad f(7)=6,\qquad f(n)=n\quad(n\ne6,7).
\]

Let physical constituent types have sizes 2 and 3, and a whole type have size 7. Map phenomenal types using \(f\). The equivalence biconditional holds because \(f\) is injective. Nevertheless,

\[
7\ne2\cdot3,\qquad f(7)=6=f(2)\cdot f(3).
\]

Thus a nonproduct physical type can correspond to the product of the constituent phenomenal types while the bare C1-OI equivalence formula is satisfied. This is an abstract countermodel to an implication of the formula, not a claim that unstructured finite sets are adequate physical or phenomenal models. It pinpoints the missing algebraic commitment. Additional physical restrictions may exclude it, but those restrictions must be stated.

## 6.3 Non-summative combination from the intended identity

Use the strong tokenwise identity C1 in a common comparison category with the finite products needed for the argument. Independent juxtaposition on each side is represented by this same standard product. Ordinary products preserve isomorphisms: if each \(h_i:X_i\cong Y_i\), then

\[
\bigotimes_i h_i:\bigotimes_i X_i\cong\bigotimes_i Y_i.
\]

This is the relevant mathematical consequence of expressing the same constituent organizations in two descriptions. It is not another physical mechanism of experiential combination. The use of a common signature and product must be declared; the weak type-equivalence formula alone cannot supply it.

**Theorem 6.3 (non-summative combination in the declared product comparison).** Let \(M\) be an actual whole process and \(P_i\) its actual constituents in the nonoverlapping slots of the specified comparison. If

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)\not\cong
\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

then C1 implies

\[
\Phi(M)\not\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i).
\]

**Proof.** Let \(h_M\) and \(h_i\) be the tokenwise isomorphisms supplied by C1. If \(\Phi(M)\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)\), compose that isomorphism with \(h_M\) and the inverse of the product of the \(h_i\). This yields

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M)
\cong\Phi(M)
\cong\bigotimes_i\Phi(P_i)
\cong\bigotimes_i\mathbf D^{ontic}(P_i),
\]

contradicting the physical premise. The abstract comparison product need not be an actual token, so no extension of U1 to imaginary processes is used. \(\square\)

The result says that actual additional constitutive relations are not erased in the experiential description. It does not add together subjective substances. Its nonproduct conclusion is relative to the specified constituent product, not every possible decomposition. Continuing constituent processes remain experience-bearing by U1 and P3. Neither the theorem nor the theory requires a unique numerical subject count before admitting the whole's experiential organization.

If identity is deliberately weakened back to the pairwise C1-OI formula alone, the countermodel in Section 6.2 still applies and this proof is unavailable. The repair therefore makes the intended premise explicit rather than silently inferring a stronger premise from a weaker formula.

## 6.4 Macro invariance with micro change

Suppose actual macroprocess tokens obey

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_A)\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(M_B),
\]

while an independently identified pair of lower-scale actual tokens obeys

\[
\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^A)\not\cong\mathbf D^{ontic}(p^B).
\]

C1-W suffices for \(\Phi(M_A)\cong\Phi(M_B)\). The converse component of C1-OI additionally yields \(\Phi(p^A)\not\cong\Phi(p^B)\). Only the stated pairwise consequences are needed for these claims; the product construction is not involved.

The result is conditional: it does not show that any specified material replacement actually preserves complete macro organization. Nor can one establish it merely by observing the same coarse statistic of the same token. The macro token must have an independently supported realization and boundary; lower-scale physical differences must not be constitutive differences of that macro token. Appendix B demonstrates the corresponding distinction between a fixed whole-process token and an invariant implemented sub-process in an idealized finite model. It is not a general biological replacement theorem.


# 7. Subject selection under symmetry: an orbit obstruction

UCT distinguishes experience, structural unity, macroprocess tokenhood, subjecthood, and self-modeling. The result below concerns a proposed selection rule. It neither derives subjecthood from U1 nor treats every experience-bearing process as one canonical subject.

## 7.1 Equivariant selection is a union of candidate orbits

Let \(W\) be a physical structure and \(\Gamma\) a group of its structure-preserving automorphisms. Let \(\mathcal C(W)\) be a candidate family closed under this group. A deterministic selector returns \(S(W)\subseteq\mathcal C(W)\) and is equivariant:

\[
S(gW)=gS(W).
\]

Since \(gW=W\) for \(g\in\Gamma\), the selected family is invariant under \(\Gamma\).

**Orbit lemma.** If a candidate \(P\) is selected, its entire orbit \(\Gamma P\) is selected.

**Proof.** For each \(g\in\Gamma\), invariance gives \(gP\in gS(W)=S(W)\). Thus a selected family is a union of full orbits. \(\square\)

**Corollary 7.1 (restricted exclusive-selection obstruction).** Suppose the admissible candidates are one orbit \(O\) containing an overlapping pair. No deterministic equivariant selector can return a nonempty, pairwise disjoint subfamily of \(O\).

**Proof.** A nonempty selection contains one candidate and hence all of \(O\). It then contains the overlapping pair, contrary to pairwise disjointness. \(\square\)

This corollary requires the stated restriction of the candidate family and a nonempty-output requirement. These are selection requirements, not consequences of the process ontology plus C1/U1/U2.

## 7.2 The three-part example and its counterlimits

For a reflection-symmetric world with parts \(a,b,c\), let the reflection exchange \(a\) and \(c\), and let the candidate orbit be

\[
O=\bigl\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\}\bigr\}.
\]

The two invariant selected subfamilies are the empty family and the whole orbit. The first violates nonemptiness; the second violates exclusivity. An unbiased random rule could choose either member with equal probability, but it would not determine one unique subject from \(W\); a realized random seed would add selection data.

The theorem does **not** rule out the invariant partitions

\[
\bigl\{\{a,b,c\}\bigr\},\qquad
\bigl\{\{a\},\{b\},\{c\}\bigr\}.
\]

If a positive subject theory admits one of these, it needs its own physical justification. Neither is excluded by the orbit lemma. The correct conclusion is a scoped obstruction to selecting nonempty exclusive candidates from an overlapping symmetry orbit, not a general theorem that intrinsic structure cannot determine any unique partition. A universal positive subject criterion is not imposed by UCT. Any additional exclusive selector must justify its own scope and purpose.


# 8. No first conscious ancestor: from physical process to conceptual self

This section is explanatory rather than foundational. Its central UCT consequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{within the UCT domain, there is no first conscious ancestor.}
}
\]

UCT does not describe evolution as a transition from nonexperience to experience. It describes transformations among already experiential actual processes. The simplest actual physical interaction processes have nonempty experiential organization by U1; chemical systems, cells, multicellular systems, nervous systems, animals, and humans instantiate progressively different organizations under the same C1 principle.

This claim concerns **actual processes**, not inert labels such as “particle,” “atom,” or “organism” taken in abstraction. An elementary-particle interaction, an atomic process, a cell, or a brain is in the UCT domain only insofar as the relevant actual process satisfies the token criteria of Section 2.

## 8.1 Corollary E1 — No First Conscious Ancestor

Define the experience-existence predicate:

\[
E(P)=\mathbf 1[\Phi(P)\neq\varnothing].
\]

By U1:

\[
\boxed{
E(P)=1
\qquad
\forall P\in\mathcal P.
}
\]

Let

\[
\gamma:\Lambda\to\mathcal P
\]

be a family all of whose members are **actually instantiated valid process tokens in the scenario under evaluation**. Then:

\[
\boxed{
E(\gamma(\lambda))=1
\qquad
\forall\lambda\in\Lambda.
}
\]

For the All-Ancestors-Survive thought experiment below, simultaneous actualization of the comparison family is part of the stipulation: one imagines a physically admissible scenario in which representatives of the intermediate organizations are all instantiated. U1 is applied only to those stipulated actual tokens, not to a merely drawn state-space point or an uninstantiated mathematical possibility.

Therefore no \(\lambda_\ast\) inside the UCT domain can mark a transition of the form:

\[
E(\gamma(\lambda<\lambda_\ast))=0,
\qquad
E(\gamma(\lambda>\lambda_\ast))=1.
\]

**Proof.** Every \(\gamma(\lambda)\) is, by construction, an actual valid process token. U1 applies to every such token, so \(\Phi(\gamma(\lambda))\neq\varnothing\) for every \(\lambda\). Hence \(E(\gamma(\lambda))=1\) throughout. \(\square\)

This result does **not** say that every stage has the same experience, the same richness, the same unity, or the same cognitive capacities. It states only that the experience-existence predicate does not switch from zero to one inside the declared UCT domain.

Corollary E1 is not claimed as historically novel merely because it rejects a first conscious biological threshold. Panexperientialist and panpsychist traditions have long treated experience or mentality as fundamental and widespread. The contribution claimed here is the explicit derivation of this no-ignition result inside UCT's process-token/structural-identity dependency architecture, not the general idea that experience extends below animals or brains.

There may be genuine organizational thresholds for:

- recurrent processing;
- long-range access;
- memory;
- predictive control;
- self-modeling;
- metacognition;
- language;
- conceptual autobiographical selfhood.

Those are transitions **within experiential organization**, not transitions from no experience to experience.

## 8.2 The All-Ancestors-Survive Thought Experiment

Imagine that representatives of every sufficiently fine intermediate form in the physical history from simple actual interactions through stable chemistry, early cells, multicellular organization, nervous systems, animals, and humans were simultaneously instantiated and available for comparison.

“Ancestor” is used schematically in the name of this thought experiment. Elementary physical and prebiotic chemical stages are not biological ancestors in the strict Darwinian sense. The argument joins a physical/chemical prehistory to a biological evolutionary lineage; it does not claim that an electron or molecule is literally an organismal ancestor.

Represent the family schematically as:

\[
P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_n,
\]

or, in a finer idealization,

\[
\gamma:\Lambda\to\mathcal P.
\]

Suppose neighboring members can be chosen so that their relevant physical organizations differ only slightly in a predeclared structural geometry:

\[
\delta_{\mathcal K}
\!\left(
d(\lambda),
d(\lambda+\Delta\lambda)
\right)
\to 0
\qquad
\text{as }
\Delta\lambda\to0.
\]

UCT does not search this family for an index \(i^\ast\) satisfying:

\[
E(P_{i^\ast-1})=0,
\qquad
E(P_{i^\ast})=1.
\]

Corollary E1 has already ruled out such an onset within the UCT domain. The scientific question is instead:

> Which physically instantiated distinctions and relations are added, removed, reorganized, stabilized, or made available as the process family changes?

If the structural-type path

\[
d(\lambda)=
[\mathbf D^{ontic}_{\mathcal K}(\gamma(\lambda))]_{\cong}
\]

is continuous in the predeclared invariant topology on \(\mathfrak S_{\mathcal K}\), U2 gives:

\[
\phi(\lambda)=
[\Phi_{\mathcal K}(\gamma(\lambda))]_{\cong}
=
d(\lambda),
\]

and therefore:

\[
\boxed{
d\text{ continuous}
\Longrightarrow
\phi\text{ continuous}.
}
\]

The intended picture is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonempty experience throughout}
+
\text{changing experiential organization}.
}
\]

This is the formal expression of the core evolutionary intuition motivating UCT.

### Corollary E2 — Fine-Grained Structural Chains

The user's intended continuity need not be an exactly differentiable or even globally continuous biological path. Fix an isomorphism-invariant pseudometric \(\delta_{\mathcal K}\). Suppose a finite actual-token chain

\[
P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_n
\]

satisfies:

\[
\delta_{\mathcal K}
\!\left(
d(P_i),d(P_{i+1})
\right)
\le \varepsilon
\qquad
(i=0,\ldots,n-1).
\]

Because C1 identifies the physical and experiential descriptions with the same points of the structural-type space:

\[
\delta_{\mathcal K}
\!\left(
\phi(P_i),\phi(P_{i+1})
\right)
=
\delta_{\mathcal K}
\!\left(
d(P_i),d(P_{i+1})
\right)
\le\varepsilon.
\]

Hence every \(\varepsilon\)-fine physical structural chain is an equally \(\varepsilon\)-fine experiential structural chain.

If the comparison family can be refined so that such chains exist for arbitrarily small \(\varepsilon>0\), the experiential comparison can be refined to the same degree. This is weaker than assuming one globally smooth evolutionary trajectory and matches the intended claim that the physical/experiential development can be **gradual without requiring mathematically perfect continuity**.
