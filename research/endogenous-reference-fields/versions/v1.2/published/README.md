## What changed in v1.2

v1.2 restores the broad theoretical ambition of v1.0 — experiential attribution, subject boundaries, minimal mechanism supports, causal continuation, copying, branching, shared resources, gradual replacement, and twenty-one thought experiments — while retaining the strongest rigor improvements from v1.1: explicit shared modulation, persistence and timescale dimensions, clearer intervention semantics, stronger separation results, discriminating predictions, and more conservative scope claims.

The small computational training experiment introduced in v1.1 is **not relied upon in this revision**. v1.2 is a theoretical and conceptual paper. No model task performance, toy training result, or self-report is used as evidence that an artificial system is conscious.

## Status and limits

Published as an open-access theoretical preprint; not externally peer reviewed. The paper proposes a positive theory but explicitly separates formal organizational results from the bridging claim to phenomenal experience. It does not establish machine consciousness, a universal consciousness threshold, a unique natural grain, a complete theory of valence, or an exact universal subject count.

Substantial AI assistance was used for literature research, conceptual development, counterexample construction, formal checking, critical revision, and manuscript production under human direction. The human author of record assumes publication responsibility.
